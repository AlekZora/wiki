## Summary

Rework is 37signals' (now Basecamp) manifesto against conventional startup wisdom. Written by practitioners who stayed small, profitable, and remote while the rest of the industry grew fast and burned money, the book challenges nearly every default assumption about how companies should operate: meetings, planning, growth, headcount, fundraising, workaholism. It's structured as short, punchy essays—each challenging one conventional wisdom.

## Key Ideas

- **Embrace constraints**: Working within tight limits forces creative solutions. Small budgets and short timelines are advantages, not handicaps.
- **Build half a product, not a half-assed product**: Ship less, do it well. Cut features, not corners.
- **Start at the epicenter**: Every business problem has a core. Find the thing that would collapse everything else, start there.
- **Meetings are toxic**: Every meeting should have an agenda, a timer, a clear end goal. Most should be replaced by async communication.
- **Interruption is the enemy of productivity**: Creative work requires long unbroken stretches. The open office plan is a productivity disaster.
- **Say no by default**: Every feature request, partnership, hire is a "no" until overwhelmingly convinced. Default "no" keeps the product focused.
- **Outside money is Plan Z**: VC funding changes the incentive structure. It's not free—it's expensive in control, attention, and pressure to exit.
- **Out-teach your competition**: Publishing knowledge builds trust and audience faster than advertising.
- **Launch now**: Something live and imperfect beats a perfect product that's never shipped.
- **The real world is an excuse**: "That wouldn't work in the real world" is what pessimists say. It's not a place; it's a justification for not trying.

## My Take

Rework reads like extreme common sense—advice so obvious that almost nobody follows it. The "less is more" philosophy is directly applicable to AI product development: most AI products fail from overcomplicating the interaction, not from lack of features. The "launch now" and "half a product" maxims are especially relevant when building with fast-moving tech. The culture section is underrated: treating employees like adults, sending them home at 5, no rock stars—this is a product philosophy as much as a people philosophy.
