## Summary

Dorothy Gale is swept from Kansas to the magical land of Oz by a cyclone. To get home, she must follow the yellow brick road to the Emerald City and seek the help of the great Wizard. Along the way she gathers three companions — a Scarecrow wanting a brain, a Tin Woodman wanting a heart, and a Cowardly Lion wanting courage — each of whom already possesses the quality they seek. The Wizard is revealed as a fraud; the magic was inside the characters all along. Dorothy returns home by clicking her silver shoes three times.

## Key Ideas

- The central theme is that the qualities we think we lack — intelligence, love, courage — are often things we already possess but don't recognize.
- The Wizard is a con artist who sustains belief through theater and misdirection. He succeeds not by being powerful but by convincing others he is.
- Baum explicitly rejected the moralizing darkness of earlier fairy tales — Oz was intended as a "modernized fairy tale" free of "horrible and blood-curdling" content.
- The Land of Oz as a utopian vision: abundant, colorful, populated by eccentric communities rather than monarchies of terror.
- Political allegorical readings (silver shoes/gold standard, Oz = ounce, Yellow Brick Road = gold standard) are popular but disputed.

## Quotes

> "You had the power all along, my dear."

## My Take

The Wizard as fraud is a more interesting lesson than it first appears: Baum is arguing that institutions of authority often derive their power entirely from belief. The Wizard doesn't fail because he lied — he mostly succeeded. He fails only when Dorothy's specific need (to get home) requires something real rather than theatrical.
