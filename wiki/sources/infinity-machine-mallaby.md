## Summary

Sebastian Mallaby's biography of Demis Hassabis and the founding and growth of DeepMind — from its London origins through the Google acquisition, AlphaGo, AlphaFold, and into the race toward artificial general intelligence. The book traces how a child chess prodigy and game designer became the person most seriously pursuing superintelligence, and what that pursuit looks like from the inside. The epigraph (von Neumann) sets the tone: we may be building a monster, and we're doing it anyway.

## Key Ideas

- **Games as training ground**: Hassabis's formative insight was that games are compressed, tractable versions of reality — if you can master games, you're building general-purpose reasoning machinery. Atari → Go → protein folding is a coherent trajectory, not a pivot.
- **The DeepMind founding thesis**: Intelligence is the most powerful thing in the universe, and understanding it computationally is both the deepest scientific question and the highest-leverage problem. Hassabis framed this as science, not product.
- **Transformer + agent convergence**: The arc from early RL work to language models to agentic systems is told as an inevitable convergence — the "agent and the transformer" chapter frames current AI not as two separate things but as components assembling into something new.
- **AlphaFold as proof of concept**: Solving protein folding wasn't just a biology win — it was evidence that the approach (deep learning + large-scale compute + self-supervised signal) could crack problems previously considered intractable. A template for what comes next.
- **The safety tension**: DeepMind was explicit about existential risk from the beginning, more than any other major lab. The internal tension between "we must build it" and "this could be catastrophic" is a running thread throughout the book.
- **"We're Cooked"**: A chapter title that signals the moment when people inside the field started believing the timeline was real — that AGI wasn't a distant theoretical horizon but a near-term engineering problem.
- **Google acquisition**: Selling to Google was portrayed as both necessary (compute, scale) and compromising (safety commitments, research freedom). The negotiation included an ethics board condition that was never fully honored.

## Quotes

> "The development of full artificial intelligence could spell the end of the human race… It would take off on its own, and re-design itself at an ever increasing rate." (epigraph — von Neumann, referenced)

## My Take

The most important book currently being written about AI — not because it has answers, but because it traces the actual intellectual lineage of what's happening now. Mallaby's access appears deep. The Hassabis arc (games → neuroscience → DeepMind → Google → AGI) is the cleanest through-line in all of AI biography. The "We're Cooked" chapter title alone tells you where the internal mood is. For anyone building AI products: AlphaFold is the pattern to study — not "what can LLMs do?" but "what previously intractable domain can we now crack with this approach?"
