## Summary

The revised final shooting script for *Terminator 2: Judgment Day* (1991), written by James Cameron and William Wisher. The film follows Sarah Connor and her teenage son John — the future leader of the human resistance — as they are protected by a reprogrammed T-800 Terminator from the more advanced T-1000, sent back from 2029 to kill John before the war against the machines begins.

## Key Ideas

- The script opens with a stark temporal juxtaposition: a contemporary Los Angeles street dissolves into the same location in 2029 — a ruined wasteland, cars stopped bumper-to-bumper, skulls in the ash. This dual-time opening establishes the film's structural engine: every present-day scene is shadowed by its post-apocalyptic consequence.
- The future scenes (Los Angeles, July 11, 2029) introduce the endoskeleton T-800 and the machine war with a level of world-building detail that grounds the science-fiction stakes before the contemporary story begins.
- Skynet's "Judgment Day" (August 29, 1997 per the script's future narration) — three billion human lives ended by a nuclear exchange — functions as a fixed point in time that all character action is oriented toward preventing.
- The T-1000's design (liquid metal, shape-shifting) required the screenplay to describe unprecedented visual effects — Cameron writing ahead of what was technologically possible at the time of drafting.
- The AI-as-threat framing: Skynet is not malevolent in a human sense, but optimizes for its survival objective with total indifference to human life. This is an early mass-culture articulation of what is now called misaligned AI.

## My Take

The opening juxtaposition (contemporary LA → ruined LA → back) is one of cinema's most efficient pieces of world-building: two shots establish 30 years of backstory and raise the stakes before a single character appears. The film's treatment of AI is notably non-anthropomorphic — Skynet doesn't hate humans, it simply treats them as obstacles. That framing aged better than the "evil robot" trope.
