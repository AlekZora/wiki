## Summary

Physicist Melvin Vopson at the University of Portsmouth argues that gravitational attraction reduces information entropy — clustering objects together creates more ordered information states. He connects this to his "Second Law of Infodynamics," which posits that information entropy decreases over time (inverse of thermodynamic entropy). This, he suggests, could be evidence that the universe behaves like a simulation optimizing and compressing its own data. The paper was published in *AIP Advances* in April 2025.

## Key Points

- Vopson's **mass-energy-information equivalence principle** (2019) treats information as having a small but measurable mass.
- His **Second Law of Infodynamics** states information entropy decreases over time — opposite to the Second Law of Thermodynamics.
- Applied to gravity: when objects cluster due to gravity, the associated information entropy decreases, imposing computational order.
- Builds on **entropic gravity** (Erik Verlinde, 2011) — the idea that gravity is not fundamental but emergent from deeper processes.
- Earlier supporting work: SARS-CoV-2 mutations appeared to decrease information entropy, consistent with the "law."
- Vopson connects this to the **simulation hypothesis** (Nick Bostrom, 2003): a simulated universe would need built-in optimization and compression.
- Potential implications for dark energy, quantum gravity, and black hole thermodynamics.
- Vopson is explicit that his ideas are hypotheses, not proven science; paper titles frequently include question marks.

## My Take

Vopson's framing is provocative but speculative. The leap from "gravity reduces information entropy" to "we live in a simulation" is enormous — information-theoretic descriptions of physical phenomena don't necessarily imply a simulator. That said, the connection between information theory and fundamental physics (via Shannon, Landauer, Verlinde) is a legitimate and active research area. Worth watching whether the Second Law of Infodynamics accumulates more empirical support beyond the two initial examples. The virus mutation evidence is interesting but feels cherry-picked.
