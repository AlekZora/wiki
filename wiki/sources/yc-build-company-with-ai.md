## Summary

YC partner Diana lays out a framework for building AI-native startups. The core argument: AI shouldn't be a productivity tool layered onto existing workflows — it should be the operating system the company runs on. She introduces the concept of intelligent closed loops (vs open loops), the idea of making the entire company queryable by AI, and the software factory model where humans write specs and tests while agents generate and iterate on code.

## Key Ideas

- **AI as OS, not tool** — Every workflow, decision, and process should flow through an intelligent layer that continuously learns and improves.
- **Closed loops vs open loops** — Old companies ran as open loops (decide, execute, no systematic feedback). AI-native companies should be closed loops: actions produce artifacts, artifacts feed back into the intelligence layer, process improves.
- **Queryable organization** — Every important action should produce a legible artifact. Minimize DMs; record meetings; embed agents in all communication channels. Build dashboards covering revenue, sales, engineering, hiring, ops.
- **Software factory model** — Humans write specs and scenario-based tests defining success. Agents generate implementation and iterate until tests pass. Some companies already have repos with no handwritten code — only specs and test harnesses. Evolution of TDD.
- **No human middleware** — If a company is queryable and artifact-rich, middle managers become unnecessary. Information routing is the AI's job. Every human layer removed is a direct speed gain.
- **Three archetypes**: (1) IC/builder-operator — builds and runs things directly, everyone brings prototypes not decks; (2) DRI — one person, one outcome, no hiding; (3) AI founder — still builds, leads by example, does not delegate AI strategy.
- **Token-maxing** — The best companies maximize token usage, not headcount. High API bills are fine if they replace what would have cost more in salaries.
- **Startup advantage** — No legacy systems, no org chart to unwind. Can design systems, workflows, and culture around AI from day one. Incumbents must maintain live products while rewiring everything.

## My Take

Solid operational framework, though it sometimes feels like it's describing a destination rather than a path. The closed-loop / software factory ideas are the most actionable. The "three archetypes" framing from Jack Dorsey is interesting but a bit abstract. Most useful takeaway for early-stage: treat every process as something that should eventually be automated, and instrument everything from the start so agents have context to work with.
