## Summary

A film-theory analysis of Nolan's non-linear storytelling as a systematic reveal mechanics engine. Every structural choice about when to withhold or release information is calibrated to control the type of engagement the audience experiences — from suspense to revelation to retrospective reinterpretation.

## Key Points

- **The maze philosophy**: Nolan's explicit goal is "parity of confusion" between viewer and character, not omniscience. He argues that when the audience understands things before they're explained, frustration (not suspense) results.
- **Fabula vs. sujet** (narrative theory framework): Fabula = chronological story events; sujet = order events are presented to the audience. Nolan maximally exploits the gap between these.
  - *Memento*: sujet runs opposite to fabula — effects before causes
  - *The Prestige*: sujet hides dual-identity twist by shuffling events across two apparent timelines
  - *Dunkirk* / *Oppenheimer*: multiple simultaneous fabulas interleaved so audience synthesizes what no character alone knows
- **Three distinct reveal mechanics**:
  1. Retroactive recontextualization (*Memento*, *The Prestige*): late revelation reframes meaning of all prior scenes — reveals change perception of what happened, not anticipation of what comes
  2. Convergence reveals (*Dunkirk*): parallel timelines revealed as the same event from different perspectives — emotional resolution rather than plot surprise
  3. Diegetic puzzle reveals (*Inception*, *Tenet*): protagonist discovers info within the story world as the audience receives it simultaneously — exposition and revelation at the same beat
- **Curiosity over suspense**: Nolan explicitly inverts Hitchcock's bomb-under-the-table model. He prefers mystery and curiosity over suspense. He argues that cinema since the 1950s was flattened by TV's preference for clarity; non-linear structures restore the mystery television removed.
- **Plot points as information events** (not action events): the narrative turning point in a Nolan film is often the moment the *audience's understanding* shifts, not what happens. This makes viewers active co-constructors of meaning.

## My Take

The distinction between retroactive recontextualization and convergence reveals is genuinely useful as a taxonomy. Most film theory treats Nolan's structures as a single category of "non-linear," but the emotional mechanism is completely different: one is a reframe, one is a completion. The "plot points as information events" formulation is the sharpest conceptual tool in the analysis.
