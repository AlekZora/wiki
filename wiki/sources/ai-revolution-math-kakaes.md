## Summary

The summer of 2025 marked a tipping point: AI models solved five of six IMO problems, and mathematicians who had dismissed LLMs began experimenting. By early 2026, the "First Proof" challenge showed AI models completing over half of research-level math problems in a week. Terence Tao, Geordie Williamson, and others describe AI as a powerful collaborator — not a replacement — that lets mathematicians explore in days what would have taken months. Tools like Google DeepMind's AlphaEvolve use Gemini and genetic algorithms to evolve Python programs that discover new mathematical structures.

## Key Points

- **Inflection point**: July 2025 — multiple AI models scored 5/6 at IMO. February 2026 — "First Proof" challenge solved >50% of research-level math in a week.
- **AlphaEvolve** (DeepMind, with Tao and Gómez-Serrano): evolved Python programs over ~67 problems; improved best-known solutions on 23 of them. Each result took a day or two instead of months by a field expert.
- **Emergent discovery**: AlphaEvolve found hypercube structure in Bruhat intervals that mathematicians had missed for 50 years — it answered a question they hadn't thought to ask.
- **Proof by LLM conversation**: Ernest Ryu used ChatGPT to prove a 42-year-old open problem in optimization (Nesterov's convergence). The model made mistakes but generated useful partial results, with Ryu as verifier.
- **Ravi Vakil's group**: used a bespoke Gemini system (FullProof) to produce an elegant proof of sphere embedding in flag varieties; credited the AI with the key idea.
- **Autoformalization**: converting natural-language proofs to formal logical language is an active area; Tao believes AI could formalize "a significant fraction of mathematics."
- **Critiques**: "ocean of slop" of AI-generated nonsense polluting journals (Joel Hamkins); students increasingly outsourcing homework; risk of losing mathematicians as a profession while accelerating research.
- **Tao's mountain metaphor**: humans plan long routes to Everest; current AI parkours to 6-foot walls. The walls may grow to 10 or 100 feet, but AI can't yet do long-term strategic planning.
- **Daniel Litt**: "It's very likely that this technology is *bigger than the computer*." Expects AI generating better-than-human mathematics within 20 years.
- Positive reinforcement in prompts seems to help AlphaEvolve — the team noticed it performed better when told "You can do this."

## Quotes

## My Take

The most striking thing here is serendipitous discovery — AlphaEvolve found a structure no one was looking for. That's different from "helping" math; it's starting to do something closer to mathematical intuition. The Venkatesh tension is real: more theorems per month doesn't automatically mean mathematics is healthier as a discipline, any more than more books automatically means better culture. The craft matters.
