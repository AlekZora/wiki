## Summary

Sequoia partner Julien Bek argues that the next $1T company will be a software firm disguised as a services firm — selling work rather than tools. AI has crossed a threshold where it can perform most "intelligence work" autonomously in many professions, turning copilots (tools) into autopilots (outcomes). The right wedge is outsourced, intelligence-heavy tasks where a budget line already exists; the long-term TAM is all labor spend.

## Key Points

- Intelligence work vs. judgement work: software engineering is mostly intelligence (translating specs to code, debugging); knowing what to build is judgement. AI crosses the intelligence threshold first.
- Copilot = sells the tool (professional is the customer). Autopilot = sells the work (buyer purchases an outcome directly, like Crosby selling NDA drafting to the company, not to outside counsel).
- For every $1 spent on software, $6 is spent on services. Autopilots target the service budget.
- The wedge is always where outsourcing already exists: the company has accepted external work can be done, a budget line exists, and the buyer is purchasing an outcome.
- Autopilot playbook: start with outsourced intelligence-heavy task → nail distribution → expand toward insourced, judgement-heavy work as AI compounds.
- Opportunity map across verticals (labor TAM): insurance brokerage ($140-200B), accounting/audit ($50-80B), healthcare revenue cycle ($50-80B), claims adjusting ($50-80B), tax advisory ($30-35B), legal transactional ($20-25B), IT managed services ($100B+), supply chain ($200B+), recruitment/staffing ($200B+), management consulting ($300-400B).
- Today's judgement becomes tomorrow's intelligence as AI accumulates proprietary domain data.

## Quotes

> "The next $1T company will be a software company masquerading as a services firm."

> "If you sell the tool, you're in a race against the model. But if you sell the work, every improvement in the model makes your service faster, cheaper, and harder to compete with."

> "Replacing an outsourcing contract with an AI-native services provider is a vendor swap. Replacing headcount is a reorg."

## My Take

The vendor swap vs. reorg insight is the most actionable thing here — it explains why outsourcing is the right entry point rather than going after internal headcount. The intelligence/judgement spectrum is a clean analytical tool for identifying which fields are ripe for autopilots. The "copilots face the innovator's dilemma" point at the end is sharp: selling the work means cutting your customers out of doing it.
