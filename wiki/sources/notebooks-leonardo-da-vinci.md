## Summary

A compilation of Leonardo's private notebooks — sketches, scientific observations, engineering designs, anatomical studies, philosophical reflections, and notes toward never-completed treatises on painting, water, flight, and anatomy. The notebooks were not intended for publication; they are raw thinking: unfinished, recursive, cross-disciplinary, and deeply personal. They constitute perhaps the greatest surviving document of a single human mind at work across domains.

## Key Ideas

- Leonardo wrote in mirror script (right to left) — possibly for secrecy, possibly because he was left-handed, possibly habit.
- The notebooks demonstrate what genuine cross-domain thinking looks like: observations in one field immediately trigger hypotheses in another. Flow of water → flow of blood → flow of wind → mechanics of bird flight → engineering of a flying machine.
- He rarely finished major projects — not out of laziness but because his curiosity about how things worked exceeded his interest in the finished artifact.
- His anatomical studies required dissecting dozens of bodies; his flight studies required years of observing birds. The notebooks show a scientist who insisted on primary observation rather than received authority.
- Many of his engineering concepts (tank, helicopter, solar power concentrator, armored vehicle) were centuries ahead of practical implementation.
- His painting notes treat light, shadow, and atmosphere as subjects for systematic scientific investigation rather than intuitive craft.

## My Take

Complementary to the Isaacson biography — where Isaacson contextualizes Leonardo's life, the notebooks are Leonardo's own voice, unmediated. The notebooks make it clear that his famous "procrastination" was usually extended investigation. He didn't finish the Adoration of the Magi because he got fascinated by the geometry of the crowd arrangement.
