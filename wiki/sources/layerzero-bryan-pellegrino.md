## Summary

A profile of Bryan Pellegrino, co-founder and CEO of LayerZero Labs, tracing his journey from world-class poker pro to blockchain infrastructure builder. After a decade-plus of online poker, Pellegrino found success hollow once he'd reached the top — no leverage, no meaning beyond money. He then pursued AI/ML (selling baseball pitch prediction tools to MLB clubs), then crypto, and ultimately built LayerZero: a foundational messaging protocol enabling frictionless, secure communication across all blockchains.

## Key Points

- Pellegrino started poker at 15 using his father's account, accumulated $500K+ in World Series of Poker winnings, was one of the world's best — then found it empty. "Once I got there, I realized, hey—there's no leverage. There's no anything."
- He spent years identifying "Holy Grail" problems worth his time: baseball ML tools sold to MLB clubs, then crypto infrastructure.
- DeepMind's Agent57 mastering all 57 Atari games from pixels alone inspired Pellegrino to pursue ML seriously.
- The genesis of LayerZero: trying to build an NFT gladiator game across Binance Smart Chain and Ethereum, they had to build their own bridge and found existing bridges horrifying from a security standpoint.
- $14 billion in crypto hacks in 2021 — mostly through vulnerable bridges.
- LayerZero is not a bridge but a generic messaging layer enabling direct cross-chain communication without centralized intermediaries. TCP/IP analogy: standardized protocol beneath all the apps.
- The Stargate app (March 2022) proved the concept: $250M in crypto moved in the first 10 days.
- Co-founders Ryan Zarick and Caleb Banister were friends from UNR computer network research labs.
- During Sequoia negotiations, Pellegrino deliberately didn't play poker — chose directness and partnership over extraction.
- Published a research paper on poker AI "Supremus" in 2020, cited by DeepMind.

## My Take

The poker-to-infrastructure arc is a clean story about transitioning from zero-sum competition (no leverage, pure redistribution) to positive-sum infrastructure building (the more people use it, the more valuable it becomes). The "utility payoff" reframe is simple but important: what is the impact of solving this problem beyond winning the game? The TCP/IP analogy for LayerZero is apt — boring but essential plumbing.
