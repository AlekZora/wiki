## Summary

Y Combinator's Spring 2026 list of startup ideas they want to fund. Focuses on AI-native workflows, new financial primitives, modernized industrial systems, and AI-human collaboration in physical work. Several ideas come directly from YC founders reporting opportunities they see on the frontier.

## Key Points

- **Cursor for Product Managers** (Andrew Miklas): Coding tools handle implementation once specs exist, but there's no AI system for the full discovery loop — user interviews → synthesis → feature definition → UI proposals → task breakdown for coding agents. The PM role (figuring out what to build) is the next frontier.
- **AI-native hedge funds** (Charlie Holtz): Quant trading is the 1980s analogy — computers seemed silly then, are now obvious. The next Renaissance/D.E. Shaw won't bolt AI onto existing strategies but will use it to find entirely new ones. Swarms of agents combing 10-Ks, earnings calls, SEC filings.
- **AI-native agencies** (Aaron Epstein): Agencies are margin-constrained by headcount. AI flips this: charge for the finished product (design, video ads, legal docs), use AI as the labor. Agency economics → software economics. Design firms winning work before the contract is signed by producing AI deliverables upfront.
- **Stablecoin financial services** (Daivik Goel): GENIUS and CLARITY Acts put stablecoins in the regulatory middle between DeFi and TradFi. Opportunity: compliant but crypto-native financial services — yield-bearing accounts, tokenized real-world assets, cross-border payments. Regulatory window is open now.
- **AI for government** (Tom Blomfield): Government will receive the flood of AI-generated form submissions but is still processing paper by hand. AI tools to modernize government intake, processing, and decision-making. Hard to sell, very sticky once in.
- **Modern metal mills** (Zane Hengsperger): US aluminum/steel mills have 8–30 week lead times, can't sell direct, thin margins despite high prices. Problem: legacy planning and scheduling systems. Opportunity: AI-driven production planning + MES + modern automation + smarter energy (on-site generation, next-gen nuclear). Software-defined mills.
- **AI guidance for physical work** (David Lieb): Real-time AI coaching via phone/AirPods/smart glasses for field workers (HVAC, nursing, manufacturing). AI sees through the worker's camera, reasons about the situation, talks them through the task. Three enablers now: multimodal models, ubiquitous hardware, skilled labor shortage.
- **Large spatial models** (Ryan McLinko): LLMs are constrained to language-expressible domains. Next wave requires first-class spatial reasoning — 2D/3D geometry, physical structure, mental rotation. Could define the next AI foundation model category.
- **Infra for government fraud hunters** (Garry Tan): Medicare alone loses tens of billions/year to fraud. False Claims Act qui tam provision lets private citizens sue and keep a percentage. Current process: painfully slow manual document review. Opportunity: intelligent systems (parse PDFs, trace corporate structures, produce complaint-ready filings) to 10x the speed of whistleblower law firms and state AGs.
- **Make LLMs easier to train** (Gabriel Birnbaum): Despite AI attention, LLM training tooling has barely improved. Broken SDKs, unreliable GPU instances, major open-source bugs, no good data management. Need: APIs abstracting training, databases for large datasets, ML-native dev environments.

## Quotes

> "Now instead of selling software to customers to help them do the work, you can charge way more by using the software yourself and selling them the finished product at 100x the price." — Aaron Epstein on AI-native agencies

## My Take

The PM tool idea (Cursor for PMs) is the most interesting — it frames the remaining gap clearly: once you have coding agents, the bottleneck becomes spec quality and product intuition, not implementation speed. The spatial models idea is the most ambitious and potentially category-defining. The physical work guidance angle is the most underhyped — massive labor markets, multimodal models are finally good enough, and the value is immediate and unambiguous. The fraud hunting infra idea is a very specific, high-value niche with a structural incentive (qui tam percentages) that aligns well with AI capabilities.
