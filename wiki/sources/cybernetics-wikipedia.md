## Summary

The Wikipedia overview of cybernetics — the transdisciplinary study of circular causal processes, especially feedback, in biological, technological, social, and cognitive systems. Covers the field's origins (1940s Macy conferences, Norbert Wiener), three waves of development, key concepts (feedback, autopoiesis, second-order cybernetics), and its relationships to AI, computer science, cognitive science, and systems theory.

## Key Points

- Core definition: study of **circular causal feedback processes** — where a system's outputs return as inputs and influence subsequent actions. Named after the Greek *kybernetes* (steersman): steering a ship by continuously responding to observed course deviation.
- Norbert Wiener coined the term in 1947, published *Cybernetics: Or Control and Communication in the Animal and the Machine* in 1948. His definition: "control and communication in the animal and the machine."
- Gregory Bateson's definition: "a branch of mathematics dealing with problems of control, recursiveness, and information, focuses on forms and the patterns that connect."
- **First wave (1940s–1960s)**: Technical focus. Macy conferences (1946–1953) brought together Wiener, von Neumann, Mead, Bateson, Ashby, von Foerster. Foundational texts: "Behavior, Purpose and Teleology" (1943) and McCulloch-Pitts neural network paper (1943). AI split off at Dartmouth (1956) and computer science separated; cybernetics fragmented.
- **Second wave (1960s–1980s)**: Social/philosophical focus. Maturana and Varela's **autopoiesis** (self-producing systems); Stafford Beer's **viable system model** (applied to Chilean economy under Allende in Project Cybersyn); second-order cybernetics (cybernetics of cybernetics — how the observer is part of the system). Bateson's family therapy applications.
- **Third wave (1990s–)**: Renewed interest via machine learning (neural nets), feminist technoscience, posthumanism, design disciplines. AI research explicitly returns to cybernetic foundations.
- **Key concepts**: negative feedback (reduces deviation from desired state), positive feedback (amplifies deviation), feedforward, recursion, reflexivity. Also: autopoiesis, black box, double bind theory, good regulator theorem, heterarchy, perceptual control theory, radical constructivism, second-order cybernetics, self-organisation, variety and requisite variety, viable system model.
- Many fields trace origins to cybernetics: AI, bionics, cognitive science, control theory, complexity science, computer science, information theory, robotics.
- Requisite variety (Ashby): a controller must have at least as much variety (possible states) as the system it is controlling.

## Quotes

> "All the existing terminology has too heavy a bias to one side or another... we have been forced to coin at least one artificial neo-Greek expression to fill the gap. We have decided to call the entire field of control and communication theory, whether in the machine or in the animal, by the name Cybernetics." — Norbert Wiener

## My Take

Cybernetics is the intellectual ancestor of a huge amount of modern AI and systems thinking, but it's rarely recognized as such. The three-wave framing is useful: the first wave gave us feedback loops and neural nets (which became ML); the second gave us the observer-dependent, self-referential perspective that later showed up in complex systems theory; the third is the current moment where we're back to taking the original synthesis seriously. Auto research and agentic loops are cybernetic systems in exactly Wiener's sense — outputs feeding back as inputs to modify future actions toward a goal.
