## Summary

Volume II of Beckmann's scholarly historical compendium cataloguing the origins of practical inventions, technologies, and discoveries from antiquity through the early modern period. Originally written in German in the late 18th century and translated into English by William Johnston, edited by William Francis and J. W. Griffith. The work traces individual inventions — playing cards, sugar, gunpowder, spectacles, lottery, clocks — through historical documents and philological analysis to establish their true origins, often debunking popular myths about who invented what and when.

## Key Ideas

- Beckmann was the first to systematically apply historical-philological method to the history of technology — he is considered a founder of the academic field of the history of technology.
- Many "obvious" inventions have surprisingly obscure or contested origins; Beckmann's method of tracking earliest documentary evidence was novel for its era.
- The book covers a wide range of subjects: food processing, games, textiles, medical practices, military technology, instruments of measurement.
- It is an 18th-century work, so some attributions and sources have been revised by subsequent scholarship.

## My Take

A reference work more than a reading work — most useful as a jumping-off point for tracing when specific technologies entered use. Interesting to read alongside more recent history-of-technology scholarship to see which of Beckmann's attributions have held up.
