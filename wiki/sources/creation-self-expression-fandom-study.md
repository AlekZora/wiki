## Summary

A short news item reporting Fandom's fourth annual "Inside Gaming" study. Stress relief is still the top self-reported reason people play, but 46% of gamers now cite "creation, imagination and self-expression" as a main motivation — up 10 percentage points from the prior year. The study further reports that players who game for self-expression are 30% more likely to *increase* their playtime, "feeling more drawn to the gaming world than ever before."

## Key Points

- 46% of players name creation/imagination/self-expression as a main gaming motivation (up ~10 pts year-over-year — a fast-moving trend, not a stable baseline).
- Stress relief remains the single top reason; self-expression is the *fastest-growing* one.
- Self-expression-motivated players are ~30% more likely to increase their gaming time — i.e., this motivation correlates with deepening engagement, not casual play.
- Source is a press summary of an industry report, not the underlying methodology. Treat the precise figures as directional.

## My Take

Thin as a source, but it supplies a dated, quantified data point for a claim the wiki keeps circling: players increasingly want games as a medium for *externalizing identity*, not just consuming content. The 30%-more-engagement figure is the load-bearing part — it links self-expression to persistence, which connects this directly to [[social-identity-theory]] and the Jansz persistence paper, and to the "why players recommend games" synthesis (recommendation as identity act). For a project whose thesis is that AI should be the *substance* of the experience, the relevant reframe is: if creation/self-expression is the rising motivation, then an AI that the player shapes — and that visibly carries the marks of how they shaped it — is a self-expression medium, not a feature. The artifact the player co-authors *is* the expression. Logged primarily as evidence feeding [[self-expression-as-play]].
