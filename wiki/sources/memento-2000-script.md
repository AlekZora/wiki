## Summary

The shooting script for *Memento* (2000), written by Christopher Nolan and based on Jonathan Nolan's short story "Memento Mori." The script opens in reverse — a Polaroid photograph of a dead man un-develops as the sequence plays backward — immediately establishing the film's central structural conceit: color sequences run in reverse chronological order, black-and-white sequences run forward. The protagonist Leonard, a man unable to form new memories since a home invasion that killed his wife, uses Polaroids and body tattoos as his external memory system while hunting his wife's killer.

## Key Ideas

- The two-track structure (color backward, B&W forward) is visible on page 1: the opening color sequence is labeled "COLOUR SEQUENCE" and the immediately following scene is labeled "BLACK AND WHITE SEQUENCE," establishing the alternating architecture from the first pages.
- The reverse structure means the audience always receives the effect of an action before learning its cause — epistemically mimicking Leonard's amnesia.
- The script is a shooting script dated August 10, 1999 (with revisions through October 1999), indicating this is the production version rather than a development draft.
- Opening image: a Polaroid photograph being un-exposed by being fanned, then sucked back into a Polaroid camera — the film's thesis about memory (it fades, it un-makes itself) stated visually before a single word of dialogue.

## My Take

Reading the first pages alongside the finished film reveals how precisely Nolan's visual storytelling is encoded in the script. The structural conceit isn't a post-production choice — it's baked into the page. The opening un-Polaroid sequence is a complete statement of theme in visual terms before any character or plot is established.
