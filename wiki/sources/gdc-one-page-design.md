## Summary
Stone Le Brandy, a creative director at EA Maxis, argues for the superiority of the "one-page design document" over traditional, text-heavy "design bibles" and fragmented wikis. He posits that long-form documentation is rarely read, hard to update, and fails to show the interconnectedness of game systems. In contrast, a well-crafted one-page design—a single, visually compelling, information-dense document—forces the designer to achieve clarity and conciseness. Drawing inspiration from architectural blueprints, LEGO instructions, and famous data visualizations, he demonstrates through examples from his work on Diablo, The Simpsons, and Spore how these documents can effectively communicate complex level flows, abstract mechanics, and entire game systems to the whole team, making them tools people actually want to engage with.

## Key Ideas
- Traditional "design bibles" are thorough but impractical; they are difficult to update, are rarely read cover-to-cover, and don't scale with modern team sizes.
- Design wikis, while easier to update, tend to fragment information into isolated pages, hiding the crucial relationships between different game systems.
- The core problem with most documentation is that team members don't read it due to its length and density. The solution is to create a single-page document that is visually engaging and concise.
- One-page designs are inspired by fields that excel at visual communication, like architecture (blueprints), toys (LEGO instructions), and data visualization (Napoleon's March diagram).
- The process of creating a one-page design forces the designer to distill a system to its essential components and highlights a system's underlying structure and interdependencies.
- These documents act as a "common focus" in meetings, ensuring everyone is looking at and discussing the same representation of the system.
- The physical act of printing and personally handing out these documents is crucial for team engagement and feedback. They are working documents meant to be written on.

## My Take
The speaker's manual process of wrestling a complex, multi-dimensional system (like Spore's 256-state consequence system) into an elegant, intuitive visualization is a perfect analogy for the core challenge of Explainable AI (XAI). This talk provides a powerful framework for how AI systems could communicate their internal state or decision-making processes to humans. An AI could be trained to automatically generate such a "one-pager" from its own complex internal logic, transforming an inscrutable wall of data into a visual map that highlights key relationships and causal chains. This would solve a major problem in AI development and deployment: making complex models understandable to their creators and users. The principles Le Brandy outlines—using a central visual metaphor, focusing on relationships over lists, and forcing conciseness—are a ready-made recipe for designing effective XAI outputs that cater to the limits and strengths of human cognition.
