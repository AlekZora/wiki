## Summary

The shooting script for *Inception* (2010), written by Christopher Nolan. The film follows Dom Cobb, a specialist in extracting information from people's dreams, who is tasked with the reverse: *inception* — planting an idea deep enough in a target's subconscious that he believes it originated naturally. The script opens in medias res at a beach scene (later revealed to be a dream-within-a-dream), immediately establishing the nested structure that is the film's central formal architecture.

## Key Ideas

- The script opens with an older Japanese man's dream — a scene that the audience won't fully understand until the film's final act. Nolan encodes the information asymmetry into the first pages structurally: the setting is recognizable only retrospectively.
- The dream-layer architecture: each nested level operates at a different time dilation (30 minutes of outer-world time equals hours in a dream layer), creating compounding information gaps between characters operating at different levels.
- The "totem" device (the spinning top, the pewter cone seen in the opening) functions as an in-story information-management tool — a private artifact whose behavior only the owner should know, designed to verify whether one is in reality or a dream.
- The screenplay is a shooting script (not a development draft), meaning dialogue, scene structure, and technical direction are final.

## My Take

*Inception* is the clearest example of Nolan's "diegetic puzzle reveals" — the audience and Cobb receive information at the same moment, so there's no separation between plot revelation and character discovery. The nested structure is elegant on the page: each level is clearly labeled and the reader always knows which layer they're in, making the audience's confusion in the cinema a deliberate choice to withhold the visual cues the script provides.
