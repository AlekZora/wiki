## Summary

Comprehensive Wikipedia overview of neuroplasticity — the brain's ability to reorganize and rewire neural connections in response to learning, experience, injury, or environmental change. Covers the history of the concept (from William James 1890 through Merzenich's modern work), the two main types (structural and functional), and a wide range of applications: addiction, stroke recovery, phantom limbs, chronic pain, meditation, music therapy, exercise, deafness, blindness, ADHD, multilingualism, and novel depression treatments.

## Key Points

- Structural neuroplasticity: physical changes to brain anatomy — new dendritic spines, axonal sprouting, neurogenesis
- Functional neuroplasticity: four subtypes — homologous area adaptation, map expansion, cross-modal reassignment, compensatory masquerade
- Long-term potentiation (LTP) and long-term depression (LTD) are the synaptic mechanisms underlying learning and memory
- Critical periods exist for each sensory system but can be reopened in adults
- Neuroplasticity was once thought limited to childhood; research since the 1970s shows it persists throughout life
- Music training, exercise, and meditation all produce measurable structural brain changes
- Chronic pain causes maladaptive reorganization — grey matter loss in prefrontal cortex; reversible with treatment
- Psychoplastogens (ketamine, psychedelics) rapidly promote neuroplasticity and have fast-acting antidepressant effects
- Multilingualism increases grey matter density in the inferior parietal cortex

## Quotes

> "The brain had to be plastic." — Michael Merzenich, after observing that severed and regenerated peripheral nerves produced nearly-normal cortical maps
