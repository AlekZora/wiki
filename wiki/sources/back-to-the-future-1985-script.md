## Summary

The fourth draft screenplay for *Back to the Future* (1985), written by Robert Zemeckis and Bob Gale, revised October 1984. The film follows Marty McFly, a teenager who is accidentally sent back to 1955 in a time-traveling DeLorean built by eccentric scientist Doc Brown. Stranded in the past, Marty must ensure his parents fall in love (to secure his own existence) and find a way to generate the 1.21 gigawatts of power needed to return to 1985.

## Key Ideas

- The script opens in Hill Valley's 1985 high school — establishing Marty's contemporary world through small period details before the time-travel disrupts it. The contrast between 1985 and 1955 is the film's primary comedic engine.
- The screenplay uses dramatic irony extensively: the audience understands the causal implications of Marty's actions in the past (threatening the timeline) while the 1955 characters are oblivious. This is the inverse of Nolan's preference — here the audience knows *more* than the characters.
- The time-travel mechanics are introduced through Doc Brown's meticulous explanation of the flux capacitor — exposition delivered in-character, motivated by the plot, which became a template for how Hollywood explains science-fictional devices.
- The script is marked as "Fourth Draft / Revised 10-12-84 / with pink revisions of 10-21-84" — a production-stage document, indicating all major story elements are locked.

## My Take

The *Back to the Future* script is a masterclass in motivated exposition: every piece of science-fictional world-building is delivered at the moment of maximum audience need, attached to a character's emotional urgency. Doc Brown's excitement is the vehicle for rules delivery. Compare this to Nolan's exposition-as-information-gap-closing — both solve the same problem (audience needs to understand the rules) but from opposite directions: Zemeckis delivers before the audience asks, Nolan withholds until the gap becomes the point.
