## Summary

An encyclopedic overview of narrative as a concept: its definition, basic elements (character, conflict, narrative mode, plot, setting, theme), history from ancient civilizations, and academic study (narratology). Covers how narrative functions across all human creative media and its relationship to identity and meaning-making.

## Key Points

- Basic narrative elements: character, conflict, narrative mode, plot, setting, theme
- Conflict types: character vs. character, nature, society, fate, self
- Narrative mode = the set of choices framing *how* a story is told (scope, language, medium, POV)
- Todorov's model: equilibrium -> disruption -> restoration of equilibrium
- Vladimir Propp identified 31 functional components in folk-tale plots
- Owen Flanagan: "We are inveterate storytellers" -- humans cast identity in narrative form
- Narrative is the oldest form of entertainment and a ubiquitous component of human communication
- Latin root: *narrare* (to tell), from *gnarus* (knowing/skilled)
- Aesthetics: narrative structure, temporality, characterization, multiple voices, rhetorical thrust

## My Take

Useful as foundational reference. The Todorov model (equilibrium-disruption-restoration) is worth consciously subverting in the sci-fi series -- what if restoration never comes, or what characters think is equilibrium was already a disruption? Propp's 31 functions could be interesting constraints for episode generation.

## Project Connection

The concept of "narrative mode" -- how information is framed and withheld -- is the core lever for the paranoid sci-fi series. The show's tension lives entirely in the gap between what the narrative mode reveals and what it conceals. Todorov's equilibrium model could structure each episode arc while the season-level arc is a slow disruption that never fully resolves.
