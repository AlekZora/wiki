## Summary

The Wikipedia overview of biotechnology — the application of biological organisms, systems, or processes to produce products and services. Covers the field's history from ancient fermentation and selective breeding through modern genetic engineering, its color-coded sub-disciplines, and its applications in medicine, agriculture, industry, and the environment.

## Key Points

- Definition: integration of natural and engineering sciences to achieve application of organisms and their parts for products/services (IUPAC). Term coined by Károly Ereky in 1919.
- Biotechnology in the broad sense is ancient: selective breeding of crops (Neolithic), fermentation for beer/bread/cheese (Mesopotamia, Egypt, China, India). Pasteur explained fermentation in 1857.
- Modern biotechnology typically dated to 1971 (Berg's gene splicing) and 1972 (Boyer & Cohen's plasmid transfer). Commercial viability enabled by *Diamond v. Chakrabarty* (1980 SCOTUS): GMOs can be patented.
- Genentech's 1978 synthetic insulin was the first genetically engineered medicine; previously extracted from pig/cattle pancreases.
- **Color-coded sub-disciplines**:
  - Red: medical/pharmaceutical (vaccines, antibiotics, gene therapy, pharmacogenomics)
  - Green: agricultural (GM crops, transgenic plants, biopesticides)
  - White/industrial: industrial processes (enzymes as catalysts, biofuels, biodegradable plastics)
  - Blue: marine resources (microalgae, bio-oils)
  - Yellow: food production (fermentation — wine, beer, cheese)
  - Gray: environmental (bioremediation, maintaining biodiversity)
  - Brown: arid land management (drought-resistant seeds)
  - Dark: bioterrorism/bioweapons
- **Medicine**: pharmacogenomics analyzes how genetic variation affects drug response — promises "personalized medicine." Gene therapy, biosensors (ISFET from 1970), genetic testing for inherited disease risk.
- **Agriculture**: GM crop area grew 94x between 1996–2011. Scientific consensus: currently available GM foods are no more risky than conventional food, but each case requires independent assessment. Public perception remains much more skeptical than scientific consensus.
- **Industrial**: synthetic biology + metabolic engineering for bio-based chemical production (e.g., CRISPR re-engineering of *E. coli* for 1,4-butanediol production for fiber manufacturing).
- **Ethical/regulatory tension**: genetic modification, IP rights over organisms, GMO regulation differs dramatically between US and EU. EU differentiates between cultivation approval and import/processing approval.

## Quotes

> "The utilization of biological processes, organisms or systems to produce products that are anticipated to improve human lives is termed biotechnology."

## My Take

A broad reference article — useful for orienting in the space. The most interesting aspect for current thinking is the convergence of AI and biotech: pharmacogenomics and personalized medicine require massive data processing, auto research applied to materials science or drug discovery has the same structure as Karpathy's ML experiment loops. The color taxonomy is a convenient shorthand. The gap between scientific consensus on GM food safety and public perception is a notable case study in how facts and risk perception diverge.
