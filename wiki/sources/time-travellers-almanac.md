## Summary

The definitive anthology of time travel fiction, collecting stories from H.G. Wells through the present. Organized thematically rather than chronologically: "Getting There" (the mechanics and experience of time travel), "Being There" (what it means to exist in another time), "Consequences" (what time travel does to those who use it and those they affect), "The Ethics and Politics of Time Travel," and "The Far Future and the End of Time." Over 70 stories from the genre's greatest practitioners.

## Key Ideas

- **Time travel as metaphor**: The best time travel fiction is not about mechanics—it's about memory, regret, historical determinism, identity, and the impossibility of changing the past even when you can visit it.
- **The tourist problem**: Many stories in the anthology explore what happens when time becomes just another tourist destination—history stripped of weight by accessibility.
- **Causality paradoxes as worldbuilding constraints**: Different stories adopt different rules (fixed timeline, many-worlds, mutable timeline) and each produces different ethical and narrative implications.
- **Time as experienced vs. time as measured**: Some of the strongest stories in the collection explore subjective time—the way memory works like time travel, the way aging is a kind of slow temporal displacement.
- **The ethics of intervention**: If you can change history, should you? The anthology represents every position—full intervention, strict non-intervention, and everything between.
- **Post-human and deep time**: Stories set millions of years in the future explore what time travel means when humanity has transformed beyond recognition or ceased to exist.

## My Take

The Almanac is most valuable as a survey of how a single speculative concept (time travel) generates an enormous range of philosophical and emotional territory depending on the rules you choose. For game design: time mechanics in games (Braid, Outer Wilds, Disco Elysium) are still underexplored relative to what the fiction has imagined. The causality loop narratives connect to the stub-timeline model in Gibson's The Peripheral. For AI: training data includes history up to a cutoff—in a sense, an AI system is always a time traveler from its training date, unable to change what happened but able to speak about it.
