## Summary

Marine biologist Professor Aronnax, his servant Conseil, and harpooner Ned Land are captured aboard the mysterious submarine *Nautilus*, commanded by the enigmatic Captain Nemo. Nemo has renounced humanity and retreated to the ocean, commanding a self-sufficient vessel and using its power to wage secret war against nations that wronged him. The narrative follows the Nautilus's global voyage — coral reefs, Antarctic ice, Atlantis, the Maelström — while Aronnax wrestles with his fascination for Nemo and Ned Land schemes to escape.

## Key Ideas

- Nemo is one of literature's great ambiguous figures: brilliant, principled, generous to the persecuted, yet murderous in his vengeance. He is simultaneously hero and villain, depending on the vantage.
- The *Nautilus* is a utopian self-sufficient world — powered by electricity from seawater, feeding from the ocean, beholden to no nation. Verne was exploring what total autonomy from society might look like.
- The novel is essentially a catalogue of oceanic wonders presented through Verne's extensive naturalist research — more encyclopedia than plot-driven narrative in places.
- Aronnax is corrupted by access: he stays longer than he should because the scientific opportunities are too great. This is a study in complicity through wonder.
- Twenty thousand leagues is a distance traveled (horizontal), not a depth — a common misreading.

## Quotes

> "The sea is everything. It covers seven tenths of the terrestrial globe... The sea is only the embodiment of a supernatural and wonderful existence."

## My Take

Nemo is the most interesting character Verne ever wrote precisely because he's never explained — his backstory is withheld, his nationality ambiguous, his motive partially concealed. Verne understood that mystery is more compelling than explanation.
