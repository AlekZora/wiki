## Summary

A comprehensive reference of narrative devices organized by category: Setting, Plot, Perspective, Style, and Theme. Each technique includes definition and examples from literature, film, and other media.

## Key Points

- **Plot techniques**: backstory, Chekhov's gun, cliffhanger, eucatastrophe, flashback/forward, foreshadowing, frame story, in medias res, MacGuffin, plot twist, red herring, unreliable narrator, ticking time bomb
- **Perspective techniques**: audience surrogate, author surrogate, fourth wall breaking, defamiliarization, first/second/third person, magical realism, multiperspectivity, stream of consciousness
- **Style techniques**: allegory, bathos, distancing effect, dramatic visualization, hyperbole, imagery, leitwortstil, satire, pastiche
- **Theme techniques**: irony (situational, dramatic, verbal), metaphor, thematic patterning
- Unreliable narrator: narrator misleads reader, hiding events or motivations (e.g., Murder of Roger Ackroyd)
- Multiperspectivity: multiple viewpoints incorporating various perspectives on same events
- Defamiliarization: making familiar things strange to force fresh perception (Shklovsky)

## My Take

This is a toolbox reference. Most relevant to the sci-fi series: unreliable narrator, multiperspectivity, dramatic irony (audience knows what characters don't), frame story, in medias res, and the distancing effect. The combination of dramatic irony + multiperspectivity is essentially the show's core mechanic -- audience gradually sees all the hidden layers.

## Project Connection

Direct utility for the sci-fi series. Key techniques to deploy: dramatic irony (audience knows hidden agendas before characters reveal them), multiperspectivity (each episode could shift POV), unreliable narration (characters' internal monologue vs. truth), frame story (official logs vs. reality), and defamiliarization (making the familiar station environment feel alien through shifting context).
