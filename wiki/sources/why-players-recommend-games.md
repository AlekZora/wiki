## Summary

An AI-generated synthesis of behavioral research on why players recommend games to friends. The central claim: recommendation is a byproduct of genuine play value, not a lever you can pull with referral mechanics — and a JAR study found referral incentives had *no* significant positive effect and a *negative* effect when players already felt a sense of control. "Play value" decomposes into five psychological drivers: mastery/competence signaling, self-expression/identity, shared-experience potential, emotional peak moments, and community identity. The unifying thesis: recommendation is an emotional and social act of self-presentation and bonding, not a rational consumer judgment. It also notes a structural amplifier — teens are ~1.5× more likely to become friends when they share a gaming interest — so games don't just spread through existing networks, they generate the ties that spread them.

## Key Points

- **Core finding:** intrinsic experience drives recommendation; extrinsic referral incentives do not, and *backfire* when the player already feels autonomous. This is the overjustification effect at the recommendation layer (consistent with [[intrinsic-motivation]]).
- Five drivers of "play value": (1) mastery/competence signaling — sharing says "I succeeded at this"; (2) self-expression/identity (the 46% Fandom stat); (3) shared-experience potential (co-op converts solo fun into social currency); (4) emotional peak moments (psychophysiology: arousal creates durable, retellable memories); (5) community identity (insider language, distinct aesthetic).
- **Identity affiliation is the key pathway:** players recommend games that reinforce who they are or want to be seen as — "I'm a FromSouls player," "I'm a Minecraft builder." Recommendation is identity expression, not advice. (Cites the Jansz SDT/social-identity paper directly.)
- **Friendship-formation amplifier:** shared gaming interest makes teens ~1.5× more likely to become friends — games create social ties, not just travel along them. Compounding network effect for games with distinct cultural identity.
- Recommendation is emotional and social, never a filled-out satisfaction survey. The design levers per driver are tabulated (skill expression, build/aesthetic variety, co-op, pacing/set-pieces, insider vocabulary).

## My Take

This is a secondary synthesis (AI-generated, citing sources several of which are already in this same ingestion batch — Variety/Fandom, the Jansz persistence paper, the Try Evidence psychophysiology piece). Its value is not new primary data but the *integration*: it shows the batch's threads converge on one mechanism — recommendation = identity expression + emotional peak, immune to and degraded by extrinsic incentives. For this project, given Julien's documented distribution weakness (see memory), this is strategically the most important takeaway in the batch: organic spread is not a growth-hack to bolt on; it is an emergent property of intrinsic value, identity-fit, and a retellable emotional peak. A game whose substance is a player-shaped AI is unusually well-positioned on three of the five drivers at once (self-expression, community identity, emotional peak — the AI doing something the player didn't expect *is* the retellable moment). Treat the specific stats (1.5×, JAR negative effect) as directional pending primary sources; spawns [[recommendation-as-identity]].
