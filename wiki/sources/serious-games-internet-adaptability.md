## Summary

A viewpoint paper from JMIR Games proposing a 3-stage framework and 6 design principles for using serious games (SGs) to enhance adolescents' internet adaptability (IA). Draws on both Chinese and international research. Internet adaptability means the ability to navigate online environments safely, responsibly, and effectively. The authors argue SGs work through contextualized design, real-time feedback, and dynamic tasks, producing experiential learning, self-regulation, and skill transfer.

## Key Points

- Internet adaptability (IA) is framed as a competency crucial for adolescent mental health and online behavior
- 3-stage mechanism: contextualized design → real-time feedback → dynamic tasks, leading to experiential learning → self-regulation → transfer of online skills
- 6 core design principles:
  1. Clear goal definition
  2. Interaction diversity
  3. Contextual authenticity (realistic digital scenarios)
  4. Immediate, scaffolding, and explanatory feedback
  5. Dynamically adaptive learning environment
  6. Safety-by-design for digital well-being
- Cross-contextual perspective: principles apply across different cultural and educational environments
- Emphasis on SGs as scalable, accessible intervention tools (vs. classroom-only approaches)

## My Take

The 6 principles are clean and applicable beyond internet adaptability — they describe good learning game design generally. The scaffolding feedback principle (immediate + explanatory, not just corrective) and the dynamically adaptive environment are particularly strong. The paper's real value is as a compact framework for thinking about game-mediated behavior change.
