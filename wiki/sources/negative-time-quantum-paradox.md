## Summary
Richard Fisher surveys three related quantum temporal anomalies. First, negative group delay: 1990s experiments in which light wave packets appear to exit a barrier before entering — explained by wave packet reshuffling, not actual time reversal. Second, negative duration: recent measurements by Aephraim Steinberg's group at the University of Toronto showing that photons appear to spend negative time inside a barrier, measured indirectly via atomic excitation; unlike negative delay, this has no clear physical interpretation and cannot be explained by wave reshuffling. Third, retrocausality: the theoretical proposal that particles can influence the past from the future, offered as a resolution to quantum entanglement's "spooky action at a distance" — instead of information passing instantaneously across space between entangled particles, it passes through time. The article concludes with physicist Emily Adlam's preferred interpretation: retrocausality makes most sense if we live in a block universe, where all moments of time coexist simultaneously in a 4D object and time has no intrinsic flow.

## Key Points
- Negative group delay: a wave packet peak exits a barrier before it enters; explained by selective absorption or ejection of photons inside the barrier, which reshapes the forward tail into a new peak; no particle experiences negative time; causality intact
- Negative duration: measured by tracking atomic excitation inside a barrier as photons pass; the monitor reads negative — no physical correspondence exists; currently unexplained beyond the mathematics
- Quantum entanglement produces particle correlations that cannot be explained by any common prior cause (Bell's theorem); particles have no definite properties until measured
- Retrocausality: proposed resolution to non-locality — information passes backward in time to the moment of entanglement, then forward to measurement, rather than instantaneously across space
- Block universe (Adlam's preferred frame): all temporal moments coexist in a 4D object; time is a dimension, not a flow; retrocausality needs no backward-traveling signal because everything already exists at its coordinates within the block
- Physical laws at quantum scales are time-symmetric; the arrow of time is a macroscopic phenomenon, not fundamental

## Quotes
> "We understand mathematically why it's happening, but we don't know how to talk about the physical meaning of it. There's no clear correspondence to the world that we know... And so we're just left saying, 'Okay, well, I guess quantum mechanics are just different'." — Josiah Sinclair, MIT

> "It turns out quantum mechanics says there might be one process — one event — that's described by multiple time scales." — Aephraim Steinberg

> "There's no temporal flow. Time is just another dimension within the block, rather than being a material thing that moves." — Emily Adlam

## My Take
Accessible science journalism on genuinely strange physics. Fisher does well to separate the explained (negative delay via wave packet reshuffling) from the genuinely unexplained (negative duration). The most philosophically generative part is Adlam's block universe interpretation — not a new idea, but placed here as the cleanest way to make quantum temporal weirdness mathematically consistent without requiring paradox.

The AI intersection has two specific angles. First, LLMs exist in a de facto block universe with respect to their training data: all moments of the training corpus coexist simultaneously in the model's weights, with no directional flow. A text from 1995 and a text from 2024 are equally "now" inside the model. This creates LLM temporal blindness — the model cannot intrinsically distinguish recent from old information because its internal experience of training time has no arrow. Time, for an LLM, really is just another dimension. Second, backward induction in AI planning is already retrocausal reasoning in practice: the algorithm starts from a known future state (treated as already-existing) and reasons backward to find optimal present actions. The block universe framing makes this structurally legible — it's not metaphorical, it's the same operation.
