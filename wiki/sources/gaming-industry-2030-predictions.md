## Summary

IGN asked 30+ senior figures from Xbox, Google, Blizzard, Capcom, SEGA, Riot, Niantic, Square Enix, and many others to predict what gaming would look like in 2030. Published in mid-2020 (before the pandemic fully hit). Respondents covered technology, business models, development practices, and culture. Looking back at these predictions from 2026 is itself useful.

## Key Points

**Technology — Cloud/Streaming:**
- Near-universal consensus that streaming/cloud gaming would become the dominant delivery mechanism. 5G + fiber + satellite seen as the enablers. Hardware under TVs would largely disappear.
- Prediction: phones in 2030 more powerful than 2020 desktop PCs (J. Allen Brack, Blizzard).
- Consoles may not fully disappear but home hardware would be reduced to "ultra-high-resolution screens and evolved input devices."

**Technology — VR/AR:**
- Much more divided. Some (Warhorse Studios, SEGA) enthusiastic; others (Riot, Traveller's Tales) skeptical mainstream VR arrives by 2030.
- AR/mixed reality seen as more plausible for mainstream — HoloLens as a proof of concept, but price and form factor still limiting.
- Niantic (Pokemon GO) excited about AR merging digital and physical spaces for gameplay.

**Technology — AI and NPCs:**
- Phil Harrison (Google/Stadia): AI and natural language would let players have "truly believable conversations with characters" who remember you and your backstory.
- Yoshinori Kitase (Final Fantasy VII Remake): Story would become real-time and player-driven — branching beyond pre-set routes, with AI generating story developments that even developers didn't predict.
- Paul Sage (Gearbox): Cloud-based AI speech enabling NPCs with personality profiles and believable "filler" dialogue around hand-crafted scripted content.
- AI seen as enabler for procedural content creation (reducing cost of level design, animation, NPC behavior).

**Business and Industry:**
- Subscription services (Game Pass-style) predicted to become primary access model alongside digital purchase.
- Console wars predicted to end or become irrelevant by 2030 (cloud makes hardware platform less important).
- Consolidation trend expected to continue, then be followed by new fragmentation (new platforms, new genres enabling small teams).
- Development model shift: Hollywood contracting model predicted, with project-based teams assembled and disbanded, rather than massive permanent studios.
- Middle tier of development (reasonable budget, specialist titles) seen as critically needed — the AAA vs. tiny indie split is unsustainable.
- Crunch culture: multiple respondents hope (but aren't confident) it would be resolved by 2030.

**Games and Audience:**
- Gaming predicted to become as mainstream and culturally accepted as film/TV. The generation who grew up gaming would be having children of their own.
- Demographics expanding: 35–40+ gamers as a growing, underserved segment.
- More diversity in game content and developer teams.
- Games would merge further with other entertainment: Netflix-style interactive experiences, blurring with film and streaming.
- Player creation and ownership (user-generated content, cross-play progression) predicted to grow significantly. One respondent mentioned blockchain for digital asset ownership.
- Esports would continue growing but a few dominant titles would take most viewership.

**Notable outlier (Yoko Taro, NieR: Automata):** Predicted a future of "A points" — new social currency (social media followers, credit scores as currency) — people killing each other for A points just as they do for money today. A darkly accurate observation about social media dynamics.

**Bill Gates quote (cited by Ville Heijari, Rovio):** "We always overestimate the change that will occur in the next two years and underestimate the change that will occur in the next ten."

## Quotes

> "In future generations, could we imagine ten thousand people working on a game? I don't think so. I believe that by 2030, the technology that underpins content creation has to evolve at a much faster rate." — Phil Harrison, Google

> "There will be some big changes in ways we can't even imagine right now." — Keith Schuler, Gearbox

> "Atsushi Inaba, Chief Creative Officer, PlatinumGames: I have no idea at all [what the games industry will be like in 2030]."

## My Take

Reading this in 2026, cloud gaming didn't dominate as predicted — most people still buy/download games. VR didn't go mainstream. The AI NPC predictions were right directionally but most games haven't implemented it yet. Conversational AI in games is starting to appear. The subscription model (Game Pass, PS Plus) did expand. Consolidation went further than predicted (Microsoft/Activision, etc.). The most accurate observation was probably the cultural mainstreaming of gaming — it's fully normalized now, and the demographic is older and more diverse. The article is interesting as a baseline for how hard it is to predict technology adoption curves even for industry insiders.
