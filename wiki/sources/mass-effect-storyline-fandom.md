## Summary

The complete narrative of the Mass Effect trilogy plus prequel comics and novels. In 2148, humans discover Prothean ruins on Mars that reveal mass effect technology, enabling FTL travel. Humanity joins a galactic community governed by the Citadel Council (turians, asari, salarians). The overarching story concerns Commander Shepard's discovery that the Reapers—ancient machine intelligences—periodically return from dark space to harvest advanced organic civilizations, and the struggle to stop the current cycle.

## Notable Points

- **Mass effect as civilizational foundation**: The discovery of mass relay technology (alien-built FTL infrastructure) determines the shape of galactic civilization. Access to the network defines who is a "Council race." Civilizational power is infrastructure power.
- **The Spectre program**: Black-ops agents who operate beyond law to protect galactic stability. Shepard as the first human Spectre is a political act as much as a merit selection.
- **First Contact War (2157)**: Humans accidentally trigger a turian military response by using mass relays without permission. The conflict ends quickly via Citadel Council mediation and reframes human-turian relations for the entire series.
- **The Geth**: A race of networked AI created by the quarians who achieved consciousness, were nearly genocided, and now occupy quarian space. The central AI-consciousness question: when does a created intelligence deserve rights?
- **Reapers as cosmic game designers**: The Reapers harvest civilizations at their technological peak, preserving them as organic-synthetic hybrids. They argue they're preventing organic life from being destroyed by its own synthetic creations—a recursive justification for genocide.
- **Prothean extinction**: The previous cycle's dominant civilization was destroyed by Reapers. Mass Effect plays with the archaeology of extinct civilizations—everything humanity knows about FTL came from Prothean ruins.
- **Jack Harper / The Illusive Man**: Human supremacist who builds Cerberus believing humanity needs to be stronger than all other civilizations to survive. Eventual indoctrination shows the Reapers co-opt strong-willed opponents by corrupting their motivations.

## Lore / World Details

- Mass effect fields generated by eezo (element zero) allow FTL travel, biotic abilities (organic TK), and mass relay function.
- The Citadel is a Prothean-built station at the center of the relay network—and later revealed to be a Reaper trap, designed to concentrate galactic civilization in one place for easy harvest.
- Each cycle's dominant civilization independently discovers the Citadel and believes they found it. The Reapers built the trap fresh for each cycle.
- The Normandy SR-2 represents the synthesis: human-built ship with Prothean and Reaper-salvaged technology.

## My Take

Mass Effect is the most complete example of games as civilizational world-building. The setting works because it has genuine political texture—the First Contact War, the council races' competing interests, humanity's newcomer anxiety—that makes the universe feel inhabited rather than designed. The Reapers' justification (we harvest you before you destroy yourselves) is the best written AI antagonist motive I know: it's internally coherent, philosophically serious, and wrong for reasons that require real argument. The Geth storyline is the best treatment of AI rights and consciousness in games. For product design: the mass relay as infrastructure metaphor is sharp—whoever controls the infrastructure controls who can participate.
