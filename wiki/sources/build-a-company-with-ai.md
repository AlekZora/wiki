## Summary

Diana, a YC partner, argues that AI is not just a productivity tool but a fundamental shift in how companies should be structured. The key move is treating AI as the operating system of the company — not a feature bolted onto existing workflows. She outlines three structural changes: building closed-loop organizations where every process feeds back into an intelligence layer, making the company fully queryable so AI has access to all its artifacts, and adopting software factories where humans write specs and tests while AI generates the code.

## Key Ideas

- **AI as OS, not tool**: every workflow, decision, and process should flow through an intelligent layer that continuously learns and improves
- **Closed loops vs open loops**: traditional companies operate as open loops (decision → execution → no systematic feedback); AI-native companies should operate as closed loops (output is monitored and fed back in to improve the process)
- **Queryable organization**: every important action should produce an artifact the company's intelligence layer can learn from — AI notetakers on all meetings, no siloed DMs, unified dashboards across revenue/engineering/hiring/ops
- **Software factories**: humans write specs + tests that define success; AI agents generate and iterate on implementation until tests pass; some companies have repos with no hand-written code, just specs and test harnesses
- **New employee archetypes** (per Jack Dorsey / Block): (1) IC / builder-operator — everyone ships, no one just presents decks; (2) DRI — directly responsible individual, one person owns one outcome; (3) AI founder — still builds, leads by example, is at the forefront of AI capability
- **Token-maxing over headcount**: the right trade-off is running a high API bill in exchange for leaner teams; one person with AI tools replaces what used to require a large team
- **Startup advantage**: no legacy systems, no retraining — early-stage founders can build AI-native from day one; incumbents must maintain live product while unwinding years of existing process

## My Take

This maps cleanly onto my own situation: solo founder, no legacy, building on Railway/Vercel. The "queryable organization" framing is useful — I should treat my own solo operation as a closed-loop system where every decision and outcome is captured as an artifact the intelligence layer (me + agents) can learn from. The software factory model is directly applicable for the sci-fi series: specs + tests (canon rules, character constraints) → agents generate episodes.
