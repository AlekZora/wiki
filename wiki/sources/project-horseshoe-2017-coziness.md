## Summary

A design think-tank report (practitioner consensus, not empirical research) that defines
coziness as how strongly something evokes **safety, abundance and softness**. Players' lower
needs are met, so attention can rise to higher ones such as beauty, belonging and mastery. The
report's most useful move is a list of what *negates* coziness. Anything that creates a pressing
lower-order need pulls attention down and the feeling disappears. The list includes extrinsic
rewards, danger, **responsibility**, **notifications**, intense stimuli, vast unknowable spaces,
non-consensual social presence, insincerity and opulence. From there it covers aesthetics,
mechanics (intrinsically rewarding, optional, safe rituals), narrative, business benefits
(retention through fewer churn triggers, nicer communities), and the ethics of monetizing
coziness.

## Notable Points

- **Definition:** safety (no impending loss; activities voluntary and opt-in), abundance (nothing lacking or pressing), softness (gentle stimuli, slower tempo, manageable scope, authenticity).
- **Negation by need:** "High priority, low order needs get dealt with first." Every pressing need, from an extrinsic reward to an obligation, displaces coziness.
- **Responsibility** is named directly: "mandatory maintenance, needy pets, companions, or entities that require constant, non-optional care."
- **Notifications** as the worked example of negation: intense stimulus, a new responsibility, non-consensual, often deceptive. "It is absolutely possible to design consensual notifications … but most do not."
- **Contrast enhances:** danger *outside* the safe space (rain on the window) deepens coziness; danger *inside* it (rain through a broken window) destroys it.
- **Distance:** vast spaces feel unsafe because they are unknowable. A small, bounded refuge inside a large space (a campfire in a wood) restores it.
- **Safe rituals:** activities that are safe, *known* (won't eat unexpected time or resources), and relaxed (low mental cost).
- **Emergent extrinsic rewards:** cozy activities decay once players start min-maxing. Watch for that, obscure the feedback, and rebalance rewards.
- **Retention:** coziness reduces churn by removing demotivating triggers, not by adding motivating ones.
- **Monetization ethics:** "Don't be the doctor who poisons their patient and then sells the cure." Sell things that meet real needs. Some scarcity is fine only as an opt-in part of a larger cozy whole.
- **Community:** mechanics generate emotions, emotions attract players, and players spread norms. Coziness has to exist at the systems level (The Sims Online had a cozy theme over zero-sum systems and became "a virtual dystopia").

## What It Attempted

To name a design quality that players clearly seek but designers lacked words for. It builds a
working definition, a vocabulary of what supports it and what breaks it, and patterns across
aesthetics, mechanics and narrative. It also makes the business case.

## Where It Succeeded / Where It Failed

**Succeeded:** the negation list is precise and usable as a checklist, the most reusable part of
the report. The contrast principle and "coziness at the systems level" are both sharp.
**Limits:** it's an opinion report from one 2017 workshop, with anecdotes rather than data (the
community comparison is one studio's two games). It treats coziness as player-dependent, which is
honest but makes it hard to measure.

## My Take

This fills the vault's cozy-games gap and pulls straight against a goal app. **A goal *is* a
responsibility,** and responsibility is on the negation list. So a goal app can't be cozy all the
way through. It can only make cozy spaces *inside* a responsibility: the map as a refuge, the
daily check-in as a safe ritual (known, short, low cost), and the goal's pressure kept "outside
the window" as contrast rather than brought into the room. Notifications, nagging and needy
companions are named as coziness killers almost word for word. That gives independent backing to
the vault's [compulsion-vs-craft](../concepts/compulsion-vs-craft.md) line.

**AI lens:** an AI assistant is a likely source of every negating factor: unprompted interruptions,
a companion that needs tending, social presence nobody asked for, and the insincerity of
engagement-tuned warmth. The report's warning that "coziness can be weaponized" to lower purchase
barriers applies directly to emotionally warm AI products. The design target the report implies
for an assistant is to **meet needs without creating new ones**. See [coziness](../concepts/coziness.md).
