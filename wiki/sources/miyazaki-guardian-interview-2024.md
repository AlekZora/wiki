## Summary

Guardian interview with Hidetaka Miyazaki at Summer Game Fest 2024, timed with the launch of *Elden Ring: Shadow of the Erdtree*. Miyazaki discusses his worldview ("the world was quite a harsh place"), how Elden Ring balanced harshness with moments of beauty to find a wider audience, why difficulty cannot be dialed down without breaking the game, and his philosophy of building a studio culture that allows failure. He also reflects on how the world may have "caught up" to FromSoftware's bleak aesthetic in the post-pandemic era.

## Summary

Keza MacDonald's profile of Miyazaki as FromSoftware president, taken during the launch of Shadow of the Erdtree. At 25 million copies sold, Elden Ring transformed FromSoftware from a cult studio into a genuine blockbuster maker — but Miyazaki refuses to assume success will repeat.

## Notable Points

- **Conservative forecasting as design principle**: "No decision is based on any assumption that we did it once, it's going to happen again. Allowing for this rather conservative forecast gives us room to fail — and that in turn results in better games."
- **Difficulty as the point**: lowering difficulty would strip the game of the sense of achievement that *is* the experience. "Turning down difficulty would strip the game of that joy — which, in my eyes, would break the game itself."
- **Elden Ring's beauty**: intentionally added color and "high fantasy" moments so players could "come up for air" between harsh stretches — unlike the unrelenting grimness of earlier games.
- **Failure as growth**: Miyazaki got his start taking over a struggling project (eventually *Demon's Souls*) where failure didn't matter. He tries to recreate that psychological safety for younger directors.
- **World-as-harsh-place**: "I have always felt the world was quite a harsh place. I've always lived my life with that worldview." Suggests the post-pandemic world may have converged on this feeling.
- **The moment on the icy road**: the famous origin of DS multiplayer — stuck cars on a winter hill, strangers pushing together. "A connection of mutual assistance between transient people. Oddly, that incident will probably linger in my heart for a long time."
- **Dream game**: "I am planning on making that game." If his next game is a catastrophic failure: "I've had my shot, and I'd be OK with that."
- **Self-knowledge**: "If I were to go on this soul-searching journey to find out why, I might be disappointed in myself. Perhaps I intentionally avoid doing that. It keeps the creative spring flowing."

## Lore / World Details

Shadow of the Erdtree takes players to the Lands of Shadow — an amber-skied echo of the Lands Between — with FromSoftware's most difficult content to date. Confirmed: no Elden Ring sequel; Erdtree is the end of the story.

## My Take

The line about avoiding self-understanding to keep the creative spring flowing is one of the most honest things I've read from any creator. Most artists overexplain their work. Miyazaki suspects that full self-knowledge would reveal something mundane, and actively refuses to look. That's a coherent creative philosophy, not false modesty.
