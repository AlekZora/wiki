## Summary

Britannica entry on John von Neumann — Hungarian-born American mathematician and polymath who was among the most important scientific figures of the 20th century. By his mid-twenties he was considered one of the world's foremost mathematicians. He is credited with pioneering game theory and, alongside Alan Turing and Claude Shannon, is considered a conceptual inventor of the stored-program digital computer. The clipped source has very little body content beyond the metadata description.

## Key Points

- Prodigy-to-polymath trajectory; foundational contributions across pure mathematics, quantum mechanics, economics (game theory), and computer architecture
- Game theory: formalized the mathematical framework for strategic interaction — directly relevant to information asymmetry and hidden agendas
- Stored-program computer architecture ("von Neumann architecture") underlies virtually all modern computers
- Worked at Princeton's Institute for Advanced Study alongside Einstein; contributed to the Manhattan Project
