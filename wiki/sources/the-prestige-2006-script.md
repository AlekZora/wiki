## Summary

The screenplay for *The Prestige* (2006), written by Christopher and Jonathan Nolan, adapted from Christopher Priest's novel. The film follows two rival stage magicians in Victorian London — Robert Angier and Alfred Borden — whose professional rivalry escalates into obsessive, mutual destruction. The script opens mid-performance with Cutter's voiceover explaining the three-part structure of every magic trick (the Pledge, the Turn, the Prestige), which is simultaneously the film's structural blueprint.

## Key Ideas

- Cutter's opening voiceover — "Every magic trick consists of three parts, or acts" — announces that the film is itself structured as a magic trick: the audience is shown something ordinary (Pledge), that thing seems to disappear (Turn), and then it must be brought back in an astonishing way (Prestige).
- Opening image: top hats clustered in a glade, black cats slinking between them — a visual mystery whose meaning becomes clear only at the film's climax (the cloning machine's byproduct).
- First line: "Are you watching closely?" — Borden's direct address to the audience, framing the entire film as a test of observation.
- The film's double-narrative structure (Angier reading Borden's diary, Borden reading Angier's diary) means both characters, and the audience, are receiving information filtered through their enemy's perspective.
- The central twist operates as a retroactive recontextualization: once revealed, every prior scene reads entirely differently.

## My Take

The magic-trick-as-film-structure conceit is one of cinema's most elegant meta-structural moves. The film does exactly what it promises: it shows you the trick, disappears the meaning, and then restores it in a transformed state. The diary-within-diary narration structure also means the film's information asymmetry is explicitly thematized — both characters are being misled by the framing of the other's text, and so is the audience.
