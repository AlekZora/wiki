## Summary

Fabliaux are short comic narrative poems from medieval France (c. 1150-1400), typically 300-400 lines in octosyllabic couplets. They are characterized by sexual and satirical content, subversion of social hierarchies, and criticism of the clergy and nobility. About 150 survive. They are considered the first expression of literary realism in Europe and directly influenced Boccaccio's Decameron, Chaucer's Canterbury Tales, and eventually the modern short story.

## Key Points

- Anonymous or attributed to jongleurs and clerics; notable authors include Jean Bodel and Rutebeuf
- Derived from courtly love traditions but inverted them with realism and bawdiness
- Cast includes cuckolded husbands, rapacious clergy, foolish peasants, thieves, and tricksters
- Gender role subversion is a recurring theme (e.g., "Berangier of the Long Ass")
- Audience debated: bourgeois (Bedier) vs. noble (Nykrog) vs. universal
- Humor as primary purpose, but also served as social commentary during political instability
- No direct Western literary predecessor; possibly brought from the East by crusaders
- Force derives heavily from wordplay, puns, paranomasia, and catachresis
- Replaced by prose short story in 16th century; influenced Moliere, La Fontaine, Voltaire

## My Take

Fabliaux are a fascinating early example of information asymmetry as entertainment. Many plots hinge on characters knowing things others don't (cuckolding, disguise, trickery). The genre's relationship to courtly love is essentially a parody layer -- the "official" elevated narrative vs. the "real" base motivations underneath. This mirrors the two-layer structure of the sci-fi series perfectly.

## Project Connection

The fabliau's core mechanic is deception within confined social structures -- exactly the dynamic of the paranoid contained sci-fi concept. Characters maintain a public face while pursuing hidden agendas. The genre proves this tension has been entertaining audiences for 800+ years. The "priest who peeked" and cuckolding plots are all about surveillance and secrets in tight spaces.
