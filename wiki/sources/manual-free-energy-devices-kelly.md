## Summary

A 1986 compendium by D.A. Kelly (Electrodyne Corporation) surveying alternative and unconventional energy devices — primarily from researchers working outside mainstream physics. The book covers Tesla's resonant transformer systems, T. Henry Moray's radiant energy device, the Searl Effect Generator, Viktor Schauberger's implosion vortex work, Joseph Newman's gyroscopic motor, the "N Machine" (homopolar generator), and various overunity motor/generator claims. Framed as a reference and research guide for experimenters and engineers interested in non-conventional energy.

## Key Ideas

- **Tesla as foundation**: Much of the book treats Tesla's high-frequency resonant work (particularly the magnifying transmitter and his Wardenclyffe ambitions) as the legitimate scientific foundation that later researchers built upon or claimed to extend.
- **Moray's radiant energy device**: T. Henry Moray claimed in the early 20th century to draw energy from background radiation using a specialized detector. Kelly presents his device with schematics and discusses the Swedish government's interest before dismissing it as overunity fraud.
- **Searl Effect**: John Searl's claimed rotating magnetic disk generator, alleged to produce levitation and excess energy. Kelly documents but doesn't verify.
- **Schauberger's vortex implosion**: Viktor Schauberger believed nature's energy was in implosion (inward collapse) rather than explosion, and designed water turbines and air vortex motors based on this. His work has never been reproducibly demonstrated but remains influential in fringe energy circles.
- **Newman motor**: Joseph Newman's gyroscopic energy machine, which was the subject of a US Patent Office dispute and Congressional hearing in the 1980s. Kelly covers it as contemporary.
- **Homopolar/N Machine**: The Faraday disk / homopolar generator claims of Bruce DePalma — that a spinning permanent magnet produces more output than input. Not reproducibly demonstrated.
- **Purpose of the book**: Kelly frames this as a research resource for people who believe suppressed or overlooked technologies exist. The editorial stance is credulous but not uncritical.

## Quotes

> "The energy crisis is not a crisis of resources but a crisis of imagination and political will." (paraphrased editorial stance)

## My Take

This is a historical artifact of 1980s fringe energy research, useful primarily as a map of the intellectual territory and figures that recur in alternative energy discourse (Tesla, Moray, Schauberger, Searl). None of the overunity claims in this book have been validated. The Tesla material is the most legitimate — his actual patents and experimental work are documented elsewhere more rigorously. The value here is understanding the cultural transmission of these ideas: how they persist, mutate, and attract continued interest despite non-reproducibility. Schauberger's biomimetic intuitions about water and vortex flow are the most interesting strand — stripped of the overunity claims, they point at real phenomena in fluid dynamics.
