## Summary

Jack Dorsey (Block CEO) and Roelof Botha (Sequoia) discuss Dorsey's manifesto "From Hierarchy to Intelligence." Block cut 40% of its workforce and is rebuilding around an AI "intelligence layer" — a world model of the company built from all its artifacts (Slack, email, pull requests, meetings). Dorsey proposes collapsing corporate hierarchy to just three roles (IC/builder, DRI/owner, player-coach) and eventually to a circle where AI is at the center and humans at the edge, providing judgment and alignment.

## Key Points

- Corporate hierarchy exists because information flow across many humans requires management structure. AI can now do that work — processing all company artifacts into a queryable intelligence.
- The traditional hierarchy borrowed and iterated over 2,000 years is now fundamentally questioned.
- Block's current max depth: CEO → anyone = 5 layers. Goal this year: 2-3. Ideal: everyone reports to CEO, enabled by the intelligence layer.
- Three roles: IC (builders/operators with AI agents = one person can do team-of-10 work); DRI (owns customer outcomes, assembles ICs, responsible for strategy); player-coach (builds human capability by demonstrating the work, not just managing it).
- CEO's future job: architecture of the company's intelligence, not just culture/hiring/execution. The CEO ensures the edge-of-humans alignment loop is working correctly.
- Customer outcomes become the road map: customers query the system for features that don't exist yet → if the system can compose them from existing capabilities, it does; if not, that points to a capability gap = the real road map.
- Roelof Botha's framing: this is like replacing command-and-control with a capitalist signal system — the right signals drive decisions instead of whoever pounds the table harder.
- Dorsey's key claim: AI is not a productivity tool, it's a structural change. Companies treating it as a copilot for individual productivity are missing the point.
- By January 2026, Dorsey had spent a year spending 3 hours every morning pushing AI tools to their limits, and "every single day it worked."

## Quotes

> "I don't think this is a productivity thing. I think it's a structural thing that needs to shift."

> "I think what these technologies point to are that our customers are going to have the expectation that they can ask for a feature that doesn't exist on our road map and that it just is served to them."

> "Is our company just going to be completely irrelevant in the next coming years?" — the existential dread that drove the restructuring.

## My Take

The "company as mini-AGI" framing is more than rhetorical — it reframes what corporate intelligence even is. The three-role model is interesting precisely because it eliminates the middle-management information relay function, which was always a lossy compression of what was actually happening. The most provocative claim is that customers will soon expect on-demand feature generation from their vendor's capabilities — which would make traditional product road maps obsolete. Pairs well with the Services article: both suggest the boundary between software and service is dissolving.
