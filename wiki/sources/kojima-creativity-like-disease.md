## Summary

A roundtable interview with Hideo Kojima following the release of *Death Stranding 2: On the Beach* (June 2025), exploring his creative process, the concept of the video game auteur, and the themes of connection running through his work. Kojima describes his compulsive imagination as "like a disease" — he is always generating ideas, even in conversations with family. The article also covers the trajectory of DS2's development through COVID-19, and upcoming projects (*OD* with Jordan Peele, *Physint*).

## Key Points

- Kojima is positioned as the first auteur of gaming — alongside Miyamoto, Yoko Taro, Suda51, and Miyazaki — whose personal philosophy permeates every work.
- *Metal Gear Solid 2* (2001) predicted the internet age, misinformation, AI risk, and post-truth politics — before social media or fake news were mainstream concepts.
- *Death Stranding* arrived months before COVID — its themes of isolation and reconnection became prescient by accident, not design.
- For DS2, Kojima scrapped his original outline after COVID and started over — reframing the question from "why connect?" to "should we have connected?"
- The core theme shift: DS1 = connection as necessity; DS2 = the dangers of over-connection. The logo change (strands hanging down vs. running into the logo) symbolizes this.
- He has left a USB stick with all his ideas for Kojima Productions to use after his death — a "will" for the studio.
- Current projects: *OD* (Xbox, cloud-based horror with Jordan Peele), *Physint* (Sony, stealth espionage), DS universe anime in production, A24 film.
- DS2 set in Australia; George Miller (*Mad Max*) makes a cameo.

## Quotes

> "It's almost like a disease. I'm imagining things all the time. Even when I'm talking to my family, in my head I'm in a totally different world."

> "I came back, and feel like I reconnected with myself through this project."

> "It had gotten to a point where I almost gave up."

## My Take

The "USB stick will" detail is fascinating — Kojima is building an institutional creativity infrastructure for a studio defined by one person's vision. That's a genuinely hard design problem: how do you encode auteur sensibility into something that survives the auteur?
