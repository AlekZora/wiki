## Summary

Pre-launch PlayStation Blog interview with Hidetaka Miyazaki shortly before *Elden Ring*'s February 2022 release. Covers the two major design challenges of making an open-world Souls game (maintaining freedom without losing pacing), how *Sekiro* influenced *Elden Ring*, the accessibility/difficulty discourse, the visual aesthetic shift to high fantasy, and Miyazaki's thoughts on cross-medium collaboration (inspired by the George R.R. Martin partnership).

## Notable Points

- **Two main design challenges**: (1) expanding player freedom in a vast open world while maintaining the Souls gameplay identity; (2) managing the tempo of exploration so players don't feel lost or unchallenged.
- **Sekiro influence**: enemy stance mechanics carried over; horse-mount traversal inspired by Sekiro's movement; more human drama in narrative (a step toward Sekiro's directness while maintaining Elden Ring's fragmented mythos).
- **Accessibility philosophy**: Elden Ring was not made easier — but more open. Players can leave a difficult area and return later. Clear rate expected to go up not from lower difficulty but from more options and player agency.
- **High fantasy aesthetic**: the Golden Age visual language (brightness, color) was intentional — to give a sense of lost greatness, and to avoid the oppressive unrelenting darkness of earlier titles.
- **Boss design**: Godrick the Grafted exemplifies the new approach — taking George R.R. Martin's heroic/grandiose character concepts and twisting them through madness and desperation.
- **Cross-medium collaboration**: the appeal of working with Martin was that books and games are "vastly different forms of media." Would choose any collaborator from outside game development — books, art, music — to provide stimulation impossible to achieve from within.
- **On spoilers**: wants players to go in fresh, without guides, to feel the adventure and sense of wonder.
- **Game he finished**: *It Takes Two* — played start to finish with a friend in three sittings. Admired for never letting him get bored; resonated personally as a parent.

## Lore / World Details

Elden Ring's world (The Lands Between) was co-created with George R.R. Martin, who provided the mythos and character histories. The "Shattering" and demigod politics are Martin's creation; Miyazaki's team built the gameplay world around it. Confirmed: New Game Plus and multiple endings included.

## My Take

Miyazaki's answer about cross-medium collaboration is a strong design principle: seek the stimulus you *can't* generate from inside your own medium. Games made purely by game developers tend to feel like games; the MGS2 and Elden Ring weirdnesses came from reaching outside the form.
