## Short Answer

The outsider advantage is not ignorance. It is **depth somewhere else, applied to a domain
wicked enough that its experts' pattern-matching has become a liability.** The vault supports
this from two directions and constrains it from two more — the constraint being that insight
requires a substrate to recombine, so the person who invents in an unfamiliar field is never a
beginner in general, only a beginner *there*.

Three filter questions, all answerable from notes already here: **is the domain wicked or
kind; do I have real depth somewhere that could transfer; and is this a tar pit?**

## The case for it

`power-laws.md` has the sharpest statement, and it names this exact strategy as current:

> "In a power law domain, incremental gains from experts produce normal-distribution returns;
> radical breakthroughs from outsiders produce the grand slams. Khosla's principle — 'I don't
> want a healthcare CEO for a healthcare company' — is a direct application: domain experts
> optimize within the distribution, outsiders can shift it. **AI-enabled outsiders (people who
> understand AI but enter a new domain without prior assumptions) are the current instantiation
> of this strategy.**"
> — [power-laws.md:30](../concepts/power-laws.md)

`range-epstein.md` supplies the mechanism, and it is three distinct things rather than one:

> "**The outsider advantage**: Solutions to hard problems often come from people outside the
> domain. They don't know the 'right' way to try, so they try things the experts have
> dismissed."
> — [range-epstein.md:20](../sources/range-epstein.md)

> "**Analogical thinking**: The most creative thinkers make deep analogies across domains.
> Narrow specialists can't see the analogy because they only know their domain."
> — [range-epstein.md:21](../sources/range-epstein.md)

> "**Late bloomers**: A tech founder at 50 is nearly twice as likely to start a breakout company
> as one at 30. Experience diversification is not a disadvantage — it builds pattern recognition
> that early specialists lack."
> — [range-epstein.md:23](../sources/range-epstein.md)

`wicked-vs-kind-learning-environments.md` explains why the expert is handicapped, and this is
the load-bearing part of the whole answer:

> "Experts in wicked domains who specialized early often suffer from the **Einstellung effect**:
> their existing pattern recognition blinds them to non-standard solutions. They literally can't
> see what a generalist would notice."
> — [wicked-vs-kind-learning-environments.md:25](../concepts/wicked-vs-kind-learning-environments.md)

**The qualifier that makes this usable:** the advantage only exists in *wicked* environments —
delayed, misleading, or non-repeating feedback. In kind environments (clear rules, immediate
feedback, stable patterns) the specialist wins and the outsider simply loses. Epstein's
Tiger/Roger split is exactly this: "In kind environments, Tiger wins. In wicked environments,
Roger wins more often" ([range-epstein.md:17](../sources/range-epstein.md)).

`research-craft.md` contains the trainable version — curating an information diet
that "includes old/underpriced material and cross-domain range"
([:17](../concepts/research-craft.md)) — i.e. breadth as a deliberate practice rather than a
biographical accident.

## The counterweight — the vault argues against itself here, and the objection is good

> "Insight likely requires a sufficient 'substrate' of prior experience: **you can't have an
> insight into a domain you know nothing about.** It's the recombination of existing knowledge
> into a new configuration — which connects it directly to creativity and analogy-making."
> — [insight-learning.md:20](../concepts/insight-learning.md)

This is the single most important line against a naive reading. Recombination needs something
to recombine.

`startup-idea-evaluation.md` names the outsider's specific failure mode. A **tar pit idea**
looks tractable and has a hidden structural reason nobody has solved it. The outsider is
maximally exposed, because "they don't know the right way to try" and "they don't know why the
previous twenty attempts failed" are the same sentence. That note also makes **founder-market
fit** "arguably the most important variable," which cuts directly against the outsider frame.

And `first-principles-thinking.md` — the obvious method for entering a field fresh — undercuts
itself:

> "**Where first principles thinking fails**: It's expensive to apply (requires deep domain
> knowledge to know what the actual physical constraints are)"
> — [first-principles-thinking.md](../concepts/archive/first-principles-thinking.md)

## Synthesis

Put together, the vault's position is narrower and more useful than "outsiders win":

1. **Wicked domain, or don't bother.** Kind domains reward accumulated reps; the outsider has
   none and gains nothing from lacking them.
2. **Substrate required.** The invention is a transfer, so there must be a source. "Outsider"
   means *specialist elsewhere*, not *novice generally*.
3. **The ignorance is only useful about method, not about history.** Not knowing the accepted
   technique is the asset. Not knowing that four teams already died on this exact rock is the
   tar pit.
4. **Analogy is the actual mechanism.** Not fresh eyes, not naivety — a structural
   correspondence visible only to someone who knows both sides.

## Two of the open questions, answered (2026-09-21)

Answered from vault material only, same method as the rest of this file: candidates derived
from `wiki/concepts/` and `wiki/sources/`, every claim carrying a quoted line. Where a
connection is drawn here that neither source states, it is labelled as derived.

Both questions gate the [outsider-advantage](../concepts/outsider-advantage.md) concept — the
first is its environment test, the third is its prerequisite test. **They turn out to have the
same practical resolution**, which is the most useful single result below.

## Q1 — Is there a test for wicked vs. kind that can be run *before* entering a domain?

**Short answer: yes, a partial one, and it is cheap. Two of the three defining properties are
inspectable from outside; the third is retrospective by construction. The correct response to
the residual is not a better test but a cheaper exit.**

### 1. The definition is already a checklist of inspectable properties

[wicked-vs-kind-learning-environments.md:20](../concepts/wicked-vs-kind-learning-environments.md)
defines wicked feedback by three properties, and they are not equally hidden:

> "**Delayed**: You don't know if the diagnosis was right until months later. **Misleading**:
> The patient who recovered might have recovered anyway. **Non-repeating**: The next
> negotiation isn't the same negotiation as the last one."

- **Delay is observable without entering.** It is a measurable lag between action and verdict,
  readable off other people's attempts.
- **Repetition is observable without entering.** Does the same situation recur in recognisable
  form, or is every instance bespoke?
- **Misleading is not.** Establishing that feedback misleads requires the counterfactual — the
  patient who "might have recovered anyway" — and a counterfactual is exactly what an outside
  observer does not have. This is the property that can only be established after the fact.

So the question's pessimistic half is correct, but narrowly: *one of three*, not the whole
test. And the ex-ante classification is not exotic — Hogarth and Epstein classify medicine,
business and law from outside, without practising any of them.

### 2. The strongest probe in the vault: forecast-and-check, run from outside

[research-craft.md:16](../concepts/research-craft.md) treats taste as trainable through
"repeated forecast-and-check cycles," and the source states the procedure plainly:

> "Taste is trainable by forecasting experiment outcomes before running them and checking the
> hit rate."
> — [how-to-be-good-at-research-vivek.md:21](../sources/how-to-be-good-at-research-vivek.md)

**Derived here:** nothing in that practice requires being inside the domain. Predict the
outcomes of *other people's* attempts — which launches find users, which papers replicate,
which titles sell — record the predictions, score them. The experiment still runs; it is
simply run by somebody else, and the world resolves it in public. The only hard requirement is
ordering: the prediction must be recorded before the outcome is visible to you. It costs no
entry, and it measures precisely what the wicked/kind distinction is about: whether the
environment permits a predictive model to be built at all. A domain where a motivated outsider
can reach decent calibration in fifty observations is behaving kindly. One where the hit rate
stays at chance is behaving wickedly, or is beyond your substrate — see the confound below.

**But the probe is not free, and its price is the property it measures.** *(Added 2026-09-21.)*
Delay is one of the three defining marks of a wicked environment (§1 above), so scoring fifty
*live* forecasts means waiting out fifty resolution cycles of exactly the domain you suspect is
slow. Drug efficacy resolves in years; paper replication in years; an indie game launch in
weeks. The paragraph above originally called the probe free because it costs no entry — it
costs no entry and is priced in wall-clock time, in direct proportion to the wickedness being
measured. That makes it the wrong instrument to reach for first, which is corrected in §6.

The wait is not wasted, because **how long you must wait to score anything is itself the delay
measurement.** The first of the three properties is readable before a single forecast resolves.

**The fast variant: backtest on the resolved record.** Take cases that are already settled but
that you do not personally know the outcome of, forecast them blind, then reveal. Fifty
observations in an afternoon instead of five years. Two contaminations, both specific:

- **Leakage.** You cannot unknow the famous cases, so the sample has to be drawn deliberately
  from obscure ones — harder to construct, and easy to cheat at without noticing.
- **Survivorship in the record.** The failures were never written down. The sample skews toward
  what got documented, which correlates with what worked, so the resulting hit rate flatters
  you for reasons unrelated to your calibration. This is the same sampling problem
  [outsider-advantage.md:88](../concepts/outsider-advantage.md) names about its own sources.

The live test has neither contamination and is slow. The backtest has both and is fast. They
are complementary rather than substitutes: backtest for a reading this week, then run live
forecasts in the background to check whether that reading survives a clean sample.

[research-craft.md:34](../concepts/research-craft.md) also supplies the quantity worth
measuring, which is better than the category:

> "research speed is mostly the speed at which you discover you're wrong … A researcher who
> can be wrong and *know it* in an hour is structurally faster than one who is wrong for a
> month before finding out."

**Latency to falsification is wickedness stated as an engineering number.** It is
domain-specific, ex-ante observable, and continuous rather than binary — which is a better
instrument than the two-bucket label, because the interesting question is never "is this
wicked" but "how long until this tells me I am wrong."

### 3. The confound, and how to break it

A low forecasting hit rate has two possible causes — the domain is wicked, or you lack the
substrate to forecast in it (Q3). From your own score alone they are indistinguishable.

The disambiguator is the domain's existing practitioners.
[recognition-primed-decision-making.md:51](../concepts/recognition-primed-decision-making.md)
states the dependency: "RPD works best in kind environments where prototypes generalize;
wicked environments are where RPD can lead experts astray." Expert intuition is therefore a
*reagent* for wickedness:

- **Insiders forecast well and you don't** → the environment is kind enough to reward
  accumulated reps, and the outsider bet is off regardless of which cause it is.
- **Nobody forecasts well, and senior practitioners disagree with each other** → wicked. The
  reps exist and have not converged.

That comparison is readable from the public record — published predictions, track records,
whether experienced people in the field agree about what will work. One caution, load-bearing:
score *predictions*, never *confidence*. [illusory-insight.md:12](../concepts/illusory-insight.md)
found that "those who reported the strongest Aha rated statements highest in truth," so
practitioners' felt certainty is evidence of nothing. RPD's own open question is the same
worry from the inside: "In wicked learning environments (where experience gives false
prototypes), how do you distinguish good expert intuition from learned miscalibration?"
([:59](../concepts/recognition-primed-decision-making.md)) — this answer says you distinguish
it by scoring it, which is the one method that does not route through the expert's self-report.

### 4. Three cheaper proxies, all observable before entry

- **Outcome distribution.** [power-laws.md:20](../concepts/power-laws.md): "in a normal
  distribution domain, consistency wins; in a power law domain, persistence wins. Restaurants
  need to fill tables every night — consistency. Venture capital firms need one outlier to
  cover all losses." **Derived here:** a power-law payoff structure *is* misleading feedback in
  Hogarth's sense, because per-attempt outcomes are dominated by variance rather than by
  quality, so each attempt teaches you almost nothing. Neither page makes this link —
  power-laws never mentions feedback quality and wicked-vs-kind never mentions distributions —
  but the distribution shape is public before entry, which makes it the cheapest available
  proxy for the property that resisted direct inspection.
- **Base rate of the idea space.** [startup-idea-evaluation.md](../concepts/archive/startup-idea-evaluation.md)
  makes "quality of the idea space" a screening question outright: "different spaces have
  wildly different base rates … fintech infra and vertical SaaS had high hit rates
  historically; consumer hardware and ad tech did not." A domain whose base rate is knowable at
  all is more kind than one where nobody can state it.
- **Metric divergence.** [metrics-trap.md](../concepts/metrics-trap.md) describes what wicked
  feedback looks like in practice: optimization improves the proxy while degrading the thing.
  If a domain has a headline number its own practitioners argue about, the available feedback
  is proxy feedback. wicked-vs-kind reaches the same conclusion from the other side —
  "Products die from getting the wicked signals wrong"
  ([:29](../concepts/wicked-vs-kind-learning-environments.md)).

And the one procedure the vault already prescribes is itself a pre-entry test: the tar-pit
check — "Google it, find prior attempts, talk to those founders"
([startup-idea-evaluation.md:21](../concepts/archive/startup-idea-evaluation.md)). That is
reading someone else's feedback record instead of generating your own, and
[outsider-advantage.md](../concepts/outsider-advantage.md) already notes it has gone from a
research project to an afternoon now that retrieval is cheap.

### 5. What to do about the irreducible residual

Since the misleading property survives every ex-ante test, the answer is to stop treating
wickedness as a gate that must be cleared before committing.
[failure-cost-asymmetry.md:13](../concepts/failure-cost-asymmetry.md) is the argument:
willingness to run risky bets "is governed primarily by how expensive it is to *discontinue* a
bet that doesn't work out — not by how expensive it is to *start* one," and kill criteria are
the deliberate fix, pre-authorising "the discontinuation so it doesn't require a fresh, costly
negotiation when the bad outcome actually arrives" ([:40](../concepts/failure-cost-asymmetry.md)).

Range says the same thing about wicked domains specifically: "Grit is valuable in kind
environments. In wicked environments, knowing when to quit and pivot is the skill. Sunk-cost
fallacy masquerading as grit is deadly" ([range-epstein.md:24](../sources/range-epstein.md)).
And [vertical-slice.md:17](../concepts/vertical-slice.md) gives the shape of the bounded entry
— "Only a finished-quality fragment can" answer whether the thing works, and it "forces
honesty about scope" ([:21](../concepts/vertical-slice.md)).

~~**Sequence that follows:** forecast probe from outside (free) → tar-pit retrieval (an
afternoon) → enter with kill criteria written down first.~~ **Revised 2026-09-21 — see §6.**
The forecast probe was put first on the belief that it was free; it is in fact the slowest
instrument on this list. Corrected order:

**the instrument question (today, §6) → tar-pit retrieval (an afternoon) → backtested
forecasts (an afternoon, §2) → enter with kill criteria written down first → live forecasts
accumulating in the background throughout**, with §7's re-measurement running alongside as the
one activity that manufactures an edge rather than only pricing one.

The fourth step is what substitutes for the certainty the category cannot supply. The fifth is
the only one that ever produces a clean number, and it is deliberately no longer a gate.

### 6. The first-try cases, and what they actually show

*(Added 2026-09-21, prompted by the obvious objection: has nobody ever entered a field cold and
been right immediately?)*

**Provenance warning.** The four cases below — Alvarez, Spencer, the Wright brothers, Barone —
are **not in any ingested source in this vault.** They are brought in from outside to test a
claim the vault's own material makes, and they have not been checked against a primary source
here. Everything in the rest of this file is traceable to a quoted vault line; this section is
not. Treat the *structures* as the finding and the cases as illustrations that still want
verification.

They have, and §2 should not be read as denying it. **Luis and Walter Alvarez, 1980** — a
physicist and a geologist proposed that an asteroid impact ended the Cretaceous. Neither was a
paleontologist, neither had a prior attempt in the field, and they were right; paleontology
resisted for roughly a decade until the Chicxulub crater was identified. The existence claim is
settled. What is useful is that the strong cases sort into two structures, plus a residue.

**Imported instrument.** The Alvarezes did not out-reason paleontologists. They carried a
*measurement* — the iridium anomaly in the boundary clay — from a domain where trace-element
analysis was routine into one where nobody was looking for it. Percy Spencer is the same shape:
outsider to cooking appliances, insider to magnetrons, which is why a melted chocolate bar in
his pocket meant something to him and nothing to anyone else standing in that room. In both
cases the first try is first-try *in the target domain* and roughly the thousandth in the
source domain. The forecast-and-check happened — elsewhere, earlier, on other problems. This is
[outsider-advantage.md:17](../concepts/outsider-advantage.md)'s definition cashed out, with one
addition: what transfers is frequently a specific **instrument**, not a general perspective.
That is a narrower and more checkable claim than "depth somewhere else."

**Compressed private loop.** The Wright brothers had no aeronautics training and beat Langley,
who had government money and the Smithsonian behind him. They also built a wind tunnel, tested
wing shapes in batches, and ran hundreds of glides before the flight anyone remembers. Eric
Barone had no game-industry experience and *Stardew Valley* was his first commercial title —
after roughly four years alone, rebuilding the art several times, posting builds for feedback
the entire way. Both read as one-shot outsider wins because there was one *public* resolution.
Internally they were running the densest feedback loop in their field. These are not
counterexamples to forecast-and-check; they are its strongest instances.

**Amendment — the two structures have had opposite fates, and only one is still an edge.**
*(Added 2026-09-21, second amendment.)* The section above describes both as live strategies.
That is too generous to the second one. **The compressed private loop has been largely competed
away.** Its advantage in 1902 came from a cost structure that no longer exists: the Wrights
could iterate cheaply and almost nobody else was doing it, because building and testing was
expensive for everyone. Iteration is now cheap for every participant in nearly every field.
Shipping fast and testing constantly was a structural edge then and is table stakes now —
[leverage.md:72](../concepts/leverage.md) states the general form, that AI "commoditises the
production of leverage artifacts," so "the binding constraint migrates from production to taste"
([:76](../concepts/leverage.md)). A private loop is still *necessary*; it is no longer
*differentiating*, and this answer should not sell it as the latter.

**The imported instrument did not take that hit — it got more valuable.** Its advantage never
rested on iteration being expensive for others. It rested on fields converging on shared inputs
that nobody re-derives, and the supply of those has grown enormously while the incentive to
check any particular one has not. When iteration is free, knowing *what to measure* becomes the
whole bottleneck, which is the same migration leverage.md describes arriving at the same place
from the other side. §7 specifies the method that follows from this.

**And a residue.** Some people are right once with no mechanism at all. That bucket is real and
this answer should not define it away.

**Why all three together still license no decision.** A single confirmed first-try success is
compatible with a kind domain *and* with a wicked domain carrying a fat tail — one draw from a
high-variance distribution does not separate them. §4's power-law point is the reason: when
per-attempt outcomes are dominated by variance rather than quality, single successes are the
observations that teach least. The selection effect at
[outsider-advantage.md:88](../concepts/outsider-advantage.md) is the same caution from the
other side — we hear about the geneticist who reinvented food and not about the people who
entered cold and produced nothing.

**What the two structures do buy is that they are checkable today, with no waiting.** This is
the real correction to the sequence in §5:

1. **Do I hold an instrument from my source domain that the target domain lacks?** Not "do I
   have transferable depth" in the abstract — a specific tool, measurement, or technique that
   is routine where I come from and absent where I am going. If the honest answer is no, and
   what I actually have is method-ignorance,
   [outsider-advantage.md:42](../concepts/outsider-advantage.md) has already ruled on it:
   arriving "with no method-priors and nothing to transfer … is not the outsider advantage. It
   is just being new."
2. **Can I run the loop privately and cheaply before anything resolves publicly?** If yes, the
   domain's slow public clock stops being the binding constraint, because the iterations happen
   in my own workshop rather than in the market's resolution cycle. The wind tunnel and
   [vertical-slice.md](../concepts/vertical-slice.md) are the same move described twice.

Both are answerable in an afternoon, with no forecasting record at all, which is why they now
precede the probe rather than follow it. Note what question 2 implies about a slow domain: a
private loop does not make the domain kind, it makes *your own* latency to falsification short
while the domain's stays long — which is exactly the quantity §2 identified as the one worth
measuring.

Per the amendment above, the two questions are **not** equally weighted. Question 2 is a
qualifying condition — a no is disqualifying, a yes buys nothing against competitors who can
all answer yes. Question 1 is the one that discriminates. Read question 2 as a floor and
question 1 as the actual test.

### 7. The one method here that is not time-limited

*(Added 2026-09-21, answering directly: which of this is still valid today, and likely to stay
valid?)*

**The method: re-measure the field's unchecked shared inputs.** Not "distrust the literature" —
that is the 1901 version and it is actively value-destroying now, when most published data is
abundant and correct. The specific move is narrower: find the inputs a field takes from a
common source *without re-deriving them*, audit their provenance, and re-measure the ones whose
provenance is weaker than their usage.

**Why it should outlast the era, which is the part that matters.** Every method eventually dies
with whatever it was standing on, so the only question worth asking about a historical practice
is what its substrate is and whether that substrate is still here. The compressed private loop
was standing on the 1901 cost of iteration, which is gone. This one stands on something that is
not a defect of any period: **fields economise by converging on shared inputs, and convergence
is what makes specialisation efficient in the first place.** Nobody will fix it, because it is
not broken — it is the mechanism. And the same property that makes it efficient guarantees a
standing inventory of numbers that were measured once, under conditions that have since moved,
and inherited unexamined ever since. That is a structural feature of division of labour, not of
aeronautics in 1901, which is why it survived from Lilienthal's coefficient tables to now and
should be expected to keep surviving.

**Procedure.**

1. List the field's load-bearing shared inputs — the numbers, base rates, and "everyone knows"
   facts that every practitioner takes from the same place without re-deriving.
2. For each, establish provenance: measured by whom, when, on what sample, under what
   conditions, and how many times since.
3. Flag the ones where provenance is thin relative to load — old, small-sample, self-reported,
   never replicated, or taken under conditions that have since changed.
4. Re-measure the cheapest flagged input.
5. If it holds, you have spent an afternoon and bought calibration. If it does not, **you now
   hold an instrument the field lacks.**

**Step 5 is the reason this belongs here rather than in a general methods note.** It is not a
third structure alongside §6's two — it is the *generator* of the first one. The imported
instrument is normally treated as inherited biography: you either happen to carry the right
depth from elsewhere or you don't, which makes it unactionable for anyone who doesn't. This
manufactures one instead, and it does so from the target domain rather than from a source
domain, which removes the requirement to have already spent a decade somewhere else.

**Failure modes, stated so this doesn't read as permission.**

- Re-measuring well-attested, frequently-replicated inputs is pure loss. The screen is
  *provenance quality*, not suspicion, and the difference between those two is the difference
  between this method and crankery.
- It requires enough substrate to measure competently, which lands back on Q3 — you can
  re-measure only what you are equipped to measure.
- It tests nothing about wickedness. This is instrument acquisition, not domain assessment, and
  it does not substitute for §1–§5.

**Distinguish from [metrics-trap.md](../concepts/metrics-trap.md)**, which is adjacent and not
the same. Goodhart describes a measure decoupling from the thing *because* it is optimised
against. This describes an input drifting from reality because nobody re-derives it. One is
corruption under pressure, the other is decay under neglect — but they compound, since a stale
input that everyone optimises against is both at once, and a field's headline benchmark is
usually exactly that.

**Where the unchecked inputs currently sit, including in AI.** They left the published physical
constants, which is why the 1901 reading misfires, and moved to softer places: what users
actually do versus what a field believes they do; base rates everyone quotes and nobody sourced;
any figure that is really a survey, a self-report, or a convention set once and never revisited.
Machine learning is a dense instance rather than an exception — a field whose shared inputs are
benchmarks, and where the provenance questions (what is in the test set, has it leaked into
training, does this score predict deployed behaviour) are asked far less often than the scores
are cited. That is step 3 of the procedure describing an entire field's situation. The AI lens
also cuts the other way here, and less comfortably: a model is trained on the field's consensus
and will reproduce its unchecked inputs fluently, which makes it an excellent tool for step 1
— listing what everyone believes — and a poor one for step 3, since the staleness is precisely
what the corpus cannot see. This is
[outsider-advantage.md:114](../concepts/outsider-advantage.md)'s Einstellung point arriving in
a new place: the model inherits the convergence along with the knowledge.

## Q3 — How much substrate is enough?

**Short answer: the question is on the wrong axis. There is no threshold in the vault and
there is unlikely to be one — sufficiency is three separate requirements with three different
answers, marked qualitatively rather than counted, and otherwise established by a bounded
attempt rather than estimated before it.**

### 1. The missing threshold is a pattern, not a gap

The same "minimum viable X" question recurs across the vault, unanswered every time and always
about a quantity nobody operationalizes:

| Page | The unanswered threshold |
|---|---|
| [insight-learning.md:20](../concepts/insight-learning.md) | "a sufficient 'substrate' of prior experience" — no amount given |
| [wicked-vs-kind:40](../concepts/wicked-vs-kind-learning-environments.md) | "minimum viable sampling period before specialization is beneficial" |
| [recognition-primed-decision-making:60](../concepts/recognition-primed-decision-making.md) | "minimum prototype density before the cascade-of-caveats verbal pattern emerges" |
| [learning-from-demonstration:39](../concepts/learning-from-demonstration.md) | "actual minimum viable demonstration count for a new task" |
| [closed-loop-systems:29](../concepts/closed-loop-systems.md) | "minimum artifact richness required to make a feedback loop useful vs. noisy" |
| [comprehension-floor:147](../concepts/comprehension-floor.md) | "What is the passing bar?" |
| [swarm-intelligence:62](../concepts/swarm-intelligence.md) | "minimum viable swarm for useful emergence" |
| [ideal-reader:23](../concepts/ideal-reader.md) | "minimum viable Ideal Reader" |

Eight independent instances is a finding rather than eight oversights: **the vault reliably
asks for a number where the underlying thing has no natural unit.** In every case where the
page got somewhere useful anyway, it did so by finding a *marker* — an observable that appears
once the quantity is sufficient — instead of a count. That is the move available here too.

### 2. Decompose it: three substrates, three different answers

[outsider-advantage.md:33](../concepts/outsider-advantage.md) already separates the three
mechanisms by prerequisite. Run the substrate question through that split and it stops being
one question:

1. **Depth in the source domain** — the one mechanism 2 (analogical transfer) runs on, and the
   one that produces every famous case. Requirement: **a lot, and it must already exist.** It
   cannot be acquired on the way in.
2. **History of the target domain** — why the last four attempts failed, which constraints are
   real. Requirement: **a little, and it is now cheap.** outsider-advantage's AI point is that
   "ignorance of history … is a retrieval problem, and retrieval got cheap"
   ([:119](../concepts/outsider-advantage.md)).
3. **Method of the target domain** — the accepted technique. Requirement: **deliberately
   none.** This is the asset, and the only part where more knowledge makes the bet worse.

The threshold felt unfindable because the three were being summed into one quantity called
"how much do I need to know." The honest answer is "a great deal of the first, an afternoon of
the second, and none of the third."

### 3. The marker for the first: prototypes, not rules

[recognition-primed-decision-making.md:35](../concepts/recognition-primed-decision-making.md)
reframes expertise away from volume: "not knowing more rules, but having more prototypes. Each
prototype is a compressed, experiential pattern." And it names an observable trace
([:37](../concepts/recognition-primed-decision-making.md)):

> "The 'cascade of caveats' pattern (expert explains their judgment as 'do X, except when Y,
> but also if A and not B…') is the verbal trace of a prototype that was encoded from
> experience, not a rule list."

**Self-administrable test, derived here:** describe how to do the hard thing in your source
domain. If it comes out as clean transferable rules, that is book knowledge — a rule list, and
there is nothing in it to recombine. If it comes out as a cascade of caveats, you have
prototypes, and prototypes are what the analogy is made of. Sufficiency is a **qualitative
marker, measured in the source domain**, which is also the part everyone forgets: the substrate
question is never asked about the field you are entering.

### 4. The only quantitative anchor in the vault, and what it shows

[learning-from-demonstration.md:39](../concepts/learning-from-demonstration.md) is the one
place a number appears: "Fifty demonstrations reportedly gets 'maybe half' success on a simple
pick-and-place task — where does that curve bend, and does it bend the same way across task
types?" Three things follow, and the page draws the link to Köhler's substrate itself
([:20](../concepts/learning-from-demonstration.md)):

- **Sufficiency is a curve, not a line.** There is no threshold to find — only a bend whose
  location is domain-specific and, in the vault's only measured case, unknown. Fifty careful
  demonstrations of an *easy* task buys a coin flip.
- **The same decomposition appears in a system where the quantities are measurable.** VLA
  models "pretrain broad, generalizable 'physical interaction' competence across many robots
  and tasks, then fine-tune cheaply on one specific embodiment with a small number of new
  demonstrations" ([:25](../concepts/learning-from-demonstration.md)). Large transferable base
  plus small local sample — the outsider structure, working, with numbers attached.
- **Composition beats volume.** "A policy trained on sloppy demonstrations is a sloppy policy,"
  and the fix "isn't more data indiscriminately, it's targeted iteration — record the failure
  cases specifically, retrain, remeasure" ([:20](../concepts/learning-from-demonstration.md)).
  The substrate that transfers is the one containing the failures, which is exactly the part of
  a domain's history that is not written down.

### 5. The concrete sufficiency test for the target domain

[outsider-advantage.md:91](../concepts/outsider-advantage.md) names the failure mode precisely
— the people who entered a field cold and produced nothing "misread which of its constraints
were organisational and which were physical." [first-principles-thinking.md](../concepts/archive/first-principles-thinking.md)
states the same requirement as a cost: the method "requires deep domain knowledge to know what
the actual physical constraints are."

Inverted, that is a checkable test, and it is the sharpest one here: **name the domain's
binding constraints, sort them into physical and organisational, then check the split against
the record of prior attempts.** Failure to produce the list means the history substrate is
missing. A split that turns out wrong means it was the wrong substrate. It is cheap, because it
is the same retrieval as the tar-pit check — one pass, not a career.

### 6. Why self-assessment cannot settle it

Two structural results say the person holding the substrate is the wrong instrument for
measuring it:

- [comprehension-floor.md:18](../concepts/comprehension-floor.md): the floor "is a property of
  the artifact rather than of the audience, and it is systematically invisible to the
  artifact's author, because the author cannot un-know the thing being tested" — it "can only
  be measured from outside" ([:117](../concepts/comprehension-floor.md)). Substrate sufficiency
  is the mirror image, invisible from the inside for the complementary reason: you cannot see
  the shape of what you do not know. **Its prescription transfers directly — ask what, not
  whether** ([:54](../concepts/comprehension-floor.md)). "Do I know enough about game
  distribution?" measures confidence. "What is the binding constraint, and what killed the last
  four attempts?" measures substrate.
- [illusory-insight.md:12](../concepts/illusory-insight.md): the Aha is "decoupled from
  actually having understood it." Feeling ready is not evidence of being ready, and the feeling
  gets *stronger* with the misattribution. The machine version is already an open question in
  [learning-from-demonstration.md:40](../concepts/learning-from-demonstration.md) — a policy
  "that scores well on its training demonstrations but has memorized surface correlations …
  rather than the underlying skill." Substrate that passes its own inspection and fails on
  contact is a documented failure mode on both sides of the analogy.

### 7. The dissolving move

[whole-game-learning.md:16](../concepts/whole-game-learning.md) argues against the prerequisite
chain that this question assumes: conventional sequencing, "fundamentals first, complexity
withheld until readiness is demonstrated — seems safe but routinely produces learners who can
pass tests but can't generate anything new," against fast.ai's "working deep learning model in
30 minutes, then years of unpacking how it works." With the caveat kept:
"throwing people at the whole game without any support produces frustration, not insight"
([:18](../concepts/whole-game-learning.md)).

So the threshold does not need answering in advance. Run the smallest complete attempt that
produces a real verdict, with the kill criteria from Q1 §5 set beforehand, and let the attempt
measure the substrate. That is the same resolution Q1 arrived at from the other direction.

## What the two answers share

The forecast probe measures both properties at once and **cannot separate them** — a bad hit
rate means the domain is wicked, or that you lack substrate, and only the comparison against
insiders tells you which. That confound is not a defect of the probe; it is the actual
structure of the situation, and it explains why both questions have resisted a clean answer.

Both also terminate in the same place: bounded entry with a pre-committed exit. Which sharpens
the concept's gate. It is **not** "determine wickedness and substrate before entering" — that
demands two measurements the vault says cannot be taken from outside. It is **make entry cheap
enough to survive being wrong about both**, and spend the pre-entry budget on the things that
*are* cheap: ~~scoring your own forecasts, and reading the graveyard.~~ **Revised 2026-09-21 —**
the two structural questions from §6 (do I carry an instrument, can I loop privately), reading
the graveyard, and *backtested* forecasts. Scoring forecasts live is the one pre-entry activity
that is not cheap — it costs a full resolution cycle of the domain, which is the property under
test — so it belongs alongside the entry rather than in front of it.

**What was not found.** No vault material quantifies either threshold, and none is likely to —
see §1 of Q3. The forecast-probe design is derived here and has never been run. The base rate
of outsider entry remains unknown, as [outsider-advantage.md:88](../concepts/outsider-advantage.md)
already notes, so none of this licenses a decision on its own; it prices one.
