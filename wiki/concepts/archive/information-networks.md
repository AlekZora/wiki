## Definition

Human civilization as a stack of information networks—systems for creating, transmitting, storing, and acting on information at scale. From oral traditions to writing to printing to the internet to AI, each new information technology reshapes how humans cooperate, what fictions they can sustain, and what power structures are possible.

## How I Think About It

Harari's Nexus frames three views of information:

**Naive view**: More information → more truth → better outcomes. Technology optimists, Google's mission statement, Zuckerberg's "openness" framing. Partially right (child mortality fell as medical information spread) but misses how networks can coordinate around delusion as effectively as truth.

**Populist view**: Information is a weapon. All truth claims are power plays. Foucault, Marx, Trump, QAnon. Corrodes the trust infrastructure that makes self-correcting networks possible.

**Harari's third view**: Information networks are cooperative structures that can run on truth or on useful fiction. What determines which they run on is whether they have self-correction mechanisms. Science has peer review. Democracy has elections. When those mechanisms are corrupted, the network optimizes for power instead of truth.

**AI as agent not tool**: The key novelty of AI in this framework—all previous information technologies required humans to decide what to do with the information. AI can autonomously process information and make decisions. This changes the information network's dynamics fundamentally.

**For product building**: Every product is an information network—between users, between users and the company, between the company and the world. The architecture of that network determines what can be known and acted on. Choosing which information flows where is a political act.

**Self-correcting mechanisms in AI**: Current AI systems don't have the equivalent of peer review. A network of AI agents without error-correction mechanisms will amplify whatever patterns dominate the training data, including errors and biases.
