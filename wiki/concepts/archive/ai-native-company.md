## Definition

A company designed from the ground up with AI as the operating system — not a productivity tool layered onto existing workflows. Every workflow, decision, and process flows through an intelligent layer that continuously learns and improves. The org is fully queryable by AI: every important action produces a legible artifact, meetings are recorded, agents are embedded in communication channels, and dashboards cover revenue, sales, engineering, hiring, and ops. There is no human middleware; information routing is the AI's job.

Three employee archetypes replace the classic management hierarchy: (1) the IC/builder-operator — builds and runs things directly, brings prototypes not decks; (2) the DRI (directly responsible individual) — one person, one outcome, no diffusion of accountability; (3) the AI founder — still builds hands-on, leads by example, does not delegate AI strategy. Token usage replaces headcount as the key resource metric: a high API bill is fine if it replaces what would have cost more in salaries.

## How I Think About It

The mental model is: if a new employee could understand everything about your company by querying a system, you're AI-native. If they have to ask a human, you're not. Every manager, every DM, every undocumented process is a leak in the system. The shift is from "AI helps my people" to "the company IS the AI loop."

Startups have a structural advantage here — no legacy org chart to unwind, no live product to maintain while rewiring the plumbing.

## Open Questions

- What breaks in the "no human middleware" model when something goes genuinely wrong and needs judgment no artifact captures?
- Is the three-archetype model stable at scale, or does it converge back toward hierarchy as headcount grows?
- How do you hire for the DRI / builder-operator archetype when most candidates have only worked in traditional orgs?
