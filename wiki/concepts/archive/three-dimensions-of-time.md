## Definition

A theoretical framework (Warsaw/Oxford, 2022 paper in *Classical and Quantum Gravity*) proposing that for faster-than-light (FTL) observers, the roles of space and time in special relativity are inverted: instead of 3 spatial + 1 temporal dimension, such observers would experience 1 spatial + 3 temporal dimensions. This means particles would propagate along multiple temporal paths simultaneously — naturally producing quantum mechanical behavior like wave-particle duality and entanglement.

## How I Think About It

Special relativity already mathematically permits FTL objects (tachyons) — they just can't decelerate to sub-light speeds, the mirror of how normal matter can't accelerate to light speed. The "flip" of space and time dimensions under FTL conditions follows from the math of Minkowski spacetime. What's new here is using that flip as an explanatory lens for QM: if the universe "looks like" 3T+1S to some reference frame, then the multi-path, probabilistic nature of quantum mechanics has a geometric origin rather than being an unexplained postulate. It's a creative unification attempt — deriving QM weirdness from relativistic geometry rather than treating them as separate frameworks.
