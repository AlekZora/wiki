## Definition

A set of frameworks and heuristics for identifying whether a startup idea is worth pursuing, and for generating good ideas in the first place. Primarily developed from YC's patterns across thousands of applications and companies.

## How I Think About It

The core reframe is: don't search for "a good startup idea" in the abstract — search for "a good startup idea for your specific team." Founder-market fit (whether your team is especially well-suited to this space) is arguably the most important variable, and it also acts as a filter to prune the idea search space.

The key failure modes form a useful checklist to run any idea through:

- **SISP** (solution in search of a problem): started from a technology and worked backward to a problem. The "problem" found this way is usually fake.
- **Tar pit idea**: looks tractable but has hidden structural reasons no one has solved it. Check by Googling, finding prior attempts, and talking to those founders.
- **No founder-market fit**: the team has no edge in this domain.
- **Non-acute problem**: people can live without a solution — the alternative isn't "nothing."

The 10-question YC framework (from the source):
1. Founder-market fit
2. Market size (large now or rapidly growing)
3. Problem acuteness
4. Competition (good sign, not bad)
5. Personal desire / proximity to users
6. Recently enabled by change in world
7. Proxy companies that validate demand
8. Long-term motivation
9. Scalability
10. Quality of the idea space (different spaces have wildly different base rates)

**Idea spaces** matter because they set the base rate for any idea within them. Fintech infra and vertical SaaS had high hit rates historically; consumer hardware and ad tech did not. Even if your first idea in a space fails, a good space lets you pivot into adjacent ideas.

**Contrarian signals that are actually positive:**
- Hard to start (schlep blindness — deters competition)
- Boring domain (higher hit rate, less picked-over)
- Existing competitors (proves real demand; you just need a new insight)

## Open Questions

- How do idea space hit rates shift over time? The source acknowledges fintech infra may not stay as hot — what's the current equivalent in 2026?
- Is there a principled way to identify tar pit ideas before you're stuck, beyond "Google it and talk to prior founders"?
- Does founder-market fit compensate for a weak idea, or does it only help when the idea is already directionally correct?
