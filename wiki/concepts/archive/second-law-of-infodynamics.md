## Definition

Proposed by Melvin Vopson, the Second Law of Infodynamics states that the information entropy of any system must remain constant or decrease over time. This is the inverse of the Second Law of Thermodynamics, which states that thermodynamic entropy must remain constant or increase. Vopson grounds it in Shannon's information theory and Landauer's principle (which connects thermodynamics and information erasure).

## How I Think About It

The name is deliberately provocative — calling something a "law" before it has broad empirical support is a bold move. The interesting core idea is that information-bearing systems tend toward more ordered, compressed states over time. Vopson's supporting examples so far: (1) SARS-CoV-2 mutations reducing genetic information entropy, (2) gravitational clustering reducing spatial information entropy. Both are interesting, but two data points don't establish a universal law. The concept of "information entropy" here needs careful definition to be scientifically testable.

## Open Questions

- How exactly is "information entropy" being quantified in these physical systems — is the definition consistent across applications?
- Has Vopson's Second Law of Infodynamics been independently tested or replicated?
- Is the apparent entropy decrease in virus mutations a real effect or selection/sampling artifact?
- Does the "law" apply to open systems, closed systems, or both?
