## Definition

Community biotechnology is the movement to democratize access to synthetic biology tools, knowledge, and participation—enabling people outside traditional academic and industrial labs to engage in biological research, design, and creation. It includes low-cost open-source lab equipment, community biolabs, open-source knowledge repositories, and movement-building around shared ethics and purpose.

## How I Think About It

The core claim is that access to biotechnology has always been gatekept by cost, geography, and supply chain—predominantly Western and well-resourced. The community biotech movement attacks this at multiple layers simultaneously: tools (a $10 DNA electroporator made from a flyswatter), knowledge (open coursework like "How to Grow Almost Anything"), infrastructure (300+ community biolabs globally), and culture (explicit ethics, values, movement-building with community organizing tactics borrowed from civil rights work).

What's striking about David Sun Kong's approach is that he treats the human community as the critical variable—not just the technical one. He explicitly imported a legendary community organizer (Marshall Gantz) to help the global community biolab network develop a shared purpose statement. This is unusual: most science democratization efforts stop at lowering tool costs. Kong argues that without shared values and movement identity, cheap tools can still produce harmful outcomes.

There's also a vision shift embedded here: from synthetic biology as a tool for human commercial benefit toward synthetic biology as a tool for ecosystem mutualism—bringing back extinct species, engineering organisms that help rather than harm natural systems.
