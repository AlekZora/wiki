## Definition

Two families of mechanism that both make a game hard to put down, and that are easy to confuse
because they look identical for about twenty-four hours. **Craft mechanisms** make the next
attempt genuinely attractive. **Compulsion mechanisms** make stopping feel costly. Both raise
D1. They come apart at D7 and separate completely by D30.

**Status: proposed.** Argued from design reasoning plus the retention figures in
[web-game-distribution-and-retention-2026.md](../sources/web-game-distribution-and-retention-2026.md).
Not yet tested against a build.

## The Craft List

- **Sub-second restart.** Failure to next attempt in under a second: no confirmation, no score
  screen to dismiss, no ad. Every frame between death and retry is a place the player can leave.
  Probably the highest-leverage single property in the arcade form, and nearly free.
- **Legible failure.** The player sees the exact cause and believes they could avoid it. Failure
  attributed to oneself drives retry; failure attributed to the game drives exit.
- **A near miss the player can name.** A personal best sitting just above where they keep dying,
  plus a plan for beating it. "I died at 41 and my best is 44" is a reason to continue; a bare
  number is not.
- **Mastery slope.** The player gets better; the numbers do not get bigger. Difficulty revealed
  by growing competence rather than added by escalating stats. This is the 2048 point in
  [Elegance (Game Design)](elegance-game-design.md) — difficulty generated by the player's own
  accumulation rather than a tuned dial.
- **Runs that end before satisfaction.** A run should finish while appetite is still live.
  Ending a session satisfied is a design failure in this genre.
- **Feel.** Input latency under roughly 50ms, stable frame pacing, immediate audio response.
  Feel is a retention mechanism, not end-stage polish, and belongs in the first prototype.

## The Compulsion List

These work, and they are a loan against the game's future: energy systems and forced waits;
streaks whose value is the pain of breaking them; variable-ratio reward schedules tuned for
their own sake; interstitials at emotional peaks; manufactured scarcity and countdowns.

## How I Think About It

The commercial and the ethical argument point the same way here, which is unusual enough to
state rather than treat as a happy accident.

1. Craft mechanisms compound with player skill. Compulsion mechanisms decay as the player
   habituates, which is why they need continual escalation to hold the same line.
2. Ad-supported titles already sit near 5.4% D30. A retention structure that decays is being
   applied to a business model with no margin for decay.
3. Portals promote what retains over weeks, not what spikes on day one. Optimising the spike
   optimises the wrong term — and optimising the spike *because it is the measurable one* is
   [The Metrics Trap](metrics-trap.md) exactly.
4. Guideline 4.2 and portal quality review both reward a game that feels like a game rather than
   a funnel.

**The convergence worth designing around.** In a run-based basic game the moment of failure is
simultaneously the peak of craft-side retry desire *and* the only natural, non-intrusive ad slot
— the rewarded continue. The player opts in, receives something they actually wanted, and
nothing is interrupted. The design goal and the monetization slot are the same moment, so a game
built this way is not forced to choose.

**Where the vault already has evidence.** Attractor's design note states that nothing in the
game is random, that the only force pulling danger toward the player is the player's own held
finger, and therefore that "every death is self-caused" — see
[attractor-zone.md](../projects/attractor/attractor-zone.md). That is the legible-failure
mechanism above, arrived at independently and shipped. Whether it produced the predicted
retention has not been checked against Attractor's own numbers, and doing so is the cheapest
available test of this whole concept.

The same project supplies a warning the craft list does not yet encode. The Attractor feel spec
diagnoses a near-miss cue that fired only *after* the player released and the danger had
resolved, leaving "the moment the game is built around" with no feedback at all. Legible failure
is necessary but not sufficient: the feedback has to arrive **while the decision is still open**,
not as an epilogue. That timing claim deserves promotion into the craft list once someone
confirms it held after the fix.

## What It Is Not

Not an argument that all reward scheduling is manipulation, or that games owe players a flat
experience. Variance is ancient and legitimate. The line is whether the mechanism gives the
player a reason to return or a penalty for leaving.

## Design Uses

- As a review checklist: name which family each retention mechanism in the build belongs to, and
  require a justification for anything on the second list.
- As a tiebreak when two designs test similarly in a short window — prefer the one whose
  stickiness comes from the first list, because short-window proxies cannot see the difference
  and D30 will.

## Open Questions

- Is the line actually clean, or are the strongest games in this genre using both and hiding the
  seam?
- Rewarded continue is defended above as the honest ad slot, but it also sells the player out of
  a legitimate failure. At what frequency does it become the thing it was meant to avoid?
- Does sub-second restart survive a portal SDK that wants an ad impression at the same moment?
- Attractor has live data and this concept has predictions. Nobody has compared them.
