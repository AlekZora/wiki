## Definition

The simulation hypothesis proposes that what we experience as physical reality is actually a highly sophisticated computational simulation created by beings of far greater technological advancement than us. Philosopher Nick Bostrom formalized this in 2003, arguing that at least one of three things must be true: civilizations go extinct before reaching simulation-capable technology, advanced civilizations choose not to run simulations, or we are almost certainly living in a simulation.

## How I Think About It

It's a philosophical claim dressed in technological language. The argument is structurally similar to Descartes' evil demon — unfalsifiable in its strongest form. Where it gets interesting is when physicists try to find empirical signatures: if the universe is a simulation, we might expect built-in compression, optimization, or quantization artifacts. The discreteness of quantum mechanics and the existence of maximum speeds/energies are sometimes cited as candidate artifacts.
