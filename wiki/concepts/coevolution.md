## Definition

Coevolution is the process by which two or more adaptive systems mutually shape each other's development over time, such that neither one is the fixed environment for the other. In biological evolution, predator and prey coevolve: each adaptation in the prey creates selection pressure that shapes the predator, which in turn reshapes the prey's fitness landscape. The fitness landscape is never stable because all occupants are simultaneously moving across it.

Kevin Kelly's most precise illustration is the "chameleon on the mirror": a color-adapting lizard placed in a mirrored box creates a feedback loop with its own reflection. The lizard and the mirror form a new joint entity — "lizard-glass" — that behaves differently from either component alone. The chameleon cannot be separated from its mirror; together they are a different kind of thing.

## How I Think About It

The key insight is that coevolution destroys the concept of a fixed target. In engineering, you specify a goal state and design a system to reach it. Coevolution says the goal state is itself a system participant — it moves in response to your progress toward it. This is not a failure condition; it is the normal operating mode of any system embedded in a living environment.

The practical implication is that optimization against a fixed target is only valid for closed, non-adaptive environments. Anywhere you find adaptation — markets, populations, users, cultures — you are in a coevolutionary system. The concept of "optimal solution" becomes "current equilibrium," and even that is temporary.

Kelly's three hypothetical outcomes for the chameleon on the mirror capture the general range of coevolutionary dynamics:
1. **Convergence to equilibrium** (Bateson's hypothesis): the system finds a stable attractor — a compromise color that satisfies both the lizard's adaptation and the mirror's reflection.
2. **Perpetual oscillation** (Brand's hypothesis): the system never settles, flickering continuously through configurations as each adaptation triggers a new response.
3. **Lock-in at initial condition** (Hall's hypothesis): the coupling is so tight and immediate that adaptation is impossible; the system freezes at whatever state it entered.

Which outcome occurs depends on feedback delay, sensitivity, and dampening — the same control parameters that determine behavior in any feedback circuit.

## AI Integration

- **User-AI coevolution**: AI systems and their users are in a coevolutionary relationship. The AI adapts to user feedback (RLHF, fine-tuning, usage patterns); users adapt their behavior, language, and expectations to the AI's capabilities and tendencies. Neither is the fixed environment. The alignment target moves because aligned AI changes what users want and how they think.
- **Model generation coevolution**: successive AI model versions coevolve with the training data generated by their predecessors. GPT-n outputs become training data for GPT-n+1; each generation shapes the landscape the next one is trained on. This is cultural evolution operating at the level of model generations rather than human generations.
- **Red team / blue team coevolution**: adversarial security testing is explicitly coevolutionary — attack capabilities and defense capabilities mutually drive each other's development. No fixed threat model is valid; the threat model itself evolves in response to defenses.
- **Recommendation systems**: a recommendation algorithm trained on user behavior changes that behavior, which generates new training signal, which changes the algorithm. The system and its users are lizard-glass — neither one is the independent variable. What the algorithm "recommends" is a property of the joint system, not of the algorithm alone.
- **The alignment tension**: Kelly names it precisely — "We cannot import evolution and learning without exporting control." Any system capable of learning from its environment will coevolve with that environment. Aligned AI that can genuinely learn will, in time, reshape what "aligned" means. This is not a solvable problem; it is a design constraint that must be managed continuously.
- **Stable point or oscillation?**: whether an AI-user system converges to equilibrium, oscillates, or locks in depends on feedback delay (how quickly model updates reach users), sensitivity (how strongly each update changes behavior), and dampening (how much noise or inertia resists change). Designing for coevolutionary stability means designing these parameters consciously, not just optimizing objective functions.

## Open Questions

- Is there a phase transition in coevolutionary dynamics — a connectivity or adaptation-rate threshold above which systems lock into oscillation rather than equilibrium?
- How do you design AI systems that coevolve beneficially with users rather than drifting toward engagement-maximizing pathologies?
- Can coevolutionary dynamics be used deliberately for capability development? (Train an AI against an adaptive adversary to accelerate capability growth.)
- What is the timescale of human-AI coevolution? Are we already meaningfully different users than we were before LLMs, and is the AI meaningfully different because of what we've done with it?
- At what point does a coevolving AI-user system constitute a new kind of entity — a "lizard-glass" — with properties neither the AI nor the human possesses independently?

The Side Quest AI system is in a coevolutionary relationship with the player: the NPC quest generator adapts to the player's history; the player adapts their behavior in response to what quests appear.
