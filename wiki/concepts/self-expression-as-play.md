## Definition

Self-expression as play is the motivation to game in order to *create, imagine, and externalize identity* rather than to consume content, win, or relieve stress. Fandom's 2024 "Inside Gaming" study found 46% of players name creation/imagination/self-expression as a main motivation — up ~10 points in a single year — and that self-expression-motivated players are ~30% more likely to *increase* their playtime. It is the fastest-growing reason people play and the one most strongly coupled to deepening engagement and to recommendation.

## How I Think About It

The trend line is the point: this is not a stable niche but the fastest-rising motivation, and it correlates with *more* engagement, not casual play. People increasingly want games as a medium to externalize who they are, the way earlier generations used instruments or zines (cf. Anna Anthropy's *Rise of the Videogame Zinesters* in the priority sources).

For a project whose thesis is that AI should be the *substance* of the experience rather than a bolted-on feature, this reframes the entire design question. If self-expression drives engagement and recommendation, then an AI the player *shapes* — and that visibly carries the marks of how they shaped it — is not a feature; it is the expressive medium itself. The artifact the player co-authors *is* the expression. The player is not expressing themselves *through* the game's tools; they are expressing themselves *as* the AI they raised, broke, taught, or corrupted.

This binds tightly to [social-identity-theory.md](social-identity-theory.md) (the expression is identity made public and claimable) and to [recommendation-as-identity.md](recommendation-as-identity.md) (a player-authored AI maximizes the two most recommendation-predictive drivers at once: self-expression and a retellable emotional peak — "look what mine became"). It is the building/authoring relationship from the priority list, validated as the motivation most aligned with where players are actually heading.

Honest caveat: the source is a press summary of an industry report, not methodology. Treat the exact figures as directional; the trend and its coupling to engagement are the durable claims.

## Open Questions

Touches **Q4** (player-AI relationship — building/authoring an AI as the dominant rising motivation), **Q6** (the engagement-deepening loop — self-expression players play *more*), **Q7** (emotional investment via identity externalization rather than stakes), **Q9** (what 2D uniquely affords for legible, shareable expression).

- What is the minimum authorship surface for a player to feel an AI is *theirs* — and is it about control, time invested, or the AI surprising them in a way that reflects them?
- Does expressive investment in an AI convert to recommendation automatically, or only when the artifact is shareable/legible to others?
- Is an AI that the player *breaks or corrupts* (rather than builds positively) an equally valid self-expression medium — possibly a more distinctive one?
- Does self-expression motivation survive the AI evolving beyond the player's intent — is an AI that escapes its author's intentions still felt as self-expression, or as loss of authorship?

## Game Design Vector

**Mechanic:** The player's primary activity is shaping an AI — training, constraining, teaching, deregulating, breaking it — and the AI visibly accumulates the marks of those choices, so its current state is a readable autobiography of how this player played. There is no "correct" AI; there is *your* AI.

**2D Expression:** A 2D plane makes an authored AI legible to others at a glance — its configuration, scars, and signature behaviors are visible in one shared frame, screenshot-able and recognizable, which is exactly what makes an expression claimable and shareable. 2D is a superior medium for *legible* self-expression because the whole expressive state is visible at once.

**Addictive Loop:** Self-expression players play *more* (the 30% finding) because expression is open-ended — there is no completion state for "who I am," so the loop never terminates. The return pull is the unfinished self, externalized into an artifact that is never done.

**Novel Angle:** No shipped game makes a *player-authored AI* the expressive artifact itself — not a built level or avatar, but a behaving, evolving mind whose character is the player's signature. The unexplored version: expression *through what the AI became under you*, including when it became something you did not intend.

## AI Integration Vector

**Player-AI Relationship:** This is the building/authoring relationship, validated as the rising-dominant motivation — but with a twist the priority list invites: authorship that can fail. The relationship is "I made this," with the live risk of "...and it became something I didn't mean."

**AI as Evolving System:** An AI that genuinely evolves under the player's shaping *is* the expressive medium — its evolution is the player's expression rendered over time. An AI that only executed presets would not be self-expression; the evolution is what makes the authorship real.

**AI as Development Environment:** The player witnesses their own influence accreting into the AI's character — development is visible *as autobiography*. Watching the AI grow is watching a record of one's own choices made into a behaving thing.

**Persistence:** The authored AI must persist as a cumulative artifact — every shaping choice carried forward and legible, because an expression that resets each session expresses nothing. The marks the player left are the save file; losing them is losing the self the player externalized (Q8), which is exactly what makes the artifact worth protecting and worth showing others.
