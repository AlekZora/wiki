***Flappy Bird*** is a 2013 [casual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casual_game "Casual game") [mobile game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_game "Mobile game") developed by Vietnamese [video game artist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game_artist "Video game artist") and [programmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game_programmer "Video game programmer") Dong Nguyen ([Vietnamese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_language "Vietnamese language"): *Nguyễn Hà Đông*), under his game development company [.Gears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/.Gears ".Gears").[^1] The game is a [side-scroller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Side-scrolling_video_game "Side-scrolling video game") where the player controls a bird, Faby, attempting to fly between columns of green pipes without hitting them. The player's score is determined by the number of pipes they fly through. Nguyen created the game over a period of several days, using the bird from a cancelled game made in 2012.

The game was released in May 2013 but received a sudden spike in popularity in early 2014, becoming a [sleeper hit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sleeper_hit "Sleeper hit"). *Flappy Bird* received poor reviews from some critics, who criticized its high level of difficulty and alleged plagiarism in graphics and [game mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_mechanics "Game mechanics"), while other reviewers found it addictive. At the end of January 2014, it was the most downloaded free game in the [App Store](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/App_Store_\(Apple\) "App Store (Apple)") for [iOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IOS "IOS"). During this period, its developer said that *Flappy Bird* was earning $50,000 a day from in-app advertisements as well as sales.

*Flappy Bird* was removed from both the App Store and [Google Play](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Play "Google Play") on February 10, 2014, with Nguyen claiming that he felt guilty over what he considered to be the game's addictive nature and overusage. Its popularity and sudden removal caused phones with the game installed before its removal to be put up for sale for high prices over the Internet.[^2] [^3] [Clones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game_clone "Video game clone") of *Flappy Bird* became popular on the App Store after the original app's removal, and both [Apple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_Inc. "Apple Inc.") and [Google](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google "Google") have removed games from their app stores for being too similar.[^4]

In August 2014, a revised version of *Flappy Bird*, called *Flappy Birds Family*, was released exclusively for the [Amazon Fire TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Fire_TV "Amazon Fire TV"). [Bay Tek Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_Tek_Entertainment "Bay Tek Entertainment") also released a licensed coin-operated *Flappy Bird* arcade game.[^5]

## Gameplay

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/5/52/Flappy_Bird_gameplay.png)

Faby after passing the first pair of pipes

*Flappy Bird* is an [arcade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arcade_game#Arcade_genre "Arcade game") -style game in which the player controls the bird Faby, which moves persistently to the right. They are tasked with navigating Faby through pairs of pipes that have equally sized gaps placed at random heights. Faby automatically descends and only ascends when the player taps the [touchscreen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Touchscreen "Touchscreen"). Each successful pass through a pair of pipes awards the player one point. Colliding with a pipe or the ground ends the gameplay. During the [game over](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_over "Game over") screen, the player is awarded a bronze medal if they reach ten or more points, a silver medal from twenty points, a gold medal from thirty points, and a platinum medal from forty points.[^6] [^7] [^8] [^9]

## Development

Dong Nguyen grew up in [Vạn Phúc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%E1%BA%A1n_Ph%C3%BAc,_H%C3%A0_%C4%90%C3%B4ng "Vạn Phúc, Hà Đông"), a village near [Hanoi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanoi "Hanoi"). He discovered video games by playing *[Super Mario Bros.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Mario_Bros. "Super Mario Bros.")* as a child and began coding his own at age 16. At 19, while studying programming at a local university, he won an internship at [Punch Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punch_Entertainment "Punch Entertainment"), one of the few video game companies in Vietnam. While using the [iPhone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPhone "IPhone"), he found that its most popular games such as *[Angry Birds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angry_Birds_\(video_game\) "Angry Birds (video game)")* were too complicated, and wanted to make a simpler game for people who are "always on the move".[^10]

*Flappy Bird* was created and developed by Nguyen in two to three days. The game was originally called "Flap Flap".[^11] The bird character, Faby, was originally designed in 2012 for a cancelled [platform game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platform_game "Platform game").[^12] The gameplay was inspired by the act of bouncing a ping pong ball against a paddle for as long as possible.[^13] Initially the game was significantly easier than it became in the final version, but Nguyen said he found this version to be boring and subsequently tightened up the difficulty.[^13] He described the business plan of a free download with in-game advertisements as "very common in the Japanese market".[^14]

Nguyen believes that contemporary Western games are overly complex.[^12] His company, [.Gears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/.Gears ".Gears"), describes its games as "heavily influenced by retro pixelated games in its golden age. Everything is pure, extremely hard and incredibly fun to play".[^15]

## Release

*Flappy Bird* was originally released on May 24, 2013,[^16] with support for the [iPhone 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPhone_5 "IPhone 5"). The game was subsequently updated for [iOS 7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IOS_7 "IOS 7") in September 2013. Although originally unsuccessful, the game received a massive influx of players after being reviewed by the Swedish YouTuber [PewDiePie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PewDiePie "PewDiePie").[^17] In January 2014, it topped the Free Apps chart in the US and Chinese [App Stores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/App_Store_\(iOS\) "App Store (iOS)"),[^18] and later that month topped the same section of the UK App Store where it was touted as "the new *[Angry Birds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angry_Birds "Angry Birds")* ".[^1] It ended January as the most downloaded App on the App Store.[^19] The Android version of *Flappy Bird* was released to the Google Play store on January 30, 2014.[^20] In early 2014, Nguyen said in an interview with *[The Verge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Verge "The Verge")* that the game was earning around $50,000 a day in revenue through its [in-game advertising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In-game_advertising "In-game advertising").[^14]

### Discontinuation

On February 8, 2014, Nguyen announced on [Twitter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitter "Twitter") that the game would be removed from both [Apple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_Inc. "Apple Inc.") 's [App Store](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/App_Store_\(iOS\) "App Store (iOS)") and [Google Play](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Play "Google Play"), writing: "I am sorry *Flappy Bird* users, 22 hours from now, I will take *Flappy Bird* down. I cannot take this anymore." [^21] He went on to say that taking down the game had "nothing to do with legal issues".[^22] The game was removed from both the App Store and Google Play exactly on time,[^23] [^24] much to the dismay of many fans.[^25] [^26]

*Tuoi Tre News*, the English-language edition of the Vietnamese newspaper *[Tuổi Trẻ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tu%E1%BB%95i_Tr%E1%BA%BB "Tuổi Trẻ")*, reported from a local technology expert that *Flappy Bird* 's removal could have been due to a legal challenge from [Nintendo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nintendo "Nintendo") over perceived visual similarities to the *Mario* games.[^27] This allegation was denied by a Nintendo spokesman to *[The Wall Street Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wall_Street_Journal "The Wall Street Journal")*.[^28] Lawyers in Vietnam also denied allegations that Nguyen had to remove the game due to violation of laws on Internet use in the country.[^26] [^28]

Following the removal, many media outlets reported that several merchants on [eBay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EBay "EBay") were offering phones which had the app pre-installed for [US](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_dollar "United States dollar") $1,499 or more, with some receiving bids of over $90,000;[^2] [^3] [^29] [^26] however, the listings were removed for violating eBay's rule stating that smartphones must be restored to factory settings before being sold.[^30]

In an interview with *Forbes*, Nguyen cited the game's addictive nature for its cancellation, stating: " *Flappy Bird* was designed to play in a few minutes when you are relaxed. But it happened to become an addictive product. I think it has become a problem. To solve that problem, it's best to take down *Flappy Bird*. It's gone forever." Nguyen said that the guilt that he felt over the game was affecting his sleep and that his conscience was relieved after he took down the game.[^31]

In a March 2014 interview with *[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone "Rolling Stone")*, Nguyen refused to rule out re-releasing *Flappy Bird*, on condition that it would come with a warning to "Take a break".[^10] On March 19, he announced via Twitter that the game will be rereleased onto App Stores, but not any time soon.[^32] On May 15, Nguyen told CNBC's Kelly Evans that the game would return in August, with multiplayer capability and will be "less addictive".[^33]

As Nguyen had promised, August 2014 saw a revised version of *Flappy Bird*, called *Flappy Birds Family*, released exclusively through the [Amazon Appstore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Appstore "Amazon Appstore") for the [Amazon Fire TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Fire_TV "Amazon Fire TV") platform.[^34] The new version also features new obstacles not in the original as well as an additional [multiplayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiplayer "Multiplayer") option.[^34]

### Unofficial reboot

On January 12, 2024, the trademark for *Flappy Bird* was terminated and subsequently handed over to Gametech Holdings after Dong Nguyen had failed to reclaim it.[^35] Gametech announced an unofficial reboot to the game on September 12, more than ten years after its discontinuation, under the name "The Flappy Bird Foundation", with additional features and characters. The game was released at the end of October 2024 with the mobile version being released in 2025.[^36] [^37] [^38] Nguyen stated that he has no connection with this game, nor did he sell any rights to the games.[^39]

## Reception

*Flappy Bird* received "mixed or average reviews" from critics, holding a [Metacritic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metacritic "Metacritic") score of 52/100, based on seven reviews.[^40] The app was criticized by the *[Huffington Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huffington_Post "Huffington Post")*, which described it as an "insanely irritating, difficult and frustrating game which combines a super-steep difficulty curve with bad, boring graphics and jerky movement".[^44] *[IGN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IGN "IGN")* gave the game a mediocre score of 5.4 out of 10, quipping that the game was as addictive as it was shallow. Despite criticizing its playability and its "no skill" requirement, IGN noted that the gameplay made it "an addictive short-term distraction" for the casual skill and score-obsessed players.[^42] The game's difficulty has been a source of ire for many users, with one user stating that it took him half an hour to achieve a score of five points.[^45] According to its creator, the game is slightly easier on [Android](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Android_\(operating_system\) "Android (operating system)") devices than on [iOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IOS "IOS").[^46] [^47]

### Controversy and criticism

When questioned at the time by Chocolate Lab Apps, a website for app developers, Nguyen claimed to have used no promotional methods in the marketing of *Flappy Bird*. He credited the sudden rise in the game's popularity in early 2014 to possibly be "luck".[^12] However, online marketer Carter Thomas suspected that the developer had used bots to cause its success.[^48] When questioned on this by *[The Daily Telegraph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Daily_Telegraph "The Daily Telegraph")*, Nguyen said that he respected other people's opinions and did not wish to comment, adding, "I'd like to make my games in peace." [^49] When *[Newsweek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newsweek "Newsweek")* inquired about the matter, Nguyen wrote, "If I did fake it, should Apple let it live for months?" [^50]

The game was criticised by *[Kotaku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kotaku "Kotaku")* for what it claimed was overt use of *[Mario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_\(franchise\) "Mario (franchise)")* -style graphics,[^51] referring to it as "ripped-off art". It later published a [correction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Correction_\(newspaper\) "Correction (newspaper)"), clarifying that the game's green pipe was "a new albeit unoriginal drawing".[^52]

According to some Vietnamese newspapers, including *[Thanh Niên](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thanh_Ni%C3%AAn "Thanh Niên")* and BBC Vietnamese, *Flappy Bird* is very similar to a game released in 2011 (two years before *Flappy Bird*) called *Piou Piou vs. Cactus*, from the gameplay (by tapping on the screen) to the main character design (a small yellow bird with big red beak) to the obstacles (green cacti and pipes).[^53] Several French newspapers, including [*20 minutes*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/20_minutes_\(France\) "20 minutes (France)") [^54] and *[Metronews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metronews "Metronews")*,[^55] reported on claims of *Flappy Bird* itself being a clone.[^56] *Thanh Niên* found the similarities between *Piou Piou vs. Cactus* and *Flappy Bird* to be astonishing.[^57]

The [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France "France") developer of *Piou Piou vs. Cactus*, known as Kek, told *[Pocket Gamer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pocket_Gamer "Pocket Gamer")* that he, too, noticed that *Flappy Bird* is "very similar" to his earlier game. Kek contacted Nguyen, who said that when he created *Flappy Bird*, "he doesn't think he knew about" Kek's game.[^58] Technology editor Patrick O'Rourke of [Canada.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada.com "Canada.com") also charged that *Flappy Bird* is "almost a complete ripoff" of *Piou Piou vs Cactus*, as well as that its primary gameplay mechanic is a "rip off" from a game called *Helicopter Game*, and that it heavily borrowed sound effects from [*Super Mario Bros.*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Mario_\(series\) "Super Mario (series)") games.[^59]

## Legacy

Since the game's removal, numerous remakes and parodies have been spawned,[^31] [^60] such as [Sesame Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sesame_Street "Sesame Street") 's *Flappy Bert* [^61] and [Fall Out Boy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_Out_Boy "Fall Out Boy") 's *Fall Out Bird*.[^62] *Flappy Bird* became one of the most [cloned games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game_clone "Video game clone") in Apple's App Store.[^63] At the peak of its popularity, over 60 clones per day [^64] were appearing on the App Store, prompting both Google and Apple to begin rejecting games with the word *Flappy* in the name.[^65] [CNET](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNET "CNET") reviewed seven " *Flappy* copycats" for [iOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IOS "IOS") two days after the original game's removal, describing the options as "pretty bleak", but singling out the underwater *[Splashy Fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Splashy_Fish "Splashy Fish")* as the closest approximation of *Flappy Bird*.[^66] [^67] [^68]

Shortly after the game's removal, security researchers warned that some versions of *Flappy Bird* and its imitators available on alternative Android app stores have been found to contain [malware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malware "Malware") that can lead to unauthorised charges to a user's phone bills. The number matching game *[Threes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Threes_\(video_game\) "Threes (video game)")* has been compared to *Flappy Bird* because of the similarities between how people react to them and by the chain of clones that they are both respectively part of.[^69]

In February 2014, the non-profit computer science education organization [Code.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Code.org "Code.org") unveiled a set of lessons that would allow the student to make their own *Flappy Bird* clone.[^70] [^71]

Nguyen's other games *Super Ball Juggling* and *Shuriken Block* ranked at 6th and 18th respectively on the [App Store](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/App_Store_\(iOS\) "App Store (iOS)") during early February 2014 on the back of *Flappy Bird* 's success.[^31] He revealed in March 2014 that he was developing three other games at the time, in similar formats to his previous releases.[^10] One of those games was released in 2014 as *[Swing Copters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swing_Copters "Swing Copters")* and uses similar gameplay as *Flappy Bird*.

An [Easter egg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Easter_egg_\(media\) "Easter egg (media)") in [Android Lollipop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Android_Lollipop "Android Lollipop") (version 5.0/5.1) allows users to play a modified version of *Flappy Bird*, entitled "L Land", involving a flying Android and Lollipops replacing the pipes.[^72] [^73] It was revamped in [Android Marshmallow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Android_Marshmallow "Android Marshmallow") (version 6.0) as "Marshmallow Lad", adding multiplayer support for up to 6 players.[^74]

On New Year's Eve in 2014, [Google](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google "Google") displayed an animated [Google Doodle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Doodle "Google Doodle") featuring *Flappy Bird* as one of the year's most searched phenomena, alongside the [World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup "2014 FIFA World Cup"), the [Ice Bucket Challenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_Bucket_Challenge "Ice Bucket Challenge") and the [*Philae* spacecraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philae_\(spacecraft\) "Philae (spacecraft)").[^75] A similar game featuring Talking Tom was released as a mini-game for *[My Talking Tom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Talking_Tom "My Talking Tom")*, called Flappy Tom, and the same game was featured in [*Talking Tom and Friends*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talking_Tom_and_Friends_\(TV_series\) "Talking Tom and Friends (TV series)").

In 2016, video game player and livestreamer [SethBling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SethBling "SethBling") [replicated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SethBling#Other_ventures "SethBling") *Flappy Bird* within *[Super Mario World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Mario_World "Super Mario World")* through [code injection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Code_injection "Code injection").[^76]

In June 2023, the dwarf mining game *[Deep Rock Galactic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Rock_Galactic "Deep Rock Galactic") '* s Season 04 update put an [arcade cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arcade_cabinet "Arcade cabinet") into the in-game bar containing the Flappy Bird parody game "Jetty Boot" as a "training module" for obtaining the Jet Boots players can find in the game's missions.[^77]
