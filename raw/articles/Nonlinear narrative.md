**Nonlinear narrative**, **disjointed narrative**, or **disrupted narrative** is a [narrative technique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narratology "Narratology") where events are portrayed, for example, out of [chronological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chronological "Chronological") order or in other ways where the narrative does not follow the direct causality pattern of the events featured, such as parallel distinctive plot lines, dream immersions or narrating another story inside the main plot-line. The technique is common in [electronic literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_literature "Electronic literature"), and particularly in [hypertext fiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypertext_fiction "Hypertext fiction"),[^1] and is also well-established in print and other sequential media.

## Literature

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/35/Tristram_Shandy_Plot_lines.png/250px-Tristram_Shandy_Plot_lines.png)

Non-linear plot lines, illustration from Laurence Sterne 's The Life and Opinions of Tristram Shandy, Gentleman (1759–1767)

Beginning a non-linear narrative *[in medias res](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_medias_res "In medias res")* (Latin: "into the middle of things") began in ancient times and was used as a convention of [epic poetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epic_poetry "Epic poetry"), including [Homer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homer "Homer") 's *[Iliad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iliad "Iliad")* in the 8th century BC. The technique of narrating most of the story in [flashback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flashback_\(narrative\) "Flashback (narrative)") is also seen in epic poetry, like the [Indian epic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_epic_poetry "Indian epic poetry") the *[Mahabharata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahabharata "Mahabharata")*. Several medieval *[Arabian Nights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Thousand_and_One_Nights "One Thousand and One Nights")* tales such as " [The City of Brass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_City_of_Brass_\(One_Thousand_and_One_Nights\) "The City of Brass (One Thousand and One Nights)") " and " [The Three Apples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Three_Apples "The Three Apples") " also had nonlinear narratives employing the *in medias res* and flashback techniques.[^2] The medieval English poem *[Beowulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beowulf "Beowulf")* also utilizes a non-linear structure, focusing on events throughout the life of the titular character rather than describing them in a linear narrative.[^3]

From the late 19th century and early 20th century, [modernist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modernist_literature "Modernist literature") novelists [Joseph Conrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Conrad "Joseph Conrad"), [Virginia Woolf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Woolf "Virginia Woolf"), [Ford Madox Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Madox_Ford "Ford Madox Ford"), [Marcel Proust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcel_Proust "Marcel Proust"), and [William Faulkner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Faulkner "William Faulkner") experimented with narrative chronology and abandoning linear order.[^4]

- [Thomas Carlyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Carlyle "Thomas Carlyle") 's *[Sartor Resartus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sartor_Resartus "Sartor Resartus")* (c. 1833)
- [Ford Madox Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Madox_Ford "Ford Madox Ford") 's *[The Good Soldier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Good_Soldier "The Good Soldier")* (1915)
- [William Faulkner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Faulkner "William Faulkner") 's *[The Sound and the Fury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sound_and_the_Fury "The Sound and the Fury")* (1929)

- [James Joyce's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Joyce "James Joyce") *[Ulysses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulysses_\(novel\) "Ulysses (novel)")* (1922) and *[Finnegans Wake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnegans_Wake "Finnegans Wake")* (1939)
- [Flann O'Brien (pseudonym for Brian O'Nolan)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_O%27Nolan "Brian O'Nolan") 's *[At Swim-Two-Birds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/At_Swim-Two-Birds "At Swim-Two-Birds")* (1939)

- [Kurt Vonnegut's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_Vonnegut "Kurt Vonnegut") *[Slaughterhouse-Five](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slaughterhouse-Five "Slaughterhouse-Five")* (1969)

Several of Michael Moorcock's novels, particularly those in the Jerry Cornelius series, in particular *[The English Assassin: A Romance of Entropy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_English_Assassin:_A_Romance_of_Entropy "The English Assassin: A Romance of Entropy")* (1972) and *[The Condition of Muzak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Condition_of_Muzak "The Condition of Muzak")* (1977) are notable for extending the nonlinear narrative form in order to explore the complex nature of identity within a multiversal universe.

[Scott McCloud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_McCloud "Scott McCloud") argues in *[Understanding Comics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Understanding_Comics "Understanding Comics")* that the narration of comics is nonlinear because it relies on the reader's choices and interactions.

## Film

Defining nonlinear structure in film is, at times, difficult. Films may use extensive [flashbacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flashback_\(narrative\) "Flashback (narrative)") or [flashforwards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flashforward "Flashforward") within a linear storyline, while nonlinear films often contain linear sequences.[^6] [Orson Welles'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orson_Welles "Orson Welles") *[Citizen Kane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizen_Kane "Citizen Kane")* (1941)—influenced structurally by *[The Power and the Glory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Power_and_the_Glory_\(1933_film\) "The Power and the Glory (1933 film)")* (1933)—and [Akira Kurosawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akira_Kurosawa "Akira Kurosawa") 's *[Rashomon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashomon_\(film\) "Rashomon (film)")* (1950) use a non-chronological flashback narrative that is often labeled nonlinear.[^7]

### Silent and early era

Experimentation with nonlinear structure in film dates back to the [silent film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silent_film "Silent film") era, including [D. W. Griffith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D._W._Griffith "D. W. Griffith") 's *[Intolerance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intolerance_\(film\) "Intolerance (film)")* (1916) and [Abel Gance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abel_Gance "Abel Gance") 's *[Napoléon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napol%C3%A9on_\(1927_film\) "Napoléon (1927 film)")* (1927).[^8] Nonlinear film emerged from the French [avant-garde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avant-garde "Avant-garde") \[5\] in 1924 with [René Clair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ren%C3%A9_Clair "René Clair") ’s *[Entr'acte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entr%27acte_\(film\) "Entr'acte (film)")*, Dadaïst film and then in 1929 with [Luis Buñuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Bu%C3%B1uel "Luis Buñuel") and [Salvador Dalí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvador_Dal%C3%AD "Salvador Dalí") 's *[Un Chien Andalou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Un_Chien_Andalou "Un Chien Andalou")* (English: *An Andalusian Dog*). The [surrealist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surrealist "Surrealist") film jumps into fantasy and juxtaposes images, granting the filmmakers an ability to create statements about the Church, art, and society that are left open to interpretation.[^9] Buñuel and Dalí's *[L'Âge d'Or](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%27%C3%82ge_d%27Or "L'Âge d'Or")* (1930) (English: *The Golden Age*) also uses nonlinear concepts. The revolutionary Soviet filmmakers [Sergei Eisenstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Eisenstein "Sergei Eisenstein"), [Vsevolod Pudovkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vsevolod_Pudovkin "Vsevolod Pudovkin"), and [Alexander Dovzhenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Dovzhenko "Alexander Dovzhenko") also experimented with the possibilities of nonlinearity. Eisenstein's *[Strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strike_\(1925_film\) "Strike (1925 film)")* (1925) and Dovzhenko's *[Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth_\(1930_film\) "Earth (1930 film)")* (1930) hint at a nonlinear experience.[^10] English director [Humphrey Jennings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humphrey_Jennings "Humphrey Jennings") used a nonlinear approach in his World War II documentary *[Listen to Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Listen_to_Britain "Listen to Britain")* (1942).[^10]

### Post-World War II

[Jean-Luc Godard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Luc_Godard "Jean-Luc Godard") 's works since 1959 were also important in the evolution of nonlinear film. Godard famously stated, "I agree that a film should have a beginning, a middle and an end - but not necessarily in that order".[^11] Godard's *[Week End](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Week_End_\(1967_film\) "Week End (1967 film)")* (French: *Le weekend*) (1968), as well as [Andy Warhol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Warhol "Andy Warhol") 's *[Chelsea Girls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelsea_Girls "Chelsea Girls")* (1966), defy linear structure in exchange for a chronology of events that is seemingly random.[^12] [Alain Resnais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alain_Resnais "Alain Resnais") experimented with narrative and time in his films *[Hiroshima mon amour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiroshima_mon_amour "Hiroshima mon amour")* (1959), *[L'Année dernière à Marienbad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%27Ann%C3%A9e_derni%C3%A8re_%C3%A0_Marienbad "L'Année dernière à Marienbad")* (1961), and *[Muriel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muriel_\(film\) "Muriel (film)")* (1963). [Federico Fellini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federico_Fellini "Federico Fellini") defined his own nonlinear cinema with the films *[La Strada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Strada "La Strada")* (1954), *[La Dolce Vita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Dolce_Vita "La Dolce Vita")* (1960), *[8½](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8%C2%BD "8½")* (1963), *[Fellini Satyricon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellini_Satyricon "Fellini Satyricon")* (1969), and *[Roma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roma_\(1972_film\) "Roma (1972 film)")* (1972), as did [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union") filmmaker [Andrei Tarkovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrei_Tarkovsky "Andrei Tarkovsky") with his [modernist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modernist "Modernist") films [Solaris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solaris_\(1972_film\) "Solaris (1972 film)") (1972), *[The Mirror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mirror_\(1975_film\) "The Mirror (1975 film)")* (1975) and *[Nostalghia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nostalghia "Nostalghia")* (1983). [Nicolas Roeg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolas_Roeg "Nicolas Roeg") 's films, including *[Performance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Performance_\(film\) "Performance (film)")* (1970), *[Walkabout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walkabout_\(film\) "Walkabout (film)")* (1971), *[Don't Look Now](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don%27t_Look_Now "Don't Look Now")* (1973), *[The Man Who Fell to Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Man_Who_Fell_to_Earth_\(film\) "The Man Who Fell to Earth (film)")* (1976), and *[Bad Timing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_Timing "Bad Timing")* (1980) are characterized by a nonlinear approach.[^13] Other mainstream nonlinear filmmakers include [Michelangelo Antonioni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michelangelo_Antonioni "Michelangelo Antonioni"), [Peter Greenaway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Greenaway "Peter Greenaway"), [Chris Marker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Marker "Chris Marker"), [Theo Angelopoulos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theo_Angelopoulos "Theo Angelopoulos"), [Agnès Varda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agn%C3%A8s_Varda "Agnès Varda"), [Raúl Ruiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ra%C3%BAl_Ruiz_\(director\) "Raúl Ruiz (director)"), [Carlos Saura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Saura "Carlos Saura"), [Alain Robbe-Grillet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alain_Robbe-Grillet "Alain Robbe-Grillet").[^14]

In the United States, [Robert Altman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Altman "Robert Altman") carried the nonlinear motif in his films, including *[McCabe & Mrs. Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McCabe_%26_Mrs._Miller "McCabe & Mrs. Miller")* (1971), *[Nashville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nashville_\(film\) "Nashville (film)")* (1975), *[The Player](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Player_\(1992_film\) "The Player (1992 film)")* (1992), *[Short Cuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_Cuts "Short Cuts")* (1993), and *[Gosford Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gosford_Park "Gosford Park")* (2001).[^15] [Woody Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woody_Allen "Woody Allen") embraced the experimental nature of nonlinear narrative in *[Annie Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annie_Hall "Annie Hall")* (1977), *[Interiors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interiors "Interiors")* (1978), and *[Stardust Memories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stardust_Memories "Stardust Memories")* (1980).

### 1990s and 2000s

In the 1990s, [Quentin Tarantino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quentin_Tarantino "Quentin Tarantino") influenced a tremendous increase in the popularity of nonlinear films, most notably *[Pulp Fiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulp_Fiction "Pulp Fiction")* (1994). He also used nonlinear narrative in *[Reservoir Dogs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reservoir_Dogs "Reservoir Dogs")* (1992), *Kill Bill* ([2003](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kill_Bill:_Volume_1 "Kill Bill: Volume 1") and [2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kill_Bill:_Volume_2 "Kill Bill: Volume 2")) and *[The Hateful Eight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hateful_Eight "The Hateful Eight")* (2015). Critics have referred shifting of timeline as Tarantino effect.[^9] Other important nonlinear films include [Atom Egoyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atom_Egoyan "Atom Egoyan") 's *[Exotica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exotica_\(film\) "Exotica (film)")* (1994), [Terrence Malick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrence_Malick "Terrence Malick") 's *[The Thin Red Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Thin_Red_Line_\(1998_film\) "The Thin Red Line (1998 film)")* (1998), and Karen and [Jill Sprecher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jill_Sprecher "Jill Sprecher") 's *[Thirteen Conversations About One Thing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirteen_Conversations_About_One_Thing "Thirteen Conversations About One Thing")* (2001).[^9] [David Lynch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Lynch) experimented with nonlinear narrative and [surrealism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surrealism "Surrealism") in *[Lost Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lost_Highway_\(film\) "Lost Highway (film)")* (1997), *[Mulholland Drive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulholland_Drive_\(film\) "Mulholland Drive (film)")* (2001), and *[Inland Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inland_Empire_\(film\) "Inland Empire (film)")* (2006).

In the years leading into and the beginning of the 21st century, some filmmakers have returned to the use of nonlinear narrative repeatedly, including [Steven Soderbergh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Soderbergh "Steven Soderbergh") in *[Schizopolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schizopolis "Schizopolis")* (1996), *[Out of Sight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Out_of_Sight "Out of Sight")* (1998), *[The Limey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Limey "The Limey")* (1999), *[Full Frontal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_Frontal_\(film\) "Full Frontal (film)")* (2002), *[Solaris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solaris_\(2002_film\) "Solaris (2002 film)")* (2002), and *[Che](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Che_\(2008_film\) "Che (2008 film)")* (2008); and [Christopher Nolan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Nolan "Christopher Nolan") in *[Following](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Following "Following")* (1998), *[Memento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memento_\(film\) "Memento (film)")* (2000), *[Batman Begins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batman_Begins "Batman Begins")* (2005), *[The Prestige](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Prestige "The Prestige")* (2006), *[Inception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inception "Inception")* (2010), *[The Dark Knight Rises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Dark_Knight_Rises "The Dark Knight Rises")* (2012), *[Dunkirk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunkirk_\(2017_film\) "Dunkirk (2017 film)")* (2017) and *[Oppenheimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oppenheimer_\(film\) "Oppenheimer (film)")* (2023). *Memento*, with its fragmentation and [reverse chronology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reverse_chronology "Reverse chronology"), has been described as characteristic of moving towards [postmodernism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postmodernism "Postmodernism") in contemporary cinema. Another example would be [Terrence Malick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrence_Malick "Terrence Malick") 's acclaimed *[The Tree of Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Tree_of_Life_\(film\) "The Tree of Life (film)")* (2011). The element of reverse chronology was explored further in [Gaspar Noé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaspar_No%C3%A9 "Gaspar Noé") 's 2002 film *[Irréversible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irr%C3%A9versible "Irréversible")*. Noé's 2009 film *[Enter the Void](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enter_the_Void "Enter the Void")* also used an uncommon narrative structure as a man recalls his life through flashbacks at the time of his death, induced by the use of [psychedelic drugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychedelic_drugs "Psychedelic drugs").[^16] [Richard Linklater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Linklater "Richard Linklater") used nonlinear narrative in *[Waking Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waking_Life "Waking Life")* (2001), *[A Scanner Darkly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Scanner_Darkly_\(film\) "A Scanner Darkly (film)")* (2006); [Gus Van Sant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gus_Van_Sant "Gus Van Sant") in *[Elephant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elephant_\(2003_film\) "Elephant (2003 film)")* (2003), *[Last Days](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_Days_\(2005_film\) "Last Days (2005 film)")* (2005), and *[Paranoid Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paranoid_Park_\(film\) "Paranoid Park (film)")* (2007). [Alejandro González Iñárritu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alejandro_Gonz%C3%A1lez_I%C3%B1%C3%A1rritu "Alejandro González Iñárritu") 's film *[Babel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babel_\(film\) "Babel (film)")* is an example of fragmented narrative structure. Hong Kong auteur [Wong Kar-wai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wong_Kar-wai "Wong Kar-wai") explored nonlinear storylines in the films *[Days of Being Wild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Days_of_Being_Wild "Days of Being Wild")* (1991), *[Ashes of Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashes_of_Time "Ashes of Time")* (1994), *[Chungking Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chungking_Express "Chungking Express")* (1994), *[In the Mood for Love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_the_Mood_for_Love "In the Mood for Love")* (2000), and *[2046](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2046_\(film\) "2046 (film)")* (2004). [Fernando Meirelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Meirelles "Fernando Meirelles") in *[City of God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_God_\(2002_film\) "City of God (2002 film)")* and *[The Constant Gardener](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Constant_Gardener_\(film\) "The Constant Gardener (film)")*. Some of [Alejandro González Iñárritu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alejandro_Gonz%C3%A1lez_I%C3%B1%C3%A1rritu "Alejandro González Iñárritu") 's films feature nonlinear narratives, including the ones written by Guillermo Arriaga who also uses nonlinear narratives in his other screenplays. [Charlie Kaufman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie_Kaufman "Charlie Kaufman") is also known for his fondness of nonlinear storytelling as applied in *[Adaptation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adaptation_\(film\) "Adaptation (film)")* and *[Eternal Sunshine of the Spotless Mind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eternal_Sunshine_of_the_Spotless_Mind "Eternal Sunshine of the Spotless Mind")*. [Takashi Shimizu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takashi_Shimizu "Takashi Shimizu") 's Japanese horror series, *[Ju-on](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ju-on "Ju-on")*, brought to America as *[The Grudge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Grudge "The Grudge")*, is also nonlinear in its storytelling (the only exception being *[The Grudge 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Grudge_3 "The Grudge 3")*). Director [Martin Koolhoven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Koolhoven "Martin Koolhoven") has made more movies with a nonlinear narrative, but the most notorious one is probably his controversial western *[Brimstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brimstone_\(2016_film\) "Brimstone (2016 film)")*, which premiered in the 2016 [Venice Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice_Film_Festival "Venice Film Festival"). Director [Vetrimaaran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vetrimaaran "Vetrimaaran") made the Tamil-language thriller film *[Vada Chennai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vada_Chennai "Vada Chennai")* (2018) which has a nonlinear narrative structure. Another Tamil-language film, *[Iravin Nizhal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iravin_Nizhal "Iravin Nizhal")* (2022), has a single-shot non-linear structure. *[Friend of the World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friend_of_the_World "Friend of the World")* (2020) is broken up into chapters, which has a nonlinear plot.

## Television

### United States

In [American television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_television "American television"), there are several examples of series that make use of nonlinear narrative in different forms and for different purposes. Some notable examples are *[Lost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lost_\(2004_TV_series\) "Lost (2004 TV series)")*, *[Undone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undone_\(TV_series\) "Undone (TV series)")*, *[Breaking Bad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breaking_Bad "Breaking Bad")*, *[The Walking Dead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Walking_Dead_\(TV_series\) "The Walking Dead (TV series)")*, *[Once Upon a Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Once_Upon_a_Time_\(TV_series\) "Once Upon a Time (TV series)")*, *[The Witcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Witcher_\(TV_series\) "The Witcher (TV series)")*, *[Arrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrow_\(TV_series\) "Arrow (TV series)")*, *[Orange Is the New Black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_Is_the_New_Black "Orange Is the New Black")*, and *[True Detective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/True_Detective "True Detective")*. Even though it is often found in [drama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drama "Drama"), some [comedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comedy "Comedy") shows use nonlinear narrative too, such as *[Arrested Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrested_Development "Arrested Development")* and *[How I Met Your Mother](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/How_I_Met_Your_Mother "How I Met Your Mother")*. This kind of narrative is used in several ways. Some series only have certain nonlinear episodes, such as *[Penny Dreadful](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penny_Dreadful_\(TV_series\) "Penny Dreadful (TV series)")* and *[The Leftovers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Leftovers_\(TV_series\) "The Leftovers (TV series)")*. Others use nonlinear storylines throughout the whole series, such as *[Lost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lost_\(2004_TV_series\) "Lost (2004 TV series)")* and *[Arrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrow_\(TV_series\) "Arrow (TV series)")*. Other series use nonlinear narrative in the beginning of a season and then explore the past until they meet, such as *[Damages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damages_\(TV_series\) "Damages (TV series)")* and *[Bloodline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloodline_\(TV_series\) "Bloodline (TV series)")*.

#### The past in certain episodes

Some television series use nonlinear narrative in certain episodes to fully explore an important part of the main characters' past. An example is [Showtime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Showtime_Network "Showtime Network") 's [horror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horror_fiction "Horror fiction") [drama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drama "Drama") *[Penny Dreadful](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penny_Dreadful_\(TV_series\) "Penny Dreadful (TV series)")*, which features one episode per season that is entirely devoted to exploring key moments in Vanessa Ives' ([Eva Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eva_Green "Eva Green")) past. Another example is [HBO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_Box_Office "Home Box Office") 's drama *[The Leftovers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Leftovers_\(TV_series\) "The Leftovers (TV series)")*, whose ninth episode is set in the past and explores the lives of the main characters before the critical event that drives the story took place. [Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_Broadcasting_Company "Fox Broadcasting Company") 's [sci-fi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sci-fi "Sci-fi") series *[Fringe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fringe_\(TV_series\) "Fringe (TV series)")*, the [Amazon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon.com "Amazon.com") original [comedy drama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comedy_drama "Comedy drama") *[Transparent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transparent_\(TV_series\) "Transparent (TV series)")* and the [Netflix original](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_original_programs_distributed_by_Netflix "List of original programs distributed by Netflix") [comedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comedy "Comedy") *[Grace and Frankie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grace_and_Frankie "Grace and Frankie")* use this technique only in certain episodes too.

#### The future or past throughout the series

There are certain television series that use nonlinear narrative to explore the past - or future - of one or various characters throughout its whole run. The [ABC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Broadcasting_Company "American Broadcasting Company") television series *[Lost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lost_\(2004_TV_series\) "Lost (2004 TV series)")* made extensive use of nonlinear story telling, with each episode typically featuring a primary storyline on the island as well as a secondary storyline from another point in a character's life, either past or future. So does [The CW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_CW "The CW") 's series *[Arrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrow_\(TV_series\) "Arrow (TV series)")* which, in every episode, features a storyline following the life of [Oliver Queen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Queen_\(Arrowverse\) "Oliver Queen (Arrowverse)") ([Stephen Amell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Amell "Stephen Amell")) stranded in an island and a main storyline five years later in which he goes back home and decides to become a [vigilante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vigilante "Vigilante"). Using a similar storytelling technique, [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix "Netflix") 's [original series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_original_programs_distributed_by_Netflix "List of original programs distributed by Netflix") *[Orange Is the New Black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_Is_the_New_Black "Orange Is the New Black")* explores the lives of the main characters in prison and also some important part of their past before they became [inmates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inmate "Inmate"). Another example is [FX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FX_\(TV_channel\) "FX (TV channel)") 's [horror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horror_film "Horror film") - [drama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drama "Drama") series *[The Strain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Strain_\(TV_series\) "The Strain (TV series)")*.

#### As a narrative hook

Some television series use nonlinear narrative in the beginning of a season as a [narrative hook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narrative_hook "Narrative hook"), showing an intense or shocking event, and then extensively explore the past and the reasons that lead that event to happen. A notable example is the [AMC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMC_\(TV_channel\) "AMC (TV channel)") [drama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drama "Drama") series *[Breaking Bad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breaking_Bad "Breaking Bad")*, which in the beginning of its [final season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breaking_Bad_\(season_5\) "Breaking Bad (season 5)") showed a neglected and lonesome [Walter White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_White_\(Breaking_Bad\) "Walter White (Breaking Bad)") ([Bryan Cranston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryan_Cranston "Bryan Cranston")) and then explored what had happened to him. This technique was also used in *Breaking Bad'* s [Pilot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilot_\(Breaking_Bad\) "Pilot (Breaking Bad)") and in its [second season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breaking_Bad_\(season_2\) "Breaking Bad (season 2)"). Using the same formula, [FX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FX_\(TV_channel\) "FX (TV channel)") 's [Emmy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmy_Award "Emmy Award") winning legal drama *[Damages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damages_\(TV_series\) "Damages (TV series)")* starring [Glenn Close](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenn_Close "Glenn Close"), begins each season with an intensely melodramatic event taking place and then traveling back six months earlier. Throughout the season, each episode shows events both in the past, present, and future that lead up to and follow said event. [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix "Netflix") 's [original series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_original_programs_distributed_by_Netflix "List of original programs distributed by Netflix") *[Bloodline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloodline_\(TV_series\) "Bloodline (TV series)")* and [ABC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Broadcasting_Company "American Broadcasting Company") 's [crime drama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime_drama "Crime drama") *[How to Get Away with Murder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/How_to_Get_Away_with_Murder "How to Get Away with Murder")* use a similar storytelling technique.

#### To mimic human memory

Another reason why a television series uses nonlinear narrative is to better portray the way human memory recalls events. In its [first season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/True_Detective_\(season_1\) "True Detective (season 1)"), the [HBO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HBO "HBO") [anthology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthology "Anthology") series *[True Detective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/True_Detective "True Detective")* used nonlinear narrative depicting the events that the main characters described and in the way they remembered them. [Showtime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Showtime_Network "Showtime Network") 's [Golden Globe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globes "Golden Globes") winning [drama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drama "Drama") *[The Affair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Affair_\(TV_series\) "The Affair (TV series)")* uses this narrative technique in the same way. However, by using [unreliable narrators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unreliable_narrator "Unreliable narrator"), this show emphasizes how differently two people recall the same events.

## Video games

Some video games mimic film non-linearity by presenting a single plot in a chronologically distorted way instead of letting the player determine the story flow themselves. The [first-person shooter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-person_shooter "First-person shooter") *[Tribes: Vengeance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribes:_Vengeance "Tribes: Vengeance")* is an example of this; another is Sega's *[Sonic Adventure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonic_Adventure "Sonic Adventure")*.

A nonlinear plot structure may or may not be combined with branching:

All of Quantum Games were developed nonlinear structures into the style of [hyperlink cinema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperlink_cinema "Hyperlink cinema").

Some games tell their nonlinear story without the player being able to change any (or very little) of the plot structure. For example, *[Uncharted 2: Among Thieves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncharted_2:_Among_Thieves "Uncharted 2: Among Thieves")* begins *[in medias res](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_medias_res "In medias res")*, with the lead character in the aftermath of an accident that the player only reaches several hours of gameplay later.

Indie game *[Fragments of Him](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fragments_of_Him "Fragments of Him")* also begins *[in medias res](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_medias_res "In medias res")* but, in addition to the nonlinear beginning, it later jumps between characters to build the story and character relationships in a nonlinear fashion, and a subtle branch means that players may see the stories in a different order if they walk into a different room at the beginning.

Indie developers [Dennaton Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dennaton_Games "Dennaton Games") use non-linear passages of time in their game *[Hotline Miami 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotline_Miami_2 "Hotline Miami 2")* in the same way *[Pulp Fiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulp_Fiction "Pulp Fiction")* is written. For example, some segments of the game take place before the events of the Prequel. It is used for dramatic effect in most cases, some characters have already had onscreen deaths but the player will not realise it until a later chapter of the character walking blindly to their already shown death.

Often game developers use the idea of [character amnesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amnesia "Amnesia") in games. It helps give the game a beginning because the audience only has the understanding that there is a history before the events of the game take place. Furthermore, by creating a nonlinear storyline the complexity of game play is greatly expanded. Nonlinear game play allows for greater replay value, allowing the player to put together different pieces of a potentially puzzling storyline. A fitting example of character amnesia is the 2005 video game *[Façade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fa%C3%A7ade_\(video_game\) "Façade (video game)")*. In *Façade* the player is put into a situation that lasts approximately 10 to 15 minutes in real time, yet the events recalled seem to have a basis in years of dramatic history.[^17]

## HTML narratives

In contemporary society webpages or to be more correct, hypertext, have become affluent forms of narratives, now termed [electronic literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_literature "Electronic literature").[^18] Hypertexts have great potential to create non-linear forms of narratives. They allow for individuals to navigate within the story through links, images, audio and video, consisting of multiple subtopics that do not force the audience to make their next selection based on what their previous reading experiences are.[^19] The first-person narrative *[Six Sex Scenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six_Sex_Scenes "Six Sex Scenes")* is an example of this; another example is *[Patchwork Girl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patchwork_Girl_\(hypertext\) "Patchwork Girl (hypertext)")*.
