![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/33/Cybernetics.jpg/250px-Cybernetics.jpg)

Principle diagram of a cybernetic system with a feedback loop

**Cybernetics** is the [transdisciplinary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transdisciplinary "Transdisciplinary") study of circular causal [^2] processes such as [feedback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feedback "Feedback") and [recursion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recursion "Recursion"), where the effects of a [system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/System "System") 's actions (its outputs) return as inputs to that system, influencing subsequent actions.[^3] It is concerned with general principles that are relevant across multiple contexts,[^4] including engineering, ecological, economic, [biological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_cybernetics "Biological cybernetics"), [cognitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognitive_science "Cognitive science") and [social systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_systems "Social systems") and also in practical activities such as [designing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Design "Design"),[^5] [learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Learning "Learning"), and [managing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Management_cybernetics "Management cybernetics"). Cybernetics' transdisciplinary [^6] character means that it [intersects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intersection_\(set_theory\) "Intersection (set theory)") with a number of other fields, resulting in a wide influence and diverse interpretations.

The field is named after an example of circular causal feedback—that of steering a ship (the ancient [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_alphabet "Greek alphabet") κυβερνήτης (*kybernḗtēs*) refers to the person who steers a ship). In steering a ship, the position of the rudder is adjusted in continual response to the effect it is observed as having, forming a feedback loop through which a steady course can be maintained in a changing environment, responding to disturbances from cross winds and tide.[^7] [^8]

Cybernetics has its origins in exchanges between numerous disciplines during the 1940s. Initial developments were consolidated through meetings such as the [Macy conferences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macy_conferences "Macy conferences") and the [Ratio Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ratio_Club "Ratio Club"). Early focuses included purposeful behaviour,[^9] neural networks, [heterarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heterarchy "Heterarchy"), [information theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_theory "Information theory"), and self-organising systems.[^10] As cybernetics developed, it became broader in scope to include work in design, family therapy, management and organisation, pedagogy, [sociology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sociocybernetics "Sociocybernetics"), the creative arts and the counterculture.[^11]

## Definitions

Cybernetics has been defined in a variety of ways, reflecting "the richness of its conceptual base".[^12] One of the best known definitions is that of the American scientist [Norbert Wiener](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norbert_Wiener "Norbert Wiener"), who characterised cybernetics as concerned with "control and communication in the animal and the machine".[^13] Another early definition is that of the [Macy cybernetics conferences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macy_conferences "Macy conferences"), where cybernetics was understood as the study of "circular causal and feedback mechanisms in biological and social systems".[^14] [Margaret Mead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaret_Mead "Margaret Mead") emphasised the role of cybernetics as "a form of cross-disciplinary thought which made it possible for members of many disciplines to communicate with each other easily in a language which all could understand".[^15]

Other definitions include:[^16] "the art of governing or the science of government" ([André-Marie Ampère](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9-Marie_Amp%C3%A8re "André-Marie Ampère")); "the art of steersmanship" ([Ross Ashby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ross_Ashby "Ross Ashby")); "the study of systems of any nature which are capable of receiving, storing, and processing information so as to use it for control" ([Andrey Kolmogorov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrey_Kolmogorov "Andrey Kolmogorov")); and "a branch of mathematics dealing with problems of control, recursiveness, and information, focuses on forms and the patterns that connect" ([Gregory Bateson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregory_Bateson "Gregory Bateson")).

## Etymology

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ed/Ideal_feedback_model.svg/250px-Ideal_feedback_model.svg.png)

Simple feedback model. AB < 0 for negative feedback.

The [Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek "Ancient Greek") term κυβερνητικός (*kubernētikos*, '(good at) steering') appears in [Plato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plato "Plato") 's *[Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_\(Plato\) "Republic (Plato)")* [^17] and *[*Alcibiades*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Alcibiades "First Alcibiades")*, where the metaphor of a [steersman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helmsman "Helmsman") is used to signify the [governance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governance "Governance") of people.[^18] The French word *cybernétique* was also used in 1834 by the physicist [André-Marie Ampère](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9-Marie_Amp%C3%A8re "André-Marie Ampère") to denote the sciences of government in his classification system of human knowledge.

According to Norbert Wiener, the word *cybernetics* was coined by a research group involving himself and [Arturo Rosenblueth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arturo_Rosenblueth "Arturo Rosenblueth") in the summer of 1947.[^13] It has been attested in print since at least 1948 through Wiener's book *[Cybernetics: Or Control and Communication in the Animal and the Machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cybernetics:_Or_Control_and_Communication_in_the_Animal_and_the_Machine "Cybernetics: Or Control and Communication in the Animal and the Machine")*.[^1] In the book, Wiener states:

> After much consideration, we have come to the conclusion that all the existing terminology has too heavy a bias to one side or another to serve the future development of the field as well as it should; and as happens so often to scientists, we have been forced to coin at least one artificial neo-Greek expression to fill the gap. We have decided to call the entire field of control and communication theory, whether in the machine or in the animal, by the name *Cybernetics*, which we form from the [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek "Ancient Greek") [κυβερνήτης](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%CE%BA%CF%85%CE%B2%CE%B5%CF%81%CE%BD%CE%AE%CF%84%CE%B7%CF%82#Ancient_Greek "wikt:κυβερνήτης") or *[steersman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helmsman "Helmsman")*.

Moreover, Wiener explains, the term was chosen to recognize [James Clerk Maxwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Clerk_Maxwell "James Clerk Maxwell") 's 1868 publication on feedback mechanisms involving [governors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_\(device\) "Governor (device)"), noting that the term *governor* is also derived from κυβερνήτης (*kubernḗtēs*) via a Latin corruption *[gubernator](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/gubernator#Latin "wikt:gubernator")*. Finally, Wiener motivates the choice by [steering engines of a ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steering_engine "Steering engine") being "one of the earliest and best-developed forms of feedback mechanisms".[^13]

## History

### First wave

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4d/Norbert_wiener.jpg/250px-Norbert_wiener.jpg)

Norbert Wiener

The initial focus of cybernetics was on parallels between regulatory feedback processes in biological and technological systems. Two foundational articles were published in 1943: "Behavior, Purpose and Teleology" by [Arturo Rosenblueth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arturo_Rosenblueth "Arturo Rosenblueth"), [Norbert Wiener](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norbert_Wiener "Norbert Wiener"), and [Julian Bigelow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julian_Bigelow "Julian Bigelow") – based on the research on living organisms that Rosenblueth did in Mexico – and the paper "A Logical Calculus of the Ideas Immanent in Nervous Activity" by [Warren McCulloch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warren_McCulloch "Warren McCulloch") and [Walter Pitts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Pitts "Walter Pitts").

The foundations of cybernetics were then developed through a series of transdisciplinary conferences funded by the Josiah Macy, Jr. Foundation, between 1946 and 1953. The conferences were chaired by [McCulloch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warren_Sturgis_McCulloch "Warren Sturgis McCulloch") and had participants that included [Ross Ashby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W._Ross_Ashby "W. Ross Ashby"), [Gregory Bateson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregory_Bateson "Gregory Bateson"), [Heinz von Foerster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinz_von_Foerster "Heinz von Foerster"), [Margaret Mead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaret_Mead "Margaret Mead"), [John von Neumann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_von_Neumann "John von Neumann"), and [Norbert Wiener](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norbert_Wiener "Norbert Wiener"). In the UK, similar focuses were explored by the [Ratio Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ratio_Club "Ratio Club"), an informal dining club of young psychiatrists, psychologists, physiologists, mathematicians and engineers that met between 1949 and 1958. Wiener introduced the neologism *cybernetics* to denote the study of "teleological mechanisms" and popularized it through the book *[Cybernetics: Or Control and Communication in the Animal and the Machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cybernetics:_Or_Control_and_Communication_in_the_Animal_and_the_Machine "Cybernetics: Or Control and Communication in the Animal and the Machine")*.[^13]

During the 1950s, cybernetics was developed as a primarily technical discipline, such as in [Qian Xuesen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qian_Xuesen "Qian Xuesen") 's 1954 "Engineering Cybernetics". The text was quickly translated into multiple languages and became a foundational text on automation.[^20]<sup><span title="Page: 119">: 119</span> </sup> [In the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cybernetics_in_the_Soviet_Union "Cybernetics in the Soviet Union"), Cybernetics was initially considered with suspicion [^21] but became accepted from the mid to late 1950s.

By the 1960s and 1970s, however, cybernetics' transdisciplinarity fragmented, with technical focuses separating into separate fields. [Artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence "Artificial intelligence") (AI) was founded as a distinct discipline at the [Dartmouth workshop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_workshop "Dartmouth workshop") in 1956, differentiating itself from the broader cybernetics field. After some uneasy coexistence, AI gained funding and prominence. Consequently, cybernetic sciences such as the study of [artificial neural networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_neural_network "Artificial neural network") were downplayed.[^22] Similarly, [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science "Computer science") became defined as a distinct academic discipline in the 1950s and early 1960s.[^23]

### Second wave

The second wave of cybernetics came to prominence from the 1960s onwards, with its focus shifting away from technology toward social, ecological, and philosophical concerns. It was still grounded in biology, notably [Maturana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humberto_Maturana "Humberto Maturana") and [Varela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Varela "Francisco Varela") 's [autopoiesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopoiesis "Autopoiesis"), and built on earlier work on [self-organising systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-organization_in_cybernetics "Self-organization in cybernetics") and the presence of anthropologists Mead and Bateson in the Macy meetings. The Biological Computer Laboratory, founded in 1958 and active until the mid-1970s under the direction of [Heinz von Foerster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinz_von_Foerster "Heinz von Foerster") at the [University of Illinois at Urbana–Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Urbana%E2%80%93Champaign "University of Illinois at Urbana–Champaign"), was a major incubator of this trend in cybernetics research.[^24]

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/89/CyberSyn-render-106.png/250px-CyberSyn-render-106.png)

Stafford Beer oversaw the applied implementation of Project Cybersyn, an early form of cybernetic economic planning.

Focuses of the second wave of cybernetics included management cybernetics, such as [Stafford Beer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stafford_Beer "Stafford Beer") 's biologically inspired [viable system model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viable_system_model "Viable system model"); work in family therapy, drawing on Bateson; social systems, such as in the work of [Niklas Luhmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niklas_Luhmann "Niklas Luhmann"); epistemology and pedagogy, such as in the development of radical constructivism.[^25] Cybernetics' core theme of circular causality was developed beyond goal-oriented processes to concerns with reflexivity and recursion. This was especially so in the development of [second-order cybernetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second-order_cybernetics "Second-order cybernetics") (or the cybernetics of cybernetics), developed and promoted by Heinz von Foerster, which focused on questions of observation, cognition, epistemology, and ethics.

The 1960s onwards also saw cybernetics begin to develop exchanges with the creative arts, design, and architecture, notably with the *Cybernetic Serendipity* exhibition (ICA, London, 1968), curated by [Jasia Reichardt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jasia_Reichardt "Jasia Reichardt"),[^26] [^27] and the unrealised Fun Palace project (London, unrealised, 1964 onwards), where [Gordon Pask](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_Pask "Gordon Pask") was consultant to architect Cedric Price and theatre director Joan Littlewood.[^28]

In 1962, Qian Xuesen recruited [Song Jian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_Jian "Song Jian") and [Guan Zhaozhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guan_Zhaozhi "Guan Zhaozhi") to establish China's first cybernetics laboratory with him.[^29]<sup><span title="Page: 119">: 119</span></sup>

Following the [Sino-Soviet split](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Soviet_split "Sino-Soviet split"), cybernetics was deemed disreputable in China.[^30]<sup><span title="Page: 363">: 363</span> </sup> The field was again favored in the 1970s and 1980s following [Deng Xiaoping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deng_Xiaoping "Deng Xiaoping") 's emphasis on modernisation.[^30]<sup><span title="Page: 363">: 363</span></sup>

### Third wave

From the 1990s onwards, there has been a renewed interest in cybernetics from a number of directions. Early cybernetic work on artificial neural networks has been returned to as a [paradigm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paradigm "Paradigm") in [machine learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_learning "Machine learning") and artificial intelligence. The entanglements of society with emerging technologies has led to exchanges with [feminist technoscience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feminist_technoscience "Feminist technoscience") and [posthumanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Posthumanism "Posthumanism"). Re-examinations of cybernetics' history have seen science studies scholars emphasising cybernetics' unusual qualities as a science, such as its " [performative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Performance "Performance") [ontology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontology "Ontology") ".[^31] Practical design disciplines have drawn on cybernetics for [theoretical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory "Theory") underpinning and transdisciplinary connections. Emerging topics include how cybernetics' engagements with social, human, and ecological contexts might come together with its earlier technological focus, whether as a critical discourse [^32] [^33] or a "new branch of engineering".[^34]

## Key concepts and theories

The central theme in cybernetics is [feedback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feedback "Feedback"). Feedback is a process where the observed outcomes of actions are taken as inputs for further action in ways that support the pursuit, maintenance, or disruption of particular conditions, forming a circular causal relationship. In steering a ship, the helmsperson maintains a steady course in a changing environment by adjusting their steering in continual response to the effect it is observed as having.[^7]

Other examples of circular causal feedback include: technological devices such as the [thermostat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermostat "Thermostat"), where the action of a heater responds to measured changes in temperature regulating the temperature of the room within a set range, and the [centrifugal governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centrifugal_governor "Centrifugal governor") of a steam engine, which regulates the engine speed; biological examples such as the coordination of volitional movement through the [nervous system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nervous_system "Nervous system") and the [homeostatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeostasis "Homeostasis") processes that regulate variables such as blood sugar; and processes of social interaction such as conversation.[^35]

[Negative feedback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negative_feedback "Negative feedback") processes are those that maintain particular conditions by reducing (hence 'negative') the difference from a desired state, such as where a thermostat turns on a heater when it is too cold and turns a heater off when it is too hot. [Positive feedback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_feedback "Positive feedback") processes increase (hence 'positive') the difference from a desired state. An example of positive feedback is when a microphone picks up the sound that it is producing through a speaker, which is then played through the speaker, and so on.

In addition to feedback, cybernetics is concerned with other forms of circular processes including: [feedforward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feedforward "Feedforward"), [recursion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recursion "Recursion"), and [reflexivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reflexivity_\(social_theory\) "Reflexivity (social theory)").

Other key concepts and theories in cybernetics include:

- [Autopoiesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopoiesis "Autopoiesis")
- [Black box](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_box "Black box")
- [Conversation theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conversation_theory "Conversation theory")
- [Double bind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_bind "Double bind") theory: Double binds are patterns created in interaction between two or more parties in ongoing relationships where there is a contradiction between messages at different logical levels that creates a situation with emotional threat but no possibility of withdrawal from the situation and no way to articulate the problem.[^36] The theory was first described by Gregory Bateson and colleagues in the 1950s with regard to the origins of [schizophrenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schizophrenia "Schizophrenia"),[^37] but it is also characteristic of many other social contexts.[^36]
- Experimental epistemology [^38]
- [Good regulator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_regulator "Good regulator") theorem
- [Heterarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heterarchy "Heterarchy")
- [Perceptual control theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perceptual_control_theory "Perceptual control theory"): A model of behavior based on the properties of negative feedback (cybernetic) control loops. A key insight of PCT is that the controlled variable is not the output of the system (the behavioral actions), but its input, "perception". The theory came to be known as "perceptual control theory" to distinguish from those control theorists that assert or assume that it is the system's output that is controlled. [Method of levels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Method_of_levels "Method of levels") is an approach to psychotherapy based on perceptual control theory where the therapist aims to help the patient shift their awareness to higher levels of perception to resolve conflicts and allow reorganization to take place.
- [Radical constructivism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radical_constructivism "Radical constructivism")
- [Second-order cybernetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second-order_cybernetics "Second-order cybernetics"): Also known as the cybernetics of cybernetics, second-order cybernetics is the recursive application of cybernetics to itself and the practice of cybernetics according to such a critique.
- [Schismogenesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schismogenesis "Schismogenesis")
- [Self-organisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-organization_in_cybernetics "Self-organization in cybernetics")
- Social systems theory
- [Syntegrity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syntegrity "Syntegrity")
- [Variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_\(cybernetics\) "Variety (cybernetics)") and [Requisite Variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Requisite_Variety "Requisite Variety")
- [Viable system model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viable_system_model "Viable system model")

Cybernetics' central concept of circular causality is of wide applicability, leading to diverse applications and relations with other fields. Many of the initial applications of cybernetics focused on [engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering_cybernetics "Engineering cybernetics"), [biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_cybernetics "Biological cybernetics"), and exchanges between the two, such as [medical cybernetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_cybernetics "Medical cybernetics") and [robotics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robotics "Robotics") and topics such as [neural networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neural_network "Neural network"), [heterarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heterarchy "Heterarchy").[^39] In the social and behavioral sciences, cybernetics has included and influenced work in [anthropology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Systems_theory_in_anthropology "Systems theory in anthropology"), [sociology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sociocybernetics "Sociocybernetics"), [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applications_of_cybernetics_in_economics "Applications of cybernetics in economics"), [family therapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_therapy "Family therapy"),[^40] cognitive science, and [psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Systems_psychology "Systems psychology").[^41] [^42]

As cybernetics has developed, it broadened in scope to include work in management, design,[^5] pedagogy,[^43] [^44] and the creative arts,[^45] while also developing exchanges with constructivist philosophies, counter-cultural movements,[^46] and media studies.[^47] The development of [management cybernetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Management_cybernetics "Management cybernetics") has led to a variety of applications, notably to the national economy of Chile under the [Allende](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvador_Allende "Salvador Allende") government in [Project Cybersyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Cybersyn "Project Cybersyn"), as well as other lesser known projects by Stafford Beer in Latin America [^48]. In design, cybernetics has been influential on [interactive architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interactive_architecture "Interactive architecture"), human-computer interaction,[^49] design research,[^50] and the development of [systemic design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Systemic_design "Systemic design") and [metadesign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metadesign "Metadesign") practices.

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/89/CyberSyn-render-106.png/250px-CyberSyn-render-106.png)

Project Cybersyn was an early form of cybernetic economic planning.

Cybernetics is often understood within the context of systems science, [systems theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Systems_theory "Systems theory"), and [systems thinking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Systems_thinking "Systems thinking").[^51] [^52] Systems approaches influenced by cybernetics include [critical systems thinking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critical_systems_thinking "Critical systems thinking"), which incorporates the [viable system model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viable_system_model "Viable system model"); [systemic design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Systemic_design "Systemic design"); and [system dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/System_dynamics "System dynamics"), which is based on the concept of causal feedback loops.

Many fields trace their origins in whole or part to work carried out in cybernetics, or were partially absorbed into cybernetics when it was developed. These include [artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence "Artificial intelligence"), [bionics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bionics "Bionics"), [cognitive science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognitive_science "Cognitive science"), [control theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Control_theory "Control theory"), [complexity science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complexity_science "Complexity science"), [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science "Computer science"), [information theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_theory "Information theory") and [robotics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robotics "Robotics"). Some aspects of modern [artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence "Artificial intelligence"), particularly the [social machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_machine "Social machine"), are often described in cybernetic terms.[^53]

Academic societies primarily concerned with cybernetics or aspects of it include:

- [American Society for Cybernetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Society_for_Cybernetics "American Society for Cybernetics") (ASC), founded in 1964
- British [Cybernetics Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cybernetics_Society "Cybernetics Society") (CybSoc)

The Metaphorum Group was born in a Syntegration in 2003 and have every year after developed a Conference on issues related to Organizational Cybernetics' theory and practice.
- [IEEE Systems, Man, and Cybernetics Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE_Systems,_Man,_and_Cybernetics_Society "IEEE Systems, Man, and Cybernetics Society")

- SCiO (Systems and Complexity in Organisation) is a community of systems practitioners who believe that traditional approaches to running organisations are no longer capable of dealing with the complexity and turbulence faced by organisations today and are responsible for many of the problems we see today. SCiO delivers an apprenticeship on masters level and a certification in systems practice.[^56]
